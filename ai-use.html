AI use in my research

In recent months, mathematics has seen a rapid change due to increased capabilities of artificial intelligence. This has drastically influenced how I do mathematics:

  • When thinking through problems or questions, I usually do so through conversations with an AI, mostly Codex.
  • When editing LaTeX files, I frequently use coding agents (Claude or Codex) to make the requested edits. This does not apply to joint projects for which my coauthors expressed a wish not to use coding agents.
  • For my textbook on stable homotopy theory and higher algebra, I extensively used AI agents to do proofreading, find weaknesses in the exposition/narrative and suggest improvements, and at times also draft new mathematical material.
  • For various lecture notes, I used AI agents to transform the live notes I took into proper LaTeX notes, fix typos, complete some proofs that were omitted during the lecture, look up and add citations, etcetera.

Each paper or set of notes comes with its own AI declaration. If there’s no AI declaration, it means AI was used not at all or only very minimally (e.g. there are a couple of papers from before AI agents were any good where I used it for typo correction, which didn’t seem worth mentioning at the time). For the synthetic category theory book, I have also used AI agents for editing; this will be acknowledged in the book itself in due course.

I take full responsibility for everything I publish.
