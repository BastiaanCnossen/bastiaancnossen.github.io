C.3. Presentable categories

Proposition C.5.

Let \(T\) be a presentable category, and let \(A \subseteq T\) be an essentially small full subcategory. Then there exists an essentially small full subcategory \(C \subseteq T\) which contains \(A\), is closed under finite limits, and is dense in \(T\).

Proof
Choose a regular cardinal \(\kappa\) such that \(T\) is \(\kappa\)-accessible. By [Lurie 2009, Proposition 5.4.2.2], the full subcategory \(T^{\kappa}\) of \(\kappa\)-compact objects is essentially small and generates \(T\) under \(\kappa\)-filtered colimits, so that \(T \simeq \Ind_{\kappa}(T^{\kappa})\) by [Lurie 2009, Proposition 5.3.5.11]. Under this equivalence, the restricted Yoneda functor \(T \to \PSh(T^{\kappa})\) corresponds to the inclusion \(\Ind_{\kappa}(T^{\kappa}) \subseteq \PSh(T^{\kappa})\): for \(F \in \Ind_{\kappa}(T^{\kappa})\) with image \(X \in T\) and \(c \in T^{\kappa}\), we have \(\Hom_T(c,X) \simeq \Hom_{\Ind_{\kappa}(T^{\kappa})}(h(c),F) \simeq F(c)\) by the Yoneda lemma. Hence \(T^{\kappa}\) is dense in \(T\).
A presentable category admits all small limits [Lurie 2009, Corollary 5.5.2.4]. Let \(C\) be the smallest full subcategory of \(T\) which contains \(T^{\kappa} \cup A\) and is closed under finite limits. It is essentially small by Lemma C.4, and dense by Lemma C.3.

Corollary C.6.

Let \(T\) be a presentable category, and let \(C \subseteq T\) be as in Proposition C.5. Let \(i\colon C \hookrightarrow T\) denote the inclusion. Then \(C\) admits finite limits, \(i\) is left exact, and the colimit-preserving extension \(i_!\colon \PSh(C) \to T\) is a localization whose right adjoint \(i^*\) is fully faithful. If \(T\) satisfies the Giraud axioms, then \(i_!\) is left exact.

Proof
Since \(C\) is a full subcategory closed under finite limits, it admits finite limits and \(i\) preserves them. The right adjoint of \(i_!\) is the restricted Yoneda functor \(i^*\), which is fully faithful because \(C\) is dense. The final assertion is Proposition 2.43.

Remark C.7.

The subcategory \(C\) produced by Proposition C.5 need not consist of \(\kappa\)-compact objects. Since \(C\) is essentially small, there nevertheless exists a regular cardinal \(\lambda\) such that every object of \(C\) is \(\lambda\)-compact. The restricted Yoneda functor \(i^*\) then commutes with \(\lambda\)-filtered colimits, so the localization of Corollary C.6 is accessible.

For suitable \(\tau \gg \kappa\), one may take \(C = T^{\tau}\). The subcategory \(T^{\tau}\) is closed under finite limits by [Lurie 2009, Proposition 5.4.7.4], and \(T\) is \(\tau\)-accessible by [Lurie 2009, Proposition 5.4.2.11], so \(T \simeq \Ind_{\tau}(C)\). This stronger form is used in Proposition 4.31, where presentations must be chosen compatibly along a diagram of topoi.
