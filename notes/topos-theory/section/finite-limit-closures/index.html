C.2. Finite-limit closures

Lemma C.4.

Let \(T\) be a locally small category which admits finite limits, and let \(A \subseteq T\) be an essentially small full subcategory. Then the smallest full subcategory of \(T\) which contains \(A\) and is closed under finite limits is essentially small.

Proof
Define full subcategories \(A_0 \subseteq A_1 \subseteq \cdots\) of \(T\) inductively: \(A_0 := A\), and \(A_{n+1}\) is spanned by the objects of \(A_n\), a terminal object of \(T\), and the pullbacks in \(T\) of all cospans in \(A_n\). Since \(T\) is locally small and \(A_n\) is essentially small, there is only a small set of equivalence classes of cospans in \(A_n\). Hence every \(A_n\) is essentially small, and so is \(A_\omega := \bigcup_n A_n\). Every cospan in \(A_\omega\) lies in some \(A_n\), so \(A_\omega\) is closed under pullbacks, and it contains a terminal object. By the inductive argument in the proof of (the dual of) [Lurie 2009, Corollary 4.4.2.4], every finite limit in \(T\) of a diagram in \(A_\omega\) may be built from iterated pullbacks and the terminal object, hence lies in \(A_\omega\). Thus \(A_\omega\) is the smallest full subcategory containing \(A\) and closed under finite limits.
