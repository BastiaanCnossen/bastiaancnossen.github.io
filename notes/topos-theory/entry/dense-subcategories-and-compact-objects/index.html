Remark C.7.

The subcategory \(C\) produced by Proposition C.5 need not consist of \(\kappa\)-compact objects. Since \(C\) is essentially small, there nevertheless exists a regular cardinal \(\lambda\) such that every object of \(C\) is \(\lambda\)-compact. The restricted Yoneda functor \(i^*\) then commutes with \(\lambda\)-filtered colimits, so the localization of Corollary C.6 is accessible.

For suitable \(\tau \gg \kappa\), one may take \(C = T^{\tau}\). The subcategory \(T^{\tau}\) is closed under finite limits by [Lurie 2009, Proposition 5.4.7.4], and \(T\) is \(\tau\)-accessible by [Lurie 2009, Proposition 5.4.2.11], so \(T \simeq \Ind_{\tau}(C)\). This stronger form is used in Proposition 4.31, where presentations must be chosen compatibly along a diagram of topoi.

References

  1. Jacob Lurie. Higher topos theory. Ann. Math. Stud. 170, Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press. 2009.
