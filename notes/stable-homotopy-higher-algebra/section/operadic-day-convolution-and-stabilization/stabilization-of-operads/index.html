Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Part II · Chapter 18 · Section 18.4

Stabilization of ∞-operads

← Previous chapterChapter 17 → Next chapterChapter 19 ← Previous sectionSection 18.3 → Next sectionSection 18.5

We construct the stabilization of an operad and prove its right-adjoint universal property. At the end of the section, we briefly discuss the analogous constructions for pointed, semiadditive, and additive operads.

We define the stabilization of \(\Oo \) using reduced excisive functors. To prove that the resulting operad is stable, we use the multiplicative Yoneda embedding to reduce to stabilization in a presheaf category. We then prove the right-adjoint universal property by showing that evaluation at \(S^0\) recovers \(\Oo \) when it is already stable.

Construction 18.4.1. Let \(\Oo \) be an \(\infty \)-operad that admits finite operadic limits. Consider the \(\infty \)-category \(\An _*^{\fin }\) equipped with the smash product monoidal structure from Definition 16.3.5. We define \[ \oSp (\Oo ) \quad \subseteq \quad \oDay (\Mm _{\An _*^{\fin }}, \Oo ) \] to be the full suboperad whose colors are those functors \(F\colon \An _*^{\fin } \to \Oo _{\lra {1}}\) that are reduced and excisive. Note that \[ \oSp (\Oo )_{\lra {1}} \simeq \Sp ^{\exc }(\Oo _{\lra {1}}) \] by construction; through Proposition 16.5.10, we identify this underlying \(\infty \)-category with the abstract stabilization \(\Sp (\Oo _{\lra {1}})\). The monoidal unit \(S^0\) defines an operad map \(\Comm \to \Mm _{\An _*^{\fin }}\), and restricting the evaluation map along it defines a morphism of \(\infty \)-operads \[ \Omega ^\infty \colon \oSp (\Oo ) \to \Oo . \]

Lemma 18.4.2. Let \(D\) be a small symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category and equip \(\PSh (D)\) with the Day convolution symmetric monoidal structure. Then \(\oSp (\Mm _{\PSh (D)})\) is a stable \(\infty \)-operad.

Proof. The Day convolution structure on \(\PSh (D)\) identifies \(\Mm _{\PSh (D)}\) with \(\oDay (\Mm _{D\catop },\Mm _{\An })\). Thus, using the universal property of Day convolution twice, \(\oDay (\Mm _{\An _*^{\fin }},\Mm _{\PSh (D)})\) identifies with \[ \oDay (\Mm _{D\catop },\oDay (\Mm _{\An _*^{\fin }},\Mm _{\An })). \] Under this identification, \(\oSp (\Mm _{\PSh (D)})\) is obtained by restricting the inner \(\oDay (\Mm _{\An _*^{\fin }},\Mm _{\An })\) to reduced excisive functors, using Lemma 18.3.2. By Theorem 16.6.1 and the construction of the symmetric monoidal localization there, this restriction is the multimorphism operad \(\Mm _{\Sp }\). Hence \[ \oSp (\Mm _{\PSh (D)}) \simeq \oDay (\Mm _{D\catop },\Mm _{\Sp }). \] The latter is the multimorphism operad of \(\Fun (D\catop ,\Sp )\) with Day convolution. Since \(\Sp \) is stable and its tensor product preserves colimits in each variable, so does the Day tensor product: for a fixed functor in all but one variable, it is computed as a left Kan extension of a pointwise tensor product, and both operations preserve colimits in the remaining variable. It follows that \(\oSp (\Mm _{\PSh (D)})\) is stable. □

Proposition 18.4.3. Let \(\Oo \) be an \(\infty \)-operad that admits finite operadic limits. Then \(\oSp (\Oo )\) is stable.

Proof. Put \(E:=\Env (\Oo )\). By Proposition 18.2.3, the multiplicative Yoneda embedding realizes \(\Oo \) as a full suboperad of \(\Mm _{\PSh (E)}\) and preserves finite operadic limits. It therefore induces a fully faithful functor \[ \Sp ^{\exc }(\Oo _{\lra {1}})\longrightarrow \Sp ^{\exc }(\PSh (E)) \] preserving finite limits, since these are computed pointwise. Both categories are stable by Proposition 16.5.7, so this functor is exact and its essential image is closed under finite limits and colimits. By Lemma 18.3.2, the corresponding full suboperad of \(\oSp (\Mm _{\PSh (E)})\) is \(\oSp (\Oo )\). The ambient operad is stable by Lemma 18.4.2, so Lemma 18.1.8 proves the claim. □

For stable \(\Oo \), evaluation at \(S^0\) already gives an equivalence on underlying categories (Lemma 16.5.8). We must show that it also induces equivalences on multimorphism animae. By Theorem 18.3.5, these consist of coherent families of operations \[ F_1(K_1),\dots ,F_n(K_n)\longrightarrow G(K_1\wedge \cdots \wedge K_n). \] We will show that such a family is determined by its value at \(K_1=\cdots =K_n=S^0\). The following lemma allows us to evaluate one variable at a time.

Lemma 18.4.4. Let \(T\colon (\An _*^{\fin })\catop \times \An _*^{\fin } \to \An \) be a functor with the following properties:

(1)

For every \(L \in \An _*^{\fin }\), the functor \(T(-,L)\colon (\An _*^{\fin })\catop \to \An \) sends finite colimits in \(\An _*^{\fin }\) to limits.

(2)

For every \(K \in \An _*^{\fin }\), the functor \(T(K,-)\colon \An _*^{\fin } \to \An \) is reduced and excisive.

Then the projection from the end to the component at \(S^0\), \[ \int _{K \in \An _*^{\fin }} T(K,K) \to T(S^0,S^0), \] is an equivalence.

Proof. By Lemma 22.3.9, condition (1) gives a functor \(F\colon \An _*^{\fin }\to \An _*\) and natural identifications \[ T(K,L)\simeq \Hom _{\An _*}(K,F(L)). \] Since the restricted Yoneda embedding preserves and reflects limits, condition (2) says that \(F\) is reduced and excisive. Let \(j\colon \An _*^{\fin }\hookrightarrow \An _*\) be the inclusion. The end formula for natural transformations and Lemma 16.5.14 give \begin {align*} \int _{K\in \An _*^{\fin }}T(K,K) &\simeq \Nat (j,F)\simeq \Nat (P_1j,F)\\ &\simeq \Hom _{\An _*}(S^0,F(S^0)) \simeq T(S^0,S^0). \end {align*}

The composite sends a transformation \(j\to F\) to its component at \(S^0\), so it is the stated projection. □

Proposition 18.4.5. If \(\Oo \) is stable, then the evaluation map \[ \Omega ^\infty \colon \oSp (\Oo ) \to \Oo \] is an equivalence of \(\infty \)-operads.

Proof. On underlying \(\infty \)-categories this is Lemma 16.5.8. By Lemma 14.1.8, it remains to prove that \(\Omega ^\infty \) is fully faithful.

Let \(I\) be a finite set, and let \(\{F_i\colon \An _*^{\fin } \to \Oo _{\lra {1}}\}_{i \in I}\) and \(G\colon \An _*^{\fin } \to \Oo _{\lra {1}}\) be reduced excisive functors. By Theorem 18.3.5, the source of the induced map on multimorphism animae is \[ \int _{\{K_i\}_{i \in I}\in (\An _*^{\fin })^I} \Oo (\{F_i(K_i)\}_{i \in I};G(\bigwedge \nolimits _{i \in I}K_i)). \] By naturality of Theorem 18.3.5 in the evaluation operad map and the identification \(\bigwedge _{i\in I}S^0 \simeq S^0\), the map induced by \(\Omega ^\infty \) is the projection from this end to the component where all \(K_i\) are equal to \(S^0\).

We evaluate the variables at \(S^0\) one at a time. In order to iterate the argument, we allow additional fixed colors \(z_1,\dots ,z_m\) among the inputs and prove the assertion by induction on \(|I|\). The case \(I=\emptyset \) is immediate. For \(I\neq \emptyset \), choose \(i_0\in I\). By Fubini, the end is \(\int _K T(K,K)\) for the bifunctor \[ T(K,L):= \int _{\{K_i\}_{i\neq i_0}\in (\An _*^{\fin })^{I\setminus \{i_0\}}} \Oo (z_1,\dots ,z_m,F_{i_0}(K),\{F_i(K_i)\}_{i\neq i_0}; G(L\wedge \bigwedge _{i\neq i_0}K_i)). \] We check the two hypotheses of Lemma 18.4.4. Since \(\Oo _{\lra {1}}\) is stable, the reduced excisive functor \(F_{i_0}\) preserves finite colimits. These colimits are operadic, so \(T(-,L)\) sends them to limits.

For the other variable, smashing with the fixed \(\bigwedge _{i\neq i_0}K_i\) preserves finite colimits. Applying \(G\) gives a reduced excisive functor, and its finite limits are operadic. Thus \(T(K,-)\) is reduced and excisive. In both verifications, taking the end preserves the relevant limits. These arguments also apply with the fixed inputs \(z_1,\dots ,z_m\), since operadic limits and colimits are tested on multimorphism animae with arbitrary other inputs.

By the lemma, the projection \(\int _K T(K,K)\to T(S^0,S^0)\) is an equivalence. The latter is the end over the remaining variables, with \(F_{i_0}(S^0)\) now a fixed input. The induction hypothesis identifies this end with the multimorphism anima obtained by setting all remaining \(K_i=S^0\). Taking \(m=0\), we obtain \[ \Oo (\{F_i(S^0)\}_{i\in I};G(S^0)). \] Each step is the projection to the indicated component, so the resulting equivalence is the map induced by \(\Omega ^\infty \). This proves full faithfulness. □

Theorem 18.4.6 (Stabilization of operads). Let \(\Oo \) be an \(\infty \)-operad that admits finite operadic limits. Then the \(\infty \)-operad \(\oSp (\Oo )\) is stable, and the resulting functor \[ \oSp \colon \Op _{\infty }^{\lex } \to \Op _{\infty }^{\st } \] is right adjoint to the inclusion functor.

Proof. The first claim is Proposition 18.4.3. We now prove the adjunction. Postcomposition with a map in \(\Op _{\infty }^{\lex }\) preserves reduced excisive functors, since its color functor preserves finite limits. Thus the construction of \(\oSp (\Oo )\) is functorial in \(\Oo \in \Op _{\infty }^{\lex }\), and evaluation at \(S^0\) gives a natural transformation \[ \Omega ^\infty \colon \oSp \to \id _{\Op _{\infty }^{\lex }}. \] By the dual of the recognition criterion for Bousfield localizations from Proposition 21.8.9, applied in \((\Op _{\infty }^{\lex })\catop \), it is enough to show that \(\Omega ^\infty _{\Oo }\) is an equivalence whenever \(\Oo \) is stable, and that \[ \oSp (\Omega ^\infty _{\Oo })\colon \oSp (\oSp (\Oo )) \to \oSp (\Oo ) \] is an equivalence for every \(\Oo \in \Op _{\infty }^{\lex }\). The first assertion is Proposition 18.4.5.

For the second assertion, observe that \(\oSp (\oSp (\Oo ))\) may be identified with the full suboperad of \(\oDay (\Mm _{\An _*^{\fin } \times \An _*^{\fin }},\Oo )\) spanned by bifunctors \(\An _*^{\fin } \times \An _*^{\fin } \to \Oo _{\lra {1}}\) which are reduced and excisive in each variable. Under this identification, the map \(\oSp (\Omega ^\infty _{\Oo })\) evaluates in one variable at \(S^0\), while the map \[ \Omega ^\infty _{\oSp (\Oo )}\colon \oSp (\oSp (\Oo )) \to \oSp (\Oo ) \] evaluates in the other variable at \(S^0\). These two maps are conjugate by the symmetry of \(\An _*^{\fin }\times \An _*^{\fin }\). Since \(\oSp (\Oo )\) is stable by the first part of the theorem, Proposition 18.4.5 shows that \(\Omega ^\infty _{\oSp (\Oo )}\) is an equivalence. Hence \(\oSp (\Omega ^\infty _{\Oo })\) is an equivalence as well. □

Recall from Definition 11.1.16 that a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category \(C\) is called stably symmetric monoidal if \(C\) is stable and the tensor product \(- \otimes -\colon C \times C \to C\) is exact in both variables; equivalently, if \(\Mm _C\) is a stable \(\infty \)-operad.

Corollary 18.4.7. Let \(C\) be a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category with finite limits whose operadic stabilization \(\oSp (\Mm _C)\) is represented by a symmetric monoidal structure on \(\Sp (C)\). For every stably symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category \(D\), evaluation induces an equivalence \[ \Omega ^{\infty }\circ -\colon \Fun ^{\otimes \dlax ,\lex }(D,\Sp (C))\iso \Fun ^{\otimes \dlax ,\lex }(D,C), \] where \(\lex \) means that the underlying functor preserves finite limits.

Proof. Evaluation at \(S^0\) is an operad map, giving \(\Omega ^\infty \) its lax symmetric monoidal structure. For any small \(\infty \)-category \(E\), currying identifies \[ \Sp (\Fun (E,C))\simeq \Fun (E,\Sp (C)), \] with pointwise monoidal structures on the functor categories. This identification respects the operads: reducedness and excisiveness are checked pointwise, and the multimorphism formulas agree by interchanging the ends over \(E\) and \((\An _*^{\fin })^I\) in Theorem 18.3.5. The interchange respects substitution and evaluation at \(S^0\).

Apply Theorem 18.4.6 with target \(\Mm _{\Fun (E,C)}\). By Lemma 14.2.12, this gives an equivalence between the maximal animae of \(E\)-diagrams in the two displayed functor categories; preservation of finite limits is checked pointwise. Naturality in \(E\) and the Yoneda lemma give the asserted equivalence of \(\infty \)-categories. Compare [Nikolaus (2016), Corollary 4.13] for the case \(C=\An \). □

Remark 18.4.8. The construction \(\oSp (\Mm _C)\) always produces an \(\infty \)-operad whose underlying \(\infty \)-category is \(\Sp (C)\), but this operad need not be the multimorphism operad of a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category. Thus the representability hypothesis in Corollary 18.4.7 is not automatic. It does hold when \(C\) is presentably symmetric monoidal; this will follow from the mode-theoretic construction in Corollary 18.5.5.

The other right adjoints from Section 18.1 are obtained by adapting the construction of stabilization.

Theorem 18.4.9 (Additivization of operads, [Nikolaus (2016), Theorem 5.7]). Let \(\Oo \) be an \(\infty \)-operad with an operadic terminal object, and assume finite operadic products for the monoid and group constructions. The categories of pointed objects, commutative monoids, and commutative groups in \(\Oo _{\lra {1}}\) admit natural operad structures \(\Oo _*\), \(\oCMon (\Oo )\), and \(\oCGrp (\Oo )\), respectively. The resulting functors \[ (-)_*\colon \Op _{\infty }^* \to \Op _{\infty }^{\pt }, \qquad \oCMon \colon \Op _{\infty }^{\mathrm {prod}} \to \Op _{\infty }^{\sadd } \qquadtext { and } \oCGrp \colon \Op _{\infty }^{\mathrm {prod}} \to \Op _{\infty }^{\add } \] are right adjoint to the respective inclusion functors.

For pointed objects, replace \(\An _*^{\fin }\) by \([1]\) with its minimum tensor product and use diagrams whose value at \(0\) is terminal. For commutative monoids and groups, use finite-product-preserving diagrams on \(\Span (\Fin )\), with its tensor product induced by cartesian products of finite sets, imposing the grouplike condition for groups. Restricting Day convolution to these diagrams gives the three operads; evaluation at the monoidal unit gives the counits. For a presentable category \(C\) whose cartesian product preserves colimits separately, these constructions on \(\Mm _{(C,\times )}\) recover the smash product on \(C_*\) and the monoidal structures on \(\CMon (C)\) and \(\CGrp (C)\) from Chapter 16. The constructions and proof of Theorem 18.4.9 are given in Section 18.6.
