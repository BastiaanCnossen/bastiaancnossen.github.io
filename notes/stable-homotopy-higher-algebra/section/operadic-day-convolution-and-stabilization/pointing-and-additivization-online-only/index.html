Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Part II · Chapter 18 · Section 18.6

Pointing and additivization (online only)

← Previous chapterChapter 17 → Next chapterChapter 19 ← Previous sectionSection 18.5 → Next sectionSection 19.1

We give the constructions and proof of Theorem 18.4.9. As in the construction of stabilization, we use Day convolution on a category of diagrams, replacing \(\An _*^{\fin }\) by \([1]\) for pointed objects and by \(\Span (\Fin )\) for commutative monoids and groups.

Definition 18.6.1. If \(\Oo \) is an \(\infty \)-operad with an operadic terminal object, equip \([1]\) with the symmetric monoidal structure given by the minimum, as used in the proof of Lemma 16.3.3, and denote by \[ \Oo _* \quad \subseteq \quad \oDay (\Mm _{[1]}, \Oo ) \] the full suboperad whose colors are those functors \(F\colon [1] \to \Oo _{\lra {1}}\) satisfying \(F(0) \simeq *\). If in addition \(\Oo \) has finite operadic products, equip \(\Span (\Fin )\) with the symmetric monoidal structure induced by the cartesian product of finite sets, and denote by \[ \oCGrp (\Oo ) \quad \subseteq \quad \oCMon (\Oo ) \quad \subseteq \quad \oDay (\Mm _{\Span (\Fin )}, \Oo ) \] the full suboperads whose colors are those functors \(F\colon \Span (\Fin ) \to \Oo _{\lra {1}}\) that are commutative groups or commutative monoids in \(\Oo _{\lra {1}}\), respectively.

Notice that this is not the cartesian monoidal structure on \(\Span (\Fin )\): its categorical products are disjoint unions by Lemma 13.3.8. The cartesian product of finite sets is the monoidal structure used in the Day convolution description of Proposition 16.4.1.

It is immediate from the definitions that the underlying \(\infty \)-categories of these \(\infty \)-operads are given by \[ (\Oo _*)_{\lra {1}} \simeq (\Oo _{\lra {1}})_*, \qquad \oCMon (\Oo )_{\lra {1}} \simeq \CMon (\Oo _{\lra {1}}) \qquadtext { and } \oCGrp (\Oo )_{\lra {1}} \simeq \CGrp (\Oo _{\lra {1}}), \] justifying the notation.

Lemma 18.6.2 (Day convolution on pointed objects). Let \(C\) be a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category satisfying the hypotheses of Lemma 16.3.4, and denote the resulting tensor on \(C_*\) by \(\wedge \). Then there is a natural equivalence \[ (\Mm _C)_* \simeq \Mm _{(C_*,\wedge )}. \]

Proof. The operad \((\Mm _C)_*\) is the full suboperad of the Day convolution operad on \(\Ar (C)\) spanned by the arrows with source the terminal object. The monoidal localization \[ \cofib \colon \Ar (C)^{\square }\longrightarrow (C_*,\wedge ) \] of Lemma 16.3.4 identifies this full suboperad with the multimorphism operad of its local objects. □

Lemma 18.6.3. If \(\Oo \) is pointed, then evaluation at the target defines an equivalence of \(\infty \)-operads \[ \ev _1\colon \Oo _* \to \Oo . \]

Proof. On underlying \(\infty \)-categories, evaluation identifies the category of arrows from the zero object with \(\Oo _{\lra {1}}\). It remains to prove full faithfulness on multimorphism animae.

Let \(\{F_i\}_{i\in I}\) and \(G\) be colors of \(\Oo _*\). By Theorem 18.3.5, their multimorphism anima is an end over \([1]^I\). We evaluate its variables at \(1\) one at a time. By Fubini, each step is the end of a bifunctor \(T\colon [1]\catop \times [1]\to \An \), allowing additional fixed colors among the inputs. This end is the pullback \[ T(0,0)\times _{T(0,1)}T(1,1). \] Both \(T(0,0)\) and \(T(0,1)\) are contractible because \(F_i(0)\) is the zero object and hence operadic initial. Thus this pullback is equivalent to \(T(1,1)\). Fubini for ends and induction, with the values \(F_i(1)\) already obtained treated as fixed inputs, identify the original end with \[ \Oo (\{F_i(1)\}_{i\in I};G(1)). \] By naturality of the end formula, this equivalence is the map induced by evaluation at \(1\). □

For commutative monoids, we use the following finite-product analogue of Lemma 18.4.4.

Lemma 18.6.4. Let \(T\colon \Span (\Fin )\catop \times \Span (\Fin )\to \An \) be a functor such that \(T(-,L)\) sends finite coproducts to products for every \(L\), and \(T(K,-)\) preserves finite products for every \(K\). Then the projection \[ \int _{K\in \Span (\Fin )}T(K,K)\longrightarrow T(1,1) \] is an equivalence, where \(1\) denotes a singleton.

Proof. Put \(B:=\Fun ^{\times }(\Span (\Fin )\catop ,\An )\). Reversal of spans identifies \(B\) with \(\CMon (\An )\) by Lemma 13.1.15, so \(B\) is semiadditive by Proposition 5.3.20. The Yoneda embedding factors through a finite-product-preserving functor \(j\colon \Span (\Fin )\to B\). The hypotheses on \(T\) give another such functor \(F\colon \Span (\Fin )\to B\), defined by \(F(L)=T(-,L)\). Yoneda and Proposition 5.3.17 give \[ \int _K T(K,K)\simeq \Nat (j,F) \simeq \Hom _B(j(1),F(1))\simeq T(1,1). \] The last two maps evaluate a natural transformation at \(1\) and then its component on \(\id _1\). Thus the composite is the stated projection. □

Proof of Theorem 18.4.9. We first prove the pointed case. Put \(E:=\Env (\Oo )\). The multiplicative Yoneda embedding and Lemma 18.3.2 give a fully faithful map \[ \Oo _* \hookrightarrow (\Mm _{\PSh (E)})_*. \] By Lemma 18.6.2, the target is the multimorphism operad of the pointed presheaf category and is therefore pointed by Example 18.1.7. The suboperad \(\Oo _*\) contains its zero object, since the multiplicative Yoneda embedding preserves the operadic terminal object of \(\Oo \). It follows from Lemma 18.1.8(1) that \(\Oo _*\) is pointed.

Evaluation at the target gives a natural operad map \(\Oo _*\to \Oo \). If \(\Oo \) is pointed, this map is an equivalence by Lemma 18.6.3. Applying the pointed-object construction a second time gives the same conclusion because \(\Oo _*\) is pointed. Interchanging the two coordinates of \([1]\times [1]\) exchanges the two ways of evaluating a doubly pointed object at \(1\), so both whiskerings of the evaluation transformation are equivalences. The dual of Proposition 21.8.9, applied in \((\Op _{\infty }^*)\catop \), therefore identifies \((-)_*\) as right adjoint to the inclusion of pointed operads.

For commutative monoids and groups, apply the same embedding into \(\Mm _{\PSh (E)}\). Proposition 16.4.1 constructs their Day convolution monoidal structures in \(\PSh (E)\). Their underlying categories are semiadditive and additive by Proposition 5.3.20, and their tensor products preserve colimits separately in both variables. Hence their finite biproducts are operadic. These biproducts are computed pointwise. Since the multiplicative Yoneda image is closed under finite products, the full suboperads consisting of objects valued in that image are closed under these biproducts. They are \(\oCMon (\Oo )\) and \(\oCGrp (\Oo )\), so Lemma 18.1.8(2) shows that the former is semiadditive and the latter additive.

Suppose now that \(\Oo \) is semiadditive. Evaluation at \(1\) gives an equivalence on underlying categories by Proposition 5.3.17. By Theorem 18.3.5, its map on multimorphism animae is the projection \[ \int _{\{K_i\}\in \Span (\Fin )^I} \Oo (\{F_i(K_i)\}_{i\in I};G(\prod _{i\in I}K_i)) \longrightarrow \Oo (\{F_i(1)\}_{i\in I};G(1)). \] We apply Lemma 18.6.4 one variable at a time, as in the proof of Proposition 18.4.5. Each \(F_i\) preserves finite coproducts: a finite-product-preserving functor between semiadditive categories preserves zero morphisms and hence the biproduct injections. Since these coproducts in \(\Oo \) are operadic, the contravariant variable sends them to products. For the covariant variable, \((-)\times R\) preserves disjoint unions in \(\Span (\Fin )\) for every finite set \(R\), hence preserves its finite products. Applying \(G\) and using operadic products gives the other hypothesis of the lemma. Taking ends preserves these products. The same arguments allow additional fixed inputs, so induction identifies the displayed projection with an equivalence. For \(I=\emptyset \), it is already the identity of \(\Oo (;G(1))\). Thus \(\oCMon (\Oo )\to \Oo \) is an equivalence of operads. If \(\Oo \) is additive, every commutative monoid in \(\Oo _{\lra {1}}\) is grouplike by Proposition 5.3.17, so the same conclusion holds for \(\oCGrp (\Oo )\to \Oo \).

Finally, postcomposition with maps preserving finite operadic products preserves commutative monoids and groups, and their finite products are computed pointwise. Thus both constructions and their evaluation maps belong to \(\Op _\infty ^{\mathrm {prod}}\). Applying either construction twice gives functors on \(\Span (\Fin )\times \Span (\Fin )\) satisfying the respective monoid or group conditions in each variable. Interchanging the variables exchanges the two evaluation maps. Since the once-constructed operads are semiadditive or additive, the preceding paragraph shows that both whiskerings of evaluation are equivalences. The dual of Proposition 21.8.9 therefore gives the two remaining right adjoints. □

Corollary 18.6.5. Applied to the cartesian multimorphism operad \(\Mm _{(\An ,\times )}\), the four operadic constructions recover the symmetric monoidal structures of Chapter 16: \[ (\Mm _{(\An ,\times )})_* \simeq \Mm _{(\An _*,\wedge )},\qquad \oCMon (\Mm _{(\An ,\times )}) \simeq \Mm _{\CMon (\An )}, \] \[ \oCGrp (\Mm _{(\An ,\times )}) \simeq \Mm _{\CGrp (\An )},\qquad \oSp (\Mm _{(\An ,\times )}) \simeq \Mm _{\Sp }. \]

Proof. The pointed case is Lemma 18.6.2, the semiadditive and additive cases are the symmetric monoidal localizations of Proposition 16.4.1, and the stable case is Theorem 16.6.1. □

Exercises

Exercise 18.1 (A stable suboperad of dualizable objects). Let \(C\) be a stably symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category. Show that the full subcategory \(C^{\mathrm {dual}}\subseteq C\) of dualizable objects is closed under finite limits and finite colimits. Deduce that the full suboperad of \(\Mm _C\) on the dualizable objects is stable.

Exercise 18.2 (A stable full suboperad). Let \(\Sp ^{\fin }\subseteq \Sp \) be the full subcategory of finite spectra. Show that it is closed under finite limits and finite colimits, and that the full suboperad of \(\Mm _{\Sp }\) on the finite spectra is stable.

Exercise 18.3 (The tensor table of the basic modes). Consider the sequence of modes \[ \An _*\longrightarrow \CMon (\An )\longrightarrow \CGrp (\An )\longrightarrow \Sp \] from Theorem 18.5.3. Show that the tensor product of any two of these modes is the one which occurs further to the right in this sequence. Interpret each entry in the resulting tensor table as the composite of the corresponding localizations of presentable \(\infty \)-categories.
