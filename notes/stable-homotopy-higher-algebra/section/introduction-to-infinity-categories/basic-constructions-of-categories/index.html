Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Chapter 1 · Section 1.3

Basic constructions of ∞-categories

We now record the ambient constructions on \(\infty \)-categories that we will use throughout the book: terminal and initial categories, products and coproducts, pullbacks, functor categories, and pushouts. Each universal property comes with two parts: it constructs functors from suitable data, and then describes the anima of identifications between two such functors.

1.3.1 Products and coproducts

We start with products and coproducts of \(\infty \)-categories. For animae, the products, projections, and pairings below are those already introduced in Axiom A.1. Likewise, the empty product is the one-point anima \(*\), with its functors \(p_T\colon T\to *\). The next axiom specifies their universal properties and extends products to arbitrary families of \(\infty \)-categories. It also introduces coproducts.

Axiom C.1 (Products and coproducts of \(\infty \)-categories). Let \(I\) be a set and let \(C_i\) be an \(\infty \)-category for every \(i \in I\).

(1)

There is a product \(\prod _{i \in I} C_i\), equipped with projection functors \(\pr _i\colon \prod _{i \in I} C_i \to C_i\) for every \(i\). Given another \(\infty \)-category \(T\) and functors \(f_i\colon T \to C_i\) for every \(i\), we obtain a functor \((f_i)\colon T \to \prod _{i \in I} C_i\) together with identifications \(\pr _i \circ (f_i) = f_i\). For two functors \(g,h\colon T\to \prod _{i\in I}C_i\), the projection-induced functor \[ (g=h)\longrightarrow \prod _{i\in I}(\pr _i g=\pr _i h) \] is an equivalence. If every \(C_i\) is an anima, their product is an anima.

(2)

There is a coproduct or disjoint union \(\bigsqcup _{i \in I} C_i\), equipped with inclusion functors \(\incl _i\colon C_i \hookrightarrow \bigsqcup _{i \in I} C_i\) for every \(i\). Given another \(\infty \)-category \(T\) and functors \(f_i\colon C_i \to T\) for every \(i\), we obtain a functor \(\lra {f_i}\colon \bigsqcup _{i \in I} C_i \to T\) together with identifications \(\lra {f_i} \circ \incl _i = f_i\). For two functors \(g,h\colon \bigsqcup _{i\in I}C_i\to T\), restriction induces an equivalence \[ (g=h)\longrightarrow \prod _{i\in I}(g\incl _i=h\incl _i). \]

Notation 1.3.1. The empty product is the one-point anima \(*\), which we also call the terminal \(\infty \)-category. We denote the empty coproduct by \(\emptyset \) and call it the empty \(\infty \)-category. Thus every \(\infty \)-category \(C\) has canonical functors \[ C \to *, \qquad \qquad \emptyset \to C. \]

Remark 1.3.2. In the empty-index cases the displayed equivalences say that \((f=g)\) is contractible for any two functors \(f,g\colon T\to *\), and likewise for functors \(\emptyset \to T\). Thus any two such identifications are themselves identified, as needed to identify products with pullbacks over \(*\), as in Exercise 1.3.10.

Notation 1.3.3. When \(I = \{1,2\}\), we write \(C_1 \times C_2\) and \(C_1 \sqcup C_2\) for the resulting product and coproduct. Given functors \(f_1\colon T \to C_1\) and \(f_2\colon T \to C_2\), we write \((f_1,f_2)\colon T \to C_1 \times C_2\) for the induced functor. Given functors \(g_1\colon C_1 \to T\) and \(g_2\colon C_2 \to T\), we write \(\lra {g_1,g_2}\colon C_1 \sqcup C_2 \to T\) for the induced functor.

Taking \(T = *\), we see that objects of \(C_1 \times C_2\) have the form \((x,y)\), where \(x\) is an object of \(C_1\) and \(y\) is an object of \(C_2\).

Definition 1.3.4. Let \(C\) and \(D\) be \(\infty \)-categories and let \(x\) be an object of \(D\), regarded as a functor \(x\colon * \to D\). We define the constant functor \(\const _x\colon C \to D\) as the following composite: \[ \const _x\colon C \xrightarrow {p_C} * \xrightarrow {x} D. \]

Definition 1.3.5 (Contractible category). An \(\infty \)-category \(C\) is called contractible if the functor \(p_{C}\colon C \to *\) is an equivalence.

Observe that an \(\infty \)-category \(C\) is contractible if and only if there exists an object \(x\colon * \to C\) such that the composite \(\const _x\colon C \to C\) is identified with the identity \(\id _C\colon C \to C\). An identification \(H\colon \const _x = \id _C\) is called a contraction of \(C\) onto \(x\).

The familiar symmetries of products and coproducts follow from these universal properties just as in ordinary category theory, and we will use them freely.

Exercise 1.3.6. Show that (co)products of \(\infty \)-categories are associative, commutative and unital: \[ C \times D \iso D \times C, \qquad (C \times D) \times E \iso C \times (D \times E), \qquad C \times * \iso C \iso * \times C, \] and \[ C \sqcup D \iso D \sqcup C, \qquad (C \sqcup D) \sqcup E \iso C \sqcup (D \sqcup E), \qquad \emptyset \sqcup C \iso C \iso C \sqcup \emptyset . \]

Construction 1.3.7. The formation of products and coproducts of \(\infty \)-categories is functorial: given two functors \(f\colon C \to C'\) and \(g\colon D \to D'\), we obtain new functors \begin {align*} f \times g \; &:= \; (f \circ \pr _{C}, g \circ \pr _{D}) \colon \; C \times D \to C' \times D' \\ f \sqcup g \; &:= \; \lra {\incl _{C'} \circ f, \incl _{D'} \circ g} \; \colon C \sqcup D \to C' \sqcup D'. \end {align*}

We leave it to the reader to verify that there are identifications \begin {align*} \id _{C} \times \id _{D} = \id _{C \times D} &\quadtext { and } (f' \times g') \circ (f \times g) = (f' \circ f) \times (g' \circ g), \\ \id _{C} \sqcup \id _{D} = \id _{C \sqcup D} &\quadtext { and } (f' \sqcup g') \circ (f \sqcup g) = (f' \circ f) \sqcup (g' \circ g) \end {align*}

for functors \(f\colon C \to C'\), \(f'\colon C' \to C''\), \(g\colon D \to D'\) and \(g'\colon D' \to D''\).

1.3.2 Pullbacks of \(\infty \)-categories

We will now axiomatize the pullback of two functors \(f\colon C \to E\) and \(g\colon D \to E\) of \(\infty \)-categories. This will be similar to the axiom for the product \(C \times D\), but it is more involved due to the fact that we need to record compatibilities with the structure maps to \(E\).

Axiom C.2 (Pullbacks of \(\infty \)-categories). For functors \(f\colon C\to E\) and \(g\colon D\to E\), their pullback, or fiber product over \(E\), is an \(\infty \)-category \(C\times _E D\) with projections \(\pr _1\colon C\times _E D\to C\) and \(\pr _2\colon C\times _E D\to D\), and an identification \(\tau \colon f\circ \pr _1=g\circ \pr _2\); in other words, a commutative square of the form

Given \(t_C\colon T\to C\), \(t_D\colon T\to D\), and \(\sigma \colon f\circ t_C=g\circ t_D\), there is a functor \(t=(t_C,t_D)_\sigma \colon T\to C\times _E D\) with identifications \(\alpha \colon \pr _1\circ t=t_C\), \(\beta \colon \pr _2\circ t=t_D\), and \[ \sigma \circ (f\star \alpha )=(g\star \beta )\circ (\tau \star t). \] For any \(h,k\colon T\to C\times _E D\), the projection-induced functor \[ (h=k)\longrightarrow (\pr _1\circ h=\pr _1\circ k)\times _{(f\circ \pr _1\circ h=g\circ \pr _2\circ k)}(\pr _2\circ h=\pr _2\circ k) \] is an equivalence. The two functors defining the pullback on the right are \[ \alpha \longmapsto (\tau \star k)\circ (f\star \alpha ), \qquad \beta \longmapsto (g\star \beta )\circ (\tau \star h); \] interchange supplies the identification needed to define the displayed functor. If \(C,D,E\) are animae, then \(C\times _E D\) is an anima.

Remark 1.3.8. The two clauses of the axiom may be understood as follows. The first clause says that every commutative square over the cospan \(C \xrightarrow {f} E \xleftarrow {g} D\) factors a commutative square through the pullback, compatibly with its chosen commutativity identification. The second clause says that an identification of two such functors consists of projection identifications \(\alpha \colon \pr _1\circ h=\pr _1\circ k\), \(\beta \colon \pr _2\circ h=\pr _2\circ k\), together with an identification \[ H\colon (\tau \star k)\circ (f\star \alpha )=(g\star \beta )\circ (\tau \star h). \] The compatibility \(H\) is part of the data. Formulating this as an equivalence of animae also controls identifications between such triples \((\alpha ,\beta ,H)\). The pullback on the right is the same construction applied to identification animae; this is a simultaneous specification of its universal property. See also Axiom B.6 of [Cisinski et al. (2026)].

Exercise 1.3.11. The construction of pullbacks of \(\infty \)-categories is functorial: given a commutative diagram

there is an induced functor \[ \phi \times _{\chi } \psi := (\phi \circ \pr _{C}, \psi \circ \pr _{D})\colon C \times _{E} D \to C' \times _{E'} D'. \] Show that if each of the functors \(\phi \), \(\psi \) and \(\chi \) is an equivalence, then so is \(\phi \times _{\chi } \psi \). Deduce that equivalences are stable under pullback: if \(g\colon D \to E\) is an equivalence and \(f\colon C \to E\) is an arbitrary functor, then the projection \(C \times _{E} D \to C\) is an equivalence.

Pullbacks of categories lead to the notion of a fiber of a functor:

Definition 1.3.12. Let \(f\colon C \to D\) be a functor. For every object \(x\) of \(D\), we define the fiber of \(f\) over \(x\) as the pullback

The definition of pullbacks of \(\infty \)-categories naturally leads to the notion of a pullback square.

Definition 1.3.13 (Pullback square). A commutative square

is called a pullback square if the induced functor \((s,t)\colon T \to C \times _{E} D\) is an equivalence.

The identification anima between two objects must also describe families of identifications. The following axiom makes this precise.

Axiom C.3 (Representability of identifications). For objects \(x,y\colon *\to C\), there is an identification \(\varepsilon \colon \const _x=\const _y\) of functors \((x=y)\to C\) exhibiting a pullback square

For each identification \(\alpha \colon x=y\), its restriction along \(\alpha \colon *\to (x=y)\) is identified with \(\alpha \), using the identifications supplied by the terminal universal property. We write this as \(\varepsilon (\alpha )=\alpha \).

Thus a functor \(S\to (x=y)\) supplies an identification between the constant functors \(S\to C\), by restricting \(\varepsilon \). The pullback universal property says that it records exactly this data, including identifications between such families.

We record one further property of coproducts.

Axiom C.4 (Universality of coproducts). Let \(I\) be a set, and let \(D_i\) be an \(\infty \)-category for every \(i \in I\).

(1)

Given functors \(f_i\colon C_i \to D_i\) for all \(i \in I\), the commutative square

is a pullback square.

(2)

For a functor \(h\colon E \to \bigsqcup _{i \in I} D_i\), the functor \[ \lra {\pr _E}_i \colon \bigsqcup _{i \in I}(E \times _{\bigsqcup _{i \in I} D_i} D_i) \to E \] is an equivalence.

(3)

The inclusions \(\{i\} \hookrightarrow I\) for \(i \in I\) induce an equivalence \(\bigsqcup _{i \in I} \{i\} \iso I\).

Remark 1.3.17. This is the categorical analogue of three basic facts about disjoint unions of sets: base change along an inclusion picks out the corresponding summand, every map into a disjoint union decomposes the source into the corresponding fibers, and every set is the disjoint union of its elements.

1.3.3 Functor categories

Functor categories let us package families of functors internally, and they will also allow us to define pushouts by duality in the next subsection.

Axiom C.5 (Functor category). The functor category between two \(\infty \)-categories \(C\) and \(D\) is an \(\infty \)-category denoted by \(\Fun (C,D)\). It comes equipped with a functor \(\ev \colon \Fun (C,D)\times C\to D\), called the evaluation functor. For another \(\infty \)-category \(E\) and a functor \(g\colon E\to \Fun (C,D)\), we may then define its uncurrying \(g^u\colon E\times C\to D\) as the composite \[ E\times C\xrightarrow {g\times \id _C}\Fun (C,D)\times C\xrightarrow {\ev }D. \] We demand that every functor \(f\colon E\times C\to D\) gives rise to a functor \(f_c\colon E\to \Fun (C,D)\), called the currying of \(f\), equipped with an identification \(f=(f_c)^u\). Moreover, for two functors \(g,h\colon E\to \Fun (C,D)\), uncurrying induces an equivalence \[ (g=h)\longrightarrow (g^u=h^u). \] Thus a specified identification between the uncurried functors lifts to one between \(g\) and \(h\). By Lemma 1.2.9, identifications between prescribed identifications lift as well. This allows us to translate back and forth between curried and uncurried functors.

In the case \(E=*\), objects \(*\to \Fun (C,D)\) of the functor category correspond to functors \(C\simeq *\times C\to D\) from \(C\) to \(D\), justifying the terminology for \(\Fun (C,D)\).

Exercise 1.3.18. Show that, for \(k\colon E'\to E\) and \(g\colon E\to \Fun (C,D)\), there is a preferred identification \((gk)^u=g^u(k\times \id _C)\), compatible with identifications of \(g\). Deduce the corresponding identification for currying. Hint: Expand uncurrying using evaluation and the identities expressing functoriality of products.

The functoriality of the product naturally provides functoriality for the functor categories:

Construction 1.3.19 (Functoriality of functor categories in \(C\) and \(D\)). Given a functor \(g\colon D \to E\), we define the functor \(g \circ -\colon \Fun (C,D) \to \Fun (C,E)\) as the currying of the composite \[ \Fun (C, D) \times C \xrightarrow {\ev } D \xrightarrow {g} E. \] Similarly, given a functor \(f\colon C \to D\), we define the functor \(- \circ f\colon \Fun (D,E) \to \Fun (C,E)\) as the currying of the composite \[ \Fun (D, E) \times C \xrightarrow {\id \times f} \Fun (D,E) \times D \xrightarrow {\ev } E. \] In a completely analogous way, every identification \(\beta \colon g = g'\) of functors \(D \to E\) induces an identification \((\beta \circ -) \colon (g \circ -) = (g' \circ -)\) of functors \(\Fun (C,D) \to \Fun (C, E)\), and similarly for the construction \(- \circ f\).

Alternative notations for these functors that we will frequently use are \(g_*\) and \(f^*\): \[ g_* := g \circ -\colon \Fun (C,D) \to \Fun (C,E), \qquad f^*:= - \circ f\colon \Fun (D,E) \to \Fun (C,D). \]

Exercise 1.3.20. Formulate and prove that the assignments \(g \mapsto (g \circ -)\) and \(f \mapsto (- \circ f)\) are functorial, in the sense that they respect identity functors and composition of functors.

Exercise 1.3.21. Given \(\infty \)-categories \(C\), \(D\) and \(E\), construct a composition functor \[ - \circ -\colon \Fun (D,E) \times \Fun (C,D) \to \Fun (C,E). \] Show that it reduces to the functors \(g \circ -\) and \(- \circ f\) when fixing one of the two variables.

Proposition 1.3.23 (Internal universal properties). Let \(T\) be an \(\infty \)-category and let \(\{C_i\}_{i \in I}\) be a set-indexed collection of \(\infty \)-categories. The canonical functors below are equivalences: \begin {align*} \Fun (T,*) &\iso *, & \Fun (\emptyset ,T) &\iso *, & \Fun (*,T) &\iso T, \\ \Fun \left (T,\prod _{i \in I}C_i\right ) &\iso \prod _{i \in I}\Fun (T,C_i), &&& \Fun \left (\bigsqcup _{i \in I}C_i,T\right ) &\iso \prod _{i \in I}\Fun (C_i,T). \end {align*}

Moreover, for functors \(f\colon C \to E\) and \(g\colon D \to E\) there is an equivalence \[ \Fun (T,C \times _E D) \iso \Fun (T,C) \times _{\Fun (T,E)} \Fun (T,D), \] and for \(\infty \)-categories \(C\) and \(D\) there is an equivalence \[ \Fun (T,\Fun (C,D)) \iso \Fun (T \times C,D). \]

Proof sketch.

These properties are developed in Section 1.7.1 of [Cisinski et al. (2026)], with binary products treated there. We sketch the construction of the inverses for the families considered here.

Construct the displayed functors from the projections, inclusions, evaluation, and currying maps. To construct their inverses as actual functors, first uncurry: the product and pullback axioms assemble the resulting evaluation maps, after which one curries again. In the coproduct case, use universality of coproducts to distribute the product with the parameter category over \(\bigsqcup _iC_i\). In the pullback case, the projection identifications must agree over the base. After uncurrying, this agreement is supplied by the pullback constructor; the equivalence of identification animae in Axiom C.5 lifts it back. The pullback universal property then gives the inverse relation; see also Lemma 1.7.11 of [Cisinski et al. (2026)]. A complete proof is given in the supplementary material. □

1.3.4 Pushouts of \(\infty \)-categories

Using functor categories, we may now dualize the notion of pullback square and define pushouts:

Definition 1.3.24 (Pushout square). A commutative square of \(\infty \)-categories

is called a pushout square (or cocartesian) if, for every \(\infty \)-category \(E\), the induced square

is a pullback square.

We refer to the \(\infty \)-category \(C \sqcup _E D\) as the pushout of \(C\) and \(D\) along \(E\).
