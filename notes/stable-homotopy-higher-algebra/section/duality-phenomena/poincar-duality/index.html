Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Part I · Chapter 11 · Section 11.4

Poincaré duality

← Previous chapterChapter 10 → Next chapterChapter 12 ← Previous sectionSection 11.3 → Next sectionSection 12.1

For a closed oriented smooth \(n\)-manifold \(M\), classical Poincaré duality gives an isomorphism \[ - \cap [M] \colon H^{n-k}(M) \iso H_k(M) \]

by cap product with the fundamental class. We now derive this from Atiyah duality and extend it to other cohomology theories. Atiyah duality identifies the dual of \(\S [M]\) with \(\th (-T_M)\). An \(E\)-orientation of \(T_M\) gives a Thom isomorphism for this spectrum; combined with Atiyah duality, this yields an isomorphism between \(E\)-homology and shifted \(E\)-cohomology.

Theorem 11.4.1 (Poincaré duality). Let \(E\) be a commutative ring spectrum, and let \(M\) be a closed smooth \(n\)-manifold equipped with an \(E\)-orientation of the virtual tangent bundle \([T_M]\), or equivalently of \(-T_M\) by Lemma 10.4.9. Then there is an isomorphism of spectra \[ \S [M] \otimes E \cong \hom (\S [M],E[n]). \] In particular, for every \(k \in \Z \) there is an isomorphism \[ E_k(M) \cong E^{n-k}(M). \]

Proof. By Theorem 11.3.10, the dual of the spectrum \(\S [M]\) in \(\Sp \) is \(\th (-T_M)\), and in particular we obtain an isomorphism of spectra \[ \S [M] \otimes E \cong \hom (\th (-T_M), E). \] By Lemma 10.4.9, the given orientation of \([T_M]\) induces an orientation of \(-T_M\). Since this virtual bundle has rank \(-n\), the cohomological Thom isomorphism of Theorem 10.4.6 gives \[ \hom (\th (-T_M),E)\cong \hom (\S [M][-n],E)\cong \hom (\S [M],E[n]). \] This proves the first claim. Applying \(\pi _k(-)\) gives the second. □

The preceding proof is the abstract form of Poincaré duality. We next relate it to the classical fundamental-class formulation. The orientation of \(-T_M\) gives a Thom class \(u_{-T_M}\colon \th (-T_M) \to E[-n]\). Define the \(E\)-fundamental class \[ [M]_E \in E_n(M)=\pi _n(E\otimes \S [M]) \] to be represented, after shifting, by the composite \[ \S \xrightarrow {\coev } \S [M]\otimes \th (-T_M) \xrightarrow {\id \otimes u_{-T_M}} \S [M]\otimes E[-n]. \] Using the embedding \(M\hookrightarrow \R ^N\) chosen in the proof of Theorem 11.3.10, this is concretely the class determined by the Pontryagin–Thom collapse \(S^N\to \Th (\nu )\), followed by the diagonal \(\Delta _*\colon \Th (\nu )\to \Th (\nu )\wedge M_+\) and then the Thom class of the normal bundle on the first factor. For \(E=\Z \), restricting this construction to a coordinate neighborhood of \(m\in M\) shows that its image in \(H_n(M,M\setminus \{m\};\Z )\) is the local generator determined by the orientation. Thus it is the classical fundamental class.

Proposition 11.4.2. The inverse of the isomorphism in Theorem 11.4.1 is cap product with \([M]_E\): \[ -\cap [M]_E\colon E^{n-k}(M)\iso E_k(M). \]

Proof. Represent a cohomology class by a map \(a\colon \S [M]\to E[n-k]\). Cap product with \([M]_E\) is represented, after shifting, by the composite \[ \begin {aligned} \S &\xrightarrow {[M]_E} \S [M]\otimes E[-n] \xrightarrow {\S [\Delta ]\otimes \id } \S [M]\otimes \S [M]\otimes E[-n] \\ &\xrightarrow {\id \otimes a\otimes \id } \S [M]\otimes E[n-k]\otimes E[-n] \xrightarrow {\id \otimes \mu } \S [M]\otimes E[-k]. \end {aligned} \] Let \(\delta \colon \th (-T_M)\to \S [M]\otimes \th (-T_M)\) be the Thom diagonal. The construction of \(\coev \) gives \[ (\S [\Delta ]\otimes \id )\circ \coev \simeq (\id \otimes \delta )\circ \coev \colon \S \longrightarrow \S [M]\otimes \S [M]\otimes \th (-T_M). \] Indeed, both sides apply the Pontryagin–Thom collapse and record twice the point of \(M\) over which the normal vector lies. Substituting the definition of \([M]_E\) therefore rewrites the cap product as \[ \S \xrightarrow {\coev }\S [M]\otimes \th (-T_M) \xrightarrow {\id \otimes b}\S [M]\otimes E[-k], \] where \[ b\colon \th (-T_M)\xrightarrow {\delta }\S [M]\otimes \th (-T_M) \xrightarrow {a\otimes u_{-T_M}}E[n-k]\otimes E[-n] \xrightarrow {\mu }E[-k]. \] As in the proof of Corollary 10.4.8, the map \((\id \otimes u_{-T_M})\circ \delta \) extends \(E\)-linearly to the Thom isomorphism: on each fiber it applies the chosen trivialization and records the point of \(M\) over which the fiber lies. Consequently, \(b\) is the image of \(a\) under the inverse cohomological Thom isomorphism. The triangle identities identify \(b\mapsto (\id \otimes b)\circ \coev \) with the inverse of \(\S [M]\otimes E\cong \hom (\th (-T_M),E)\), after shifting by \(-k\). The displayed cap product is therefore the inverse of the composite used in Theorem 11.4.1. □

Example 11.4.3. For \(E=\F _2\), every real vector bundle is \(E\)-orientable because \(\Line _{\F _2}\simeq *\). Thus every closed smooth manifold satisfies mod-\(2\) Poincaré duality. For \(E=\Z \), Example 10.4.10 identifies an \(\Z \)-orientation with a classical orientation. This recovers integral Poincaré duality for oriented manifolds.

Example 11.4.4. The manifold \(\R P^2\) is not integrally orientable, and integral Poincaré duality indeed fails: \(H_0(\R P^2;\Z )\cong \Z \), whereas \(H^2(\R P^2;\Z )\cong \Z /2\). With \(\F _2\)-coefficients, it is orientable and Poincaré duality holds.

Remark 11.4.5. A \(\mathrm {Spin}^c\)-structure on \(M\) determines a \(\KU \)-orientation of \([T_M]\) through the construction of Atiyah et al. (1964), Theorem 12.3(ii), after stabilization. Moreover, Example 10.4.11 provides a canonical \(\MO \)-orientation of every real vector bundle. Thus every closed smooth manifold satisfies \(\MO \)-Poincaré duality.

Remark 11.4.6. For a compact smooth \(n\)-manifold \(M\) with boundary, equipped with an \(E\)-orientation of \([T_M]\), Poincaré–Lefschetz duality gives \[ E_k(M)\cong E^{n-k}(M,\partial M). \] This uses the boundary version of Atiyah duality, which we have not proved here: \[ D\bigl (\Sigma ^\infty \Pi _\infty (M/\partial M)\bigr )\cong \th (-T_M); \] see Atiyah (1961b), Proposition 3.2. Tensoring with \(E\) and applying the Thom isomorphism gives the displayed identification of the homology of \(M\) with its relative cohomology.

Exercises

Exercise 11.1 (Duality for the cartesian monoidal structure). Let \(C\) be an \(\infty \)-category with finite products, equipped with its cartesian symmetric monoidal structure. Show that an object of \(C\) is dualizable if and only if it is terminal. Deduce the corresponding statement for the cocartesian monoidal structure on an \(\infty \)-category with finite coproducts.

Exercise 11.2 (Duality and cofiber sequences). Let \(C\) be a stably symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category.

(1)

Show that the zero object is dualizable.

(2)

Let \(f\colon X\to Y\) be a morphism between dualizable objects. Show that \(\cofib (f)\) is dualizable with dual \(\fib (f^\vee \colon Y^\vee \to X^\vee )\).

(3)

Deduce that the full subcategory of dualizable objects is closed under finite colimits. Apply this to \(C=\Sp \) to give a direct proof of Corollary 11.2.8.

Hint: Use the adjunction characterization in Proposition 11.1.4 and the exactness of tensor products.

Exercise 11.3 (The dual of a quotient module). Let \(R\) be a commutative ring spectrum, let \(x\in \pi _0(R)\), and let \(R/x\) be the cofiber of multiplication by \(x\) on \(R\). The \(R\)-module \(R/x\) is perfect and hence dualizable. Compute its \(R\)-linear dual, showing that \[ \hom _R(R/x,R)\cong (R/x)[-1]. \]

Exercise 11.4 (Duals of finite wedges of spheres). Show that \(D(\S [n])\cong \S [-n]\) for every \(n\in \Z \). Deduce that \[ D\left (\bigoplus _{i=1}^r\S [n_i]\right ) \cong \bigoplus _{i=1}^r\S [-n_i]. \] Show that the analogous formula for an infinite wedge has a product on the right, and that an infinite wedge of shifted spheres is not dualizable.

Exercise 11.5 (Atiyah duality for a sphere). Let \(n\geq 0\). Use the standard embedding \(S^n\hookrightarrow \R ^{n+1}\) and its trivial normal line bundle to identify \(\th (-T_{S^n})\). Show that Atiyah duality gives \[ D(\S [S^n])\cong \S \oplus \S [-n]. \] Compare this with the splitting \(\S [S^n]\cong \S \oplus \S [n]\).

Exercise 11.6 (Consequences of Poincaré duality). Let \(M\) be a closed oriented smooth \(n\)-manifold.

(1)

Show that the pairing \[ H^k(M;\Q )\otimes _{\Q }H^{n-k}(M;\Q )\longrightarrow \Q , \qquad a\otimes b\longmapsto \langle a\smile b,[M]\rangle , \] is nondegenerate.

(2)

Deduce that the Betti numbers satisfy \(b_k(M)=b_{n-k}(M)\), and that \(M\) has Euler characteristic zero if \(n\) is odd.

(3)

Use mod-\(2\) Poincaré duality to show that every closed smooth odd-dimensional manifold has Euler characteristic zero, without assuming orientability.

Exercise 11.7 (Invertible spectra). Show that every invertible spectrum is isomorphic to \(\S [n]\) for a unique \(n\in \Z \). Conclude that the definitions of \(\Pic (\Sp )\) in Definition 10.2.7, Definition 11.1.6 agree. Let \(X\) be an invertible spectrum with inverse \(Y\), and proceed as follows:

(1)

Show that \(X\) is compact. Deduce that \(X\) is bounded below and that \(\bigoplus _kH_k(X)\) is a finitely generated abelian group.

(2)

Let \(k\) be a field. Show that \(H_*(X\otimes Y;k)\cong H_*(X;k)\otimes _kH_*(Y;k)\), and conclude that \(H_*(X;k)\) is one-dimensional.

(3)

Deduce that \(H_*(X)\cong \Z \), concentrated in a single degree \(n\).

(4)

Show that \(X\cong \S [n]\).
