Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Part II · Chapter 17 · Section 17.4

Monads and monadicity

← Previous chapterChapter 16 → Next chapterChapter 18 ← Previous sectionSection 17.3 → Next sectionSection 18.1

For an adjunction \(F\colon C\rightleftarrows D\noloc G\), every object \(d\in D\) gives an object \(Gd\in C\) with an action \(GF(Gd)\to Gd\), induced by the counit. We ask when this action determines \(d\): can \(D\) be recovered as the \(\infty \)-category of modules over the endofunctor \(GF\)? To formulate this question, we first equip endofunctors with the monoidal structure given by composition.

Evaluation exhibits \(\Fun (C,C)\) as the endomorphism object of \(C\) in \((\Cat _{\infty },\times )\), since currying gives \[ \Hom _{\Cat _{\infty }}(X,\Fun (C,C))\iso \Hom _{\Cat _{\infty }}(X\times C,C). \] By Corollary 17.3.3, Theorem 15.3.11, it therefore carries a monoidal structure with tensor product given by composition, and acts on \(C\) by evaluation. When \(C\) is not small, we work in a larger universe.

Definition 17.4.1 ([Lurie (2017), Definition 4.7.0.1]). A monad on an \(\infty \)-category \(C\) is an associative algebra object \[ T \in \Alg (\Fun (C,C)) \] for the composition monoidal structure. Its Eilenberg–Moore category is the \(\infty \)-category \[ \LMod _T(C) \] of left \(T\)-module objects in \(C\), formed using the evaluation left tensoring constructed above. It comes with a forgetful functor \(U_T\colon \LMod _T(C) \to C\).

Informally, the algebra structure consists of a unit \(\id _C \to T\) and a multiplication \(T \circ T \to T\), together with all coherent associativity and unitality data. A left \(T\)-module is an object \(c \in C\) with a coherent action \(T(c) \to c\).

Consider an adjunction \[ F\colon C \rightleftarrows D \noloc G, \] and let \(\eta \) and \(\varepsilon \) denote its unit and counit. The \(\infty \)-category \(\Fun (D,C)\) is left-tensored over \(\Fun (C,C)\) by postcomposition. For \(S\in \Fun (C,C)\), the adjunction gives a natural isomorphism \[ \Hom _{\Fun (C,C)}(S,GF)\iso \Hom _{\Fun (D,C)}(SG,G). \] It sends \(\alpha \colon S\to GF\) to \(G\varepsilon \circ \alpha G\); its inverse sends \(\beta \colon SG\to G\) to \(\beta F\circ S\eta \). The triangle identities show that these are inverse. Thus \(GF\), with the action \(G\varepsilon \colon GFG\to G\), is an endomorphism object of \(G\); see also [Lurie (2017), Lemma 4.7.3.1]. By Corollary 17.3.3, \(GF\) acquires a monad structure, with unit \(\eta \) and multiplication \(G\varepsilon F\). Under the equivalence \[ \LMod _{GF}(\Fun (D,C))\simeq \Fun (D,\LMod _{GF}(C)), \] the resulting \(GF\)-module \(G\) in \(\Fun (D,C)\) corresponds to a lift of \(G\): \[ D\longrightarrow \LMod _{GF}(C),\qquad d\longmapsto \bigl (Gd,\;G\varepsilon _d\colon GFGd\to Gd\bigr ). \]

Definition 17.4.2. A right adjoint \(G\colon D\to C\), with left adjoint \(F\), is monadic if the induced functor \(D\to \LMod _{GF}(C)\) is an equivalence.

The Barr–Beck theorem gives a criterion for monadicity in terms of conservativity and geometric realizations. The relevant simplicial objects are those that become split after applying \(G\); we first explain this condition.

Recall the augmented simplicial objects with extra degeneracies from Definition 5.2.3. We use the following terminology.

Definition 17.4.3. An augmented simplicial object is split if it admits extra degeneracies in the sense of Definition 5.2.3. A simplicial object is split if it extends to a split augmented simplicial object.

Let \(G\colon D\to C\) be a functor. A simplicial object \(X_\bullet \) of \(D\) is \(G\)-split if \(GX_\bullet \) is split.

Our convention differs from [Lurie (2017), Definition 4.7.2.2] by reversing the order on the finite linear orders.

Lemma 17.4.4 (Absolute realization of split simplicial objects). Every split augmented simplicial object is a colimit diagram. In particular, every split simplicial object admits a geometric realization, and every functor preserves this realization.

Proof. By Lemma 5.2.4, a split augmented simplicial object exhibits its augmentation as a colimit. Postcomposition with any functor preserves the extra degeneracies, so the same lemma shows that it preserves this colimit. □

The forgetful functor \(U_T\) is conservative and admits a left adjoint \(F_T\), whose underlying endofunctor is \(T\). Moreover, every \(U_T\)-split simplicial object of \(\LMod _T(C)\) admits a geometric realization which is preserved by \(U_T\); see [Lurie (2017), Corollary 4.2.3.2, Corollary 4.2.4.8, Lemma 4.7.3.12].

Theorem 17.4.5 (Barr–Beck monadicity, [Lurie (2017), Theorem 4.7.3.5]). Let \(G\colon D \to C\) be a conservative functor admitting a left adjoint. Assume that \(D\) admits geometric realizations of \(G\)-split simplicial objects and that \(G\) preserves them. Then \(G\) is monadic.

Proof sketch. Write \(F\dashv G\). The adjunction gives each object \(d\in D\) an augmented simplicial resolution whose terms are \((FG)^{n+1}d\). After applying \(G\), the unit of the adjunction supplies an extra degeneracy, so this resolution is \(G\)-split. The hypotheses and conservativity of \(G\) imply that its realization is \(d\). The corresponding free-module resolution in \(\LMod _{GF}(C)\) is also realized by its augmentation. The induced functor \(D\to \LMod _{GF}(C)\) identifies free objects and their mapping animae by the two free-forgetful adjunctions. Realizing the free resolutions recovers every \(GF\)-module from an object of \(D\); computing maps out of these realizations as limits gives full faithfulness. The coherent construction and the proof are given in [Lurie (2017), Theorem 4.7.3.5]. □

For the free-module adjunction, the associated monad is \(A\otimes -\). The forgetful functor is always monadic:

Proposition 17.4.6 (Monadicity of module categories). Let \(C\) be a monoidal \(\infty \)-category, let \(D\) be left-tensored over \(C\), and let \(A \in \Alg (C)\). The forgetful functor \[ U_A\colon \LMod _A(D) \longrightarrow D \] is monadic, and its associated monad is \(A \otimes -\colon D \to D\), with unit and multiplication induced by those of \(A\).

Proof. The left adjoint exists by Proposition 17.1.14, and its composite with \(U_A\) is \(A \otimes -\). The forgetful functor is conservative by [Lurie (2017), Corollary 4.2.3.2]. Moreover, every \(U_A\)-split simplicial object admits a geometric realization which is preserved by \(U_A\); this is [Lurie (2017), Lemma 4.7.3.12]. The claim follows from Theorem 17.4.5. □

Proposition 17.4.7 (Limits and colimits of Eilenberg–Moore categories). Let \(T\) be a monad on an \(\infty \)-category \(C\), let \(U_T\colon \LMod _T(C)\to C\) be its forgetful functor, and let \(I\) be a small \(\infty \)-category.

(1)

If \(C\) admits \(I\)-indexed limits, then \(U_T\) creates and preserves them.

(2)

If \(C\) admits \(I\)-indexed colimits and \(T\) preserves them, then \(U_T\) creates and preserves them.

Proof. For (1), apply Proposition 17.1.16(1) to the evaluation left tensoring of \(C\) over \(\Fun (C,C)\).

For (2), restrict the evaluation left tensoring to the full subcategory of \(\Fun (C,C)\) spanned by the endofunctors preserving \(I\)-indexed colimits. This subcategory contains the identity and is closed under composition, so it is monoidal. Since \(T\) preserves these colimits, its algebra structure belongs to this full monoidal subcategory. The \(\infty \)-category of \(T\)-modules is unchanged by this restriction, and every object of the acting \(\infty \)-category now preserves \(I\)-indexed colimits. Thus Proposition 17.1.16(2) applies. □

Applied to the monad \(A\otimes -\) of Proposition 17.4.6, part (2) shows that the forgetful functor \(\LMod _A(D)\to D\) creates any colimits which exist in \(D\) and are preserved by \(A\otimes -\). For a fixed algebra this sharpens Proposition 17.1.16(2), whose more uniform hypothesis ensures the same conclusion simultaneously for every algebra in \(C\).

Exercises

Exercise 17.1 (Free modules). Let \(C\) be a presentably monoidal \(\infty \)-category and let \(A\in \Alg (C)\).

(1)

Use Proposition 17.1.14 to show that the free-module functor preserves coproducts, and compute the free \(A\)-module on a finite coproduct.

(2)

Specialize to \(C=\Sp \) and identify the free \(R\)-module on \(\S [n]\) for an associative ring spectrum \(R\). Deduce that \(\pi _0\Hom _{\LMod _R}(R[n],M)\cong \pi _n(M)\) for every left \(R\)-module \(M\).

Exercise 17.2 (Transitivity of extension of scalars). Let \(A\to B\to C\) be morphisms of associative algebras in a presentably monoidal \(\infty \)-category. For a left \(A\)-module \(M\), construct a natural isomorphism \[ C\otimes _B(B\otimes _AM)\simeq C\otimes _AM. \] Check that it identifies the composite of the two extension-of-scalars adjunctions with extension of scalars along \(A\to C\).
