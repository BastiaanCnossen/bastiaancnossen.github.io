Filtered and sifted ∞-categories

Filtered and sifted colimits are distinguished by their compatibility with finite limits and finite products. This section introduces their indexing categories and records the properties needed elsewhere in the book.

Definition 21.6.1. An \(\infty \)-category \(K\) is called filtered if for every finite diagram \(F\colon I \to K\) in \(K\) there exists an object \(W \in K\) with a cocone \(F \to \const _W\); equivalently, \(F\) extends to a functor \(\widetilde {F}\colon I^{\triangleright } \to K\).

Example 21.6.2. Every directed poset is a filtered \(\infty \)-category. In particular, the poset \((\N , \leq )\) of natural numbers is filtered.

More generally, any category which is filtered in the classical sense is filtered as an \(\infty \)-category.

The following important characterization of filtered \(\infty \)-categories will be treated as a black box:

Theorem 21.6.3 ([Lurie (2009), Proposition 5.3.3.3]). An \(\infty \)-category \(K\) is filtered if and only if the functor \(\colim _K\colon \Fun (K,\An ) \to \An \) preserves finite limits.

Let us list various consequences of this characterization:

Corollary 21.6.4. A filtered colimit \(\colim _K *\) of terminal animae is again terminal.

Proof. A terminal anima is a limit over the finite category \(\emptyset \). □

Corollary 21.6.5. The functor \(\Omega \colon \An _* \to \An _*\) preserves filtered colimits.

Proof. By construction, this functor is given as the composite \[ \An _* \simeq \An _* \times _{\An _*} \An _* \hookrightarrow \Fun ([1],\An _*) \times _{\An _*} \Fun ([1],\An _*) \simeq \Fun (\pullback , \An _*) \xrightarrow {\lim } \An _*. \] The last functor preserves filtered colimits by Proposition 21.6.3. The inclusion \(\An _* \simeq (\An _*)_* \to \Fun ([1],\An _*)\) preserves filtered colimits by Corollary 21.6.4. □

Corollary 21.6.6. For every \(n \geq 0\), the functor \(\pi _n\colon \An _* \to \Set \) preserves filtered colimits.

Proof. We may factor this functor as follows: \[ \An _* \xrightarrow {\Omega ^n} \An _* \xrightarrow {\fgt } \An \xrightarrow {\pi _0} \Set . \] Since each of these functors preserves filtered colimits, the claim follows. □

Lemma 21.6.7. Every filtered \(\infty \)-category \(K\) is weakly contractible.

Proof. For every \(\infty \)-category \(I\), its geometric realization may be computed as the colimit in \(\An \) of the constant \(I\)-indexed diagram with value \(*\). Indeed, for every anima \(X\) there are natural isomorphisms \begin {align*} \Hom _{\An }\left (\colim _{i \in I} *,X\right ) &\simeq \Nat (\const _*,\const _X) \\ &\simeq \Fun (I,X) \\ &\simeq \Hom _{\An }(\geom {I},X). \end {align*}

Indeed, such a natural transformation chooses a point of \(X\) for every object of \(I\), a path between the corresponding points for every morphism of \(I\), and all the higher coherence data; this is precisely a functor \(I \to X\). The last isomorphism follows from the universal property of the localization \(I \to \geom {I}\). The Yoneda lemma therefore gives \[ \geom {I} \simeq \colim _{i \in I} *. \] Applying this to the filtered \(\infty \)-category \(K\), we conclude from Corollary 21.6.4 that \[ \geom {K} \simeq \colim _{k \in K} * \simeq *. \] □

Definition 21.6.8. An \(\infty \)-category \(I\) is called sifted if it is nonempty and the diagonal functor \(\Delta \colon I \to I \times I\) is final.

Proposition 21.6.9 (The simplex category is sifted). The \(\infty \)-category \(\simp \catop \) is sifted.

Proof. The \(\infty \)-category \(\simp \catop \) is nonempty, and the diagonal \(\simp \to \simp \times \simp \) is initial by Reference ? of [Cisinski et al. (2026)]. Passing to opposite categories shows that the diagonal of \(\simp \catop \) is final. □

Lemma 21.6.10. Every filtered \(\infty \)-category is sifted.

Proof. Let \(K\) be a filtered \(\infty \)-category. We need to show that for every object \((k,l) \in K \times K\) the relative slice category \(K \times _{K \times K} (K \times K)_{(k,l)/}\) is weakly contractible. We will show that this relative slice category is in fact a filtered \(\infty \)-category, so that Lemma 21.6.7 implies the claim. To this end, consider a finite \(\infty \)-category \(I\) and a functor \(F\colon I \to K \times _{K \times K} (K \times K)_{(k,l)/}\). Lemma 21.6.11. Let \(C\) be an \(\infty \)-category that admits finite products, and assume that for every object \(X \in C\) the functor \(X \times -\colon C \to C\) preserves colimits. Then the product functor \(- \times -\colon C \times C \to C\) preserves sifted colimits.

Proof. Let \(I\) be a sifted \(\infty \)-category and let \(X,Y\colon I \to C\) be two functors. We need to show that the map \[ \colim _{i \in I} (X_i \times Y_i) \to (\colim _{i \in I} X_i) \times (\colim _{i \in I} Y_i) \] is an equivalence. This follows from the following computation: \begin {align*} \colim _{i \in I} (X_i \times Y_i) &\overset {(1)}{\simeq } \colim _{(i,j) \in I \times I} (X_i \times Y_j) \\ &\overset {(2)}{\simeq }\colim _{i \in I} \colim _{j \in I} (X_i \times Y_j) \\ &\overset {(3)}{\simeq } \colim _{i \in I} (X_i \times \colim _{j \in I} Y_j) \\ &\overset {(4)}{\simeq } (\colim _{i \in I} X_i) \times (\colim _{j \in I} Y_j). \end {align*}

Here equivalence (1) holds because the diagonal functor \(I \to I \times I, i \mapsto (i,i)\) is final by assumption on \(I\). The equivalence (2) is an instance of Lemma 21.2.10. The equivalences (3) and (4) hold because the product in \(C\) preserves colimits in each variable. □

Lemma 21.6.12. The geometric realization functor \(\abs {-}\colon \s \An \to \An \) preserves finite products.

Proof. By definition, this functor is \(\colim _{[n] \in \simp \catop }\colon \Fun (\simp \catop ,\An ) \to \An \), and the claim is equivalent to the statement that the product functor \(- \times -\colon \An \times \An \to \An \) preserves \(\simp \catop \)-indexed colimits. The \(\infty \)-category \(\simp \catop \) is sifted by Proposition 21.6.9. Furthermore, for an anima \(X\) the functor \(X \times - \colon \An \to \An \) admits a right adjoint \(\Hom _{\An }(X,-)\colon \An \to \An \), hence preserves all colimits. This verifies the assumptions of Lemma 21.6.11, proving the claim. □
