Stable homotopy theory and higher algebra · Part III · Chapter 21 · Section 21.7

A comparison theorem

The argument below is the monad-free comparison mechanism underlying the Barr–Beck–Lurie monadicity theorem. Suppose that two categories of structured objects come with forgetful functors to the same \(\infty \)-category. To compare them, it is often enough to compare their free objects: every object can be reconstructed from free ones by a canonical simplicial resolution. We isolate this mechanism here without introducing the structured objects classified by monads.

We first recall the class of simplicial objects for which the relevant geometric realizations exist formally. Let \(\simp _{-\infty }\) be the category whose objects are the symbols \([n]\) for \(n \geq -1\) and whose morphisms \([m] \to [n]\) are the order-preserving maps \[ \{-\infty \} \sqcup [m] \longrightarrow \{-\infty \} \sqcup [n] \] which preserve \(-\infty \). There are inclusions \(\simp \subseteq \simp _+ \subseteq \simp _{-\infty }\), where a morphism belongs to \(\simp _+\) precisely when the inverse image of \(-\infty \) is \(\{-\infty \}\).

Definition 21.7.1 ([Lurie (2017), Definition 4.7.2.2]). An augmented simplicial object \(X\colon \simp _+\catop \to C\) is split if it extends to a functor \(\simp _{-\infty }\catop \to C\). A simplicial object is split if it extends to a split augmented simplicial object.

Let \(G\colon D \to C\) be a functor. A simplicial object \(X_\bullet \colon \simp \catop \to D\) is \(G\)-split if \(GX_\bullet \) is split in \(C\).

Lemma 21.7.2 (Absolute realization of split simplicial objects). Every split augmented simplicial object is a colimit diagram. In particular, every split simplicial object admits a geometric realization, and every functor preserves this realization.

Proof. See Reference ? of [Cisinski et al. (2026)]. □

The following theorem is the monad-free form of [Lurie (2017), Corollary 4.7.3.16]. The bar-resolution argument in its proof is also the engine of the proof of the \(\infty \)-categorical Barr–Beck theorem [Lurie (2017), Theorem 4.7.3.5, Proposition 4.7.3.14].

Theorem 21.7.3 (Comparison over a base). Let

be a triangle equipped with a natural isomorphism \[ \theta \colon G'K \iso G \] Assume that \(G\) and \(G'\) admit left adjoints \(F\) and \(F'\), and that the mate \(\theta ^\flat \colon F' \to KF\) is an isomorphism. Assume furthermore that:

(1)

The functors \(G\) and \(G'\) are conservative;

(2)

The \(\infty \)-category \(D\) admits geometric realizations of \(G\)-split simplicial objects, and \(G\) preserves them;

(3)

The \(\infty \)-category \(D'\) admits geometric realizations of \(G'\)-split simplicial objects, and \(G'\) preserves them.

Then \(K\) is an equivalence of \(\infty \)-categories.

The strategy is to resolve every object functorially by objects in the image of the relevant left adjoint. The mate condition identifies the two classes of free objects, while the hypotheses on split simplicial objects ensure that both categories can reconstruct arbitrary objects from these resolutions.

Proof. The bar-resolution argument is proved in Reference ? of [Cisinski et al. (2026)]. It resolves every object by free objects, uses the mate condition to identify the two free-object theories, and then proves full faithfulness and essential surjectivity by passing to the resulting geometric realizations. □
