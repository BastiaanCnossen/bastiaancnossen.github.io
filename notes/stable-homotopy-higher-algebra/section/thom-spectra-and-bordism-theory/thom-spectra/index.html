Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Part I · Chapter 10 · Section 10.2

Thom spectra

The construction of the previous section assigns Thom spaces to vector bundles. For applications such as Atiyah duality, we also need a Thom construction for virtual vector bundles. The normal bundle \(\nu \) of an embedding \(M \hookrightarrow \R ^{n+k}\) of a closed \(n\)-manifold is not an invariant of \(M\): enlarging the ambient space changes it by trivial summands. What is intrinsic is the stable normal bundle, the virtual vector bundle \[ \nu - \ul {\R ^k} \quad = \quad \ul {\R ^n} - T_M \] of virtual rank \(0\), which is classified by a map \(M \to \bB O\). Closely related is the virtual bundle \(-T_M = \nu - \ul {\R ^{n+k}}\) of virtual rank \(-n\), which occurs in Atiyah duality in Section 11.3. These virtual bundles need not be represented by vector bundles, so Definition 10.1.2 does not apply to them in general.

To extend the construction, we describe the underlying anima of a Thom space as the colimit of the family of pointed spheres associated to a vector bundle. After stabilization, these spheres become invertible spectra, allowing us to extend the construction to virtual bundles.

10.2.1 Thom animae

We start by making precise the claim that Thom spaces are colimits of parametrized families of spheres.

Definition 10.2.1 (Spherical fibration). Let \(B\) be an anima and let \(n \geq 0\). A rank \(n\) spherical fibration over \(B\) is a functor \(\xi \colon B \to \An _*\) which lands in the full subcategory spanned by the \(n\)-sphere \(S^n\). A spherical fibration is a rank \(n\) spherical fibration for some \(n\).

Definition 10.2.2 (Thom anima). We define the Thom anima of a spherical fibration \(\xi \colon B \to \An _*\) as the pointed anima given by its colimit: \[ \Th (\xi ) \quad := \quad \colim (\xi \colon B \to \An _*). \]

Recall from Corollary 23.2.8 the straightening-unstraightening equivalence \(\An _{/B} \simeq \Fun (B,\An )\). Passing to pointed objects, this provides an equivalence \[ \Fun (B,\An _*) \; \simeq \; \Fun (B,\An )_* \; \simeq \; (\An _{/B})_*, \] so that a rank \(n\) spherical fibration over \(B\) is the same as a pair \((p,s)\) of a morphism \(p\colon A \to B\) of animae and a section \(s\colon B \to A\) of \(p\) such that each fiber \(A_b\) of \(p\) is isomorphic to the \(n\)-sphere \(S^n\).

Remark 10.2.3 (Thom anima as a pushout). Suppose that a functor \(\xi \colon B\to \An _*\) corresponds to a pointed object \(B\xrightarrow {s}A\to B\) of \(\An _{/B}\). Then \[ \colim _B\xi \; \simeq \; A \amalg _B *, \] where the pushout is formed in \(\An \) and pointed by the map from \(*\). In particular, if \(\xi \) is a spherical fibration, this pushout is its Thom anima.

Indeed, the colimit of the underlying functor \(B\to \An \) is \(A\) by Proposition 23.5.2: its unstraightening is the anima \(A\), so the localization in that result does not change it. The colimit of the constant basepoint diagram is \(B\). The formula now follows by computing colimits in pointed objects as pushouts.

Example 10.2.4 (Sphere bundle). Let \(X\) be a topological space and consider an \(n\)-dimensional sphere bundle \(Y \to X\) over \(X\), i.e. a fiber bundle whose typical fiber is \(S^n\) and whose structure group is the homeomorphism group \(\mathrm {Homeo}_*(S^n)\). The fixed basepoint of \(S^n\) defines a section of \(Y\to X\). After applying \(\Pi _{\infty }\), this bundle with section corresponds to a rank \(n\) spherical fibration over \(\Pi _{\infty }(X)\): by Proposition 2.3.19, Corollary 2.4.15, the fibers of \(\Pi _{\infty }(Y)\to \Pi _{\infty }(X)\) are the spheres \(S^n\).

Example 10.2.5 (Spherical fibration of vector bundle). Given a rank \(n\) vector bundle \(p\colon E \to X\), we may apply the previous example to the fiberwise one-point compactification \(S^E \to X\) from Construction 10.1.1. We denote the resulting spherical fibration by \[ \xi _E\colon \Pi _{\infty }(X) \to \An _*. \] Informally, \(\xi _E\) is the functor which sends a point \(x \in X\) to \(\Pi _{\infty }(S^{E_x})\), where \(S^{E_x}\) is the one-point compactification of the fiber \(E_x\) of \(p\) over \(x\).

We can now identify the underlying pointed anima of a Thom space.

Proposition 10.2.6. Let \(p\colon E\to X\) be a vector bundle over a paracompact Hausdorff space of the homotopy type of a cell complex. Then the underlying pointed anima of its Thom space is isomorphic to the Thom anima of \(\xi _E\): \[ \Pi _{\infty }(\Th (E)) \quad \cong \quad \Th (\xi _E). \]

Proof. Choose a homotopy equivalence \(f\colon X'\to X\) with \(X'\) a cell complex. By Lemma 10.1.8, the induced map \(\Th (f^*E)\to \Th (E)\) is a pointed homotopy equivalence. On the other hand, \(\xi _{f^*E}\) is the pullback of \(\xi _E\) along the isomorphism \(\Pi _{\infty }(f)\), so their colimits are isomorphic. We may therefore assume that \(X\) is a cell complex.

Under pointed straightening, \(\xi _E\) corresponds to the infinity section \(X\to S^E\), so we obtain \[ \Th (\xi _E) \simeq \Pi _{\infty }(S^E)\amalg _{\Pi _{\infty }(X)} * \] by Remark 10.2.3. Since the infinity section is a relative cell complex by Lemma 10.1.8, we may applying Proposition 2.3.9 to its pushout along \(X\to *\) to compute \[ \Pi _{\infty }(S^E)\amalg _{\Pi _{\infty }(X)} * \simeq \Pi _{\infty }\big (S^E/s_{\infty }(X)\big ) = \Pi _{\infty }(\Th (E)). \qedhere \] □

10.2.2 Thom spectra

We now replace pointed spheres by shifts of the sphere spectrum and take colimits in spectra.

Definition 10.2.7 (Stable spherical fibration). Let \(\Pic (\Sp ) \subseteq \Sp ^{\simeq }\) denote the full subanima spanned by shifts \(\S [n]\) of the sphere spectrum. For an anima \(A\), we define a stable spherical fibration on \(A\) to be a functor \(\xi \colon A \to \Sp \) which lands in \(\Pic (\Sp )\). For an integer \(n \in \Z \) we say that \(\xi \) has rank \(n\) if it lands in the full subanima of \(\Pic (\Sp )\) spanned by \(\S [n]\). In general, the rank may vary between connected components of \(A\).

The shifts of the sphere spectrum are precisely the invertible spectra: those spectra \(X\) for which there is a spectrum \(X^{-1}\) and an isomorphism \(X \otimes X^{-1} \cong \S \); see Rognes (2008), Example 3.3.2 and also Chapterexercise 11.7. Thus this definition of \(\Pic (\Sp )\) agrees with the general definition of the Picard anima in Definition 11.1.4 below. Tensor product restricts to a symmetric monoidal structure on \(\Pic (\Sp )\), with unit \(\S \) and inverse to \(\S [n]\) given by \(\S [-n]\). In particular, \(\Pic (\Sp )\) is a commutative group in animae.

If \(\xi \colon A \to \An _*\) is a rank \(n\) spherical fibration, then \(\Sigma ^{\infty }\xi \) is a rank \(n\) stable spherical fibration. In particular, a rank \(n\) vector bundle \(E\to X\) determines the stable spherical fibration \(\Sigma ^{\infty }\xi _E\) on \(\Pi _{\infty }(X)\).

Definition 10.2.8 (Thom spectrum). Given a stable spherical fibration \(\xi \colon A \to \Sp \), we define its Thom spectrum as its colimit: \[ \th (\xi ) \quad := \quad \colim (\xi \colon A \to \Sp ). \]

For a vector bundle \(E \to X\), we write \(\th (E) := \th (\Sigma ^{\infty }\xi _E)\) for its Thom spectrum. If \(X\) is paracompact Hausdorff and has the homotopy type of a cell complex, we obtain \[ \th (E) \quad \cong \quad \Sigma ^{\infty } \Th (\xi _E) \quad \cong \quad \Sigma ^{\infty }\Pi _{\infty } \Th (E), \] where the first isomorphism follows since \(\Sigma ^{\infty }\) preserves colimits, and the second is Proposition 10.2.6. Throughout, \(\Th \) denotes a Thom space or Thom anima and \(\th \) a Thom spectrum.

Example 10.2.9 (Constant spherical fibrations). Let \(A\) be an anima and let \(n\in \Z \). The Thom spectrum of the constant functor \(\xi \colon A\to \Sp \) with value \(\S [n]\) is \[ \th (\xi )\simeq \S [A][n]. \] Indeed, shifts commute with colimits, and the colimit of the constant functor with value \(\S \) is \(\S [A]\). For \(n=0\), this is the suspension spectrum of \(A\) with a disjoint basepoint. Allowing negative \(n\) gives its desuspensions.

Construction 10.2.10 (Multiplicative structures on Thom spectra). Let \(A\) be a small commutative monoid in animae, regarded as a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category, and let \(\xi \colon A\to \Pic (\Sp )\subseteq \Sp \) be a symmetric monoidal functor. We equip the Thom spectrum \(\th (\xi )\) with a canonical commutative ring spectrum structure, following Antolín-Camarena and Barthel (2019). The multiplication is induced by addition in \(A\): \[ \begin {aligned} \th (\xi )\otimes \th (\xi ) &\simeq \colim _{(a,b)\in A\times A}\bigl (\xi (a)\otimes \xi (b)\bigr )\\ &\simeq \colim _{(a,b)\in A\times A}\xi (a+b) \longrightarrow \colim _{a\in A}\xi (a)=\th (\xi ). \end {aligned} \] The unit is \(\S \simeq \xi (0)\to \th (\xi )\).

To obtain the coherent commutative algebra structure, we use the Day convolution monoidal structure on \(\Fun (A,\Sp )\) from Corollary 16.2.8. The universal property of Day convolution from Lemma 16.1.4 identifies the underlying lax symmetric monoidal functor of \(\xi \) with a commutative algebra in \(\Fun (A,\Sp )\). The colimit functor \(\colim _A\colon \Fun (A,\Sp )\to \Sp \) is pointwise left Kan extension along the unique symmetric monoidal functor \(A\to *\), so it is symmetric monoidal by Lemma 16.2.11; here \(\Fun (*,\Sp )\) is identified with \(\Sp \) equipped with its usual tensor product. It therefore carries \(\xi \) to a commutative algebra in spectra whose underlying spectrum is \(\colim _A\xi =\th (\xi )\).

Remark 10.2.11. More generally, an \(\Ee _n\)-map \(A\to \Pic (\Sp )\) gives its Thom spectrum an \(\Ee _n\)-algebra structure; see Antolín-Camarena and Barthel (2019), Corollary 3.2. The operadic Day convolution used for such extensions is discussed in Remark 16.2.5.

The symmetric monoidal stable spherical fibrations used below arise from the stable J-homomorphism, which we discuss next. We apply the construction to bordism spectra in Construction 10.2.26.

10.2.3 The J-homomorphism

One-point compactification sends direct sums of vector spaces to smash products of pointed spheres. We will use this compatibility to construct the J-homomorphism: after passing to suspension spectra, it gives a symmetric monoidal functor from vector spaces to invertible spectra, which extends to virtual vector spaces by group completion.

For a topological space \(X\), let \(\Vect _{\R }^{\disc }(X)\) denote the 1-category of finite-rank real vector bundles over \(X\) and morphisms of vector bundles. As in Chapter 9, we consider the moduli anima of real vector bundles over \(X\), \[ \Vect _{\R }(X)^{\simeq } \quad := \quad \colim _{[q]\in \simp \catop } \Vect _{\R }^{\disc }(X\times \abs {\Delta ^q})^{\simeq }. \] By Corollary 19.7.10, this defines a functor \(\Top \catop \to \CRig (\An )\) whose underlying anima is computed by the same colimit; here we only use the underlying commutative monoids. When \(X=\pt \), we refer to \(\Vect _{\R }(\pt )^{\simeq }\) as the moduli anima of finite-dimensional real vector spaces.

The following proposition expresses one-point compactification as a symmetric monoidal functor. Its proof occupies Section 10.5.

Proposition 10.2.12 (One-point compactification). For every topological space \(X\), fiberwise one-point compactification defines a symmetric monoidal functor \[ S_X^{(-)}\colon \Vect _{\R }(X)^{\simeq } \longrightarrow (\An _{/\Pi _{\infty }(X)})_* \simeq \Fun (\Pi _{\infty }(X),\An _*). \] Here the source has direct sum and the target has pointwise smash product under the displayed equivalence. These functors are natural in \(X\) as symmetric monoidal functors. For \(X=\pt \), the source identifies with \(\bigsqcup _{n\geq 0}\bB O(n)\), with addition induced by block sum. On the rank \(n\) component, \(S_{\pt }^{(-)}\) is classified by the standard pointed action of \(O(n)\) on \(S^{\R ^n}\).

We also write \(S^{(-)}\) for the component \(S_{\pt }^{(-)}\) when the base is a point.

Definition 10.2.13. Write \(O:=\colim _n O(n)\) for the stable orthogonal group and \(\bB O\) for its classifying anima. We define \[ \BOP \; := \; \big (\Vect _{\R }(\pt )^{\simeq }\big )^{\grp } \qin \CGrp (\An ) \] to be the group completion of the commutative monoid of finite-dimensional real vector spaces. The telescope calculation of Corollary 9.4.3, with real vector spaces in place of complex ones, identifies its underlying anima as \[ \BOP \; \simeq \; \Z \times \bB O. \] The only additional point in applying Theorem 5.5.2 is that \(\pi _1(\bB O) \cong \pi _0(O) \cong C_2\) is abelian. The virtual-rank-zero component has the presentation \(\bB O\cong \colim _n\bB O(n),\) where the transition maps add a trivial line. Under this presentation, the map from the \(n\)-th stage to the virtual-rank-zero component sends \(V\) to \(V-\R ^n\).

Construction 10.2.14 (Stable J-homomorphism). Applying reduced suspension spectra to Proposition 10.2.12 gives a symmetric monoidal functor \[ \Vect _{\R }(\pt )^{\simeq } \xrightarrow {\,S^{(-)}\,} \An _* \xrightarrow {\Sigma ^\infty } \Sp . \] Its image lies in \(\Pic (\Sp )\), since \(\Sigma ^\infty S^V\cong \S [\dim (V)]\) is invertible. Since tensor product of spectra turns \(\Pic (\Sp )\) into a commutative group in animae, the resulting functor to \(\Pic (\Sp )\) extends uniquely over group completion, giving a symmetric monoidal functor \[ J\colon \BOP \longrightarrow \Pic (\Sp ). \] We call \(J\) the stable J-homomorphism. We use the same notation for its restriction to any component of \(\BOP \), in particular for \[ J\colon \bB O \simeq \{0\}\times \bB O \longrightarrow \Pic (\Sp ). \] Forgetting the complex structure defines a symmetric monoidal functor from complex to real vector bundles for direct sum, compatible with pullback. Applying the simplicial resolutions above therefore gives a map of commutative monoids \(\Vect (\pt )^{\simeq }\to \Vect _{\R }(\pt )^{\simeq }\). Group completion gives a map of commutative groups \(\BUP \to \BOP \), which doubles virtual rank. The complex analogue of \(J\) is the composite \(\BUP \to \BOP \xrightarrow {J}\Pic (\Sp )\).

Remark 10.2.15 (Relation to the classical J-homomorphism). The connected component of \(\S \) in \(\Pic (\Sp )\) is isomorphic to \(\bB \GL _1(\S )\), where \(\GL _1(\S ) := \Aut _{\Sp }(\S )\) is the automorphism group of the sphere spectrum. The restriction of \(J\) to the virtual-rank-zero component lands in this connected component. Passing to loops gives a group homomorphism \(\Pi _{\infty }(O)\to \GL _1(\S )\) in \(\An \), obtained by one-point compactifying orthogonal transformations and stabilizing. Its effect on positive homotopy groups is the classical stable J-homomorphism \(\pi _n(O)\to \pi _n(\S )\); see Ravenel (1986), Definition 1.1.12.

10.2.4 Virtual vector bundles

Since \(J\) is defined on all of \(\BOP \), it assigns stable spherical fibrations, and hence Thom spectra, to virtual vector bundles of arbitrary rank.

Definition 10.2.16 (Virtual vector bundle). Let \(A\) be an anima. A virtual vector bundle on \(A\) is a map of animae \[ V\colon A \longrightarrow \BOP . \] Its rank is the composite \(\rk (V)\colon A \to \Z \) with the projection to \(\Z \), and we say that \(V\) has rank \(d \in \Z \) if this composite is constant with value \(d\). A virtual vector bundle on a topological space \(X\) means one on \(\Pi _{\infty }(X)\).

Since \(\BOP \) is a commutative group in animae, so is the anima \(\Hom _{\An }(A,\BOP )\) of virtual vector bundles on \(A\). We write \(V + W\) for the sum and \(-V\) for the inverse; ranks add, and \(\rk (-V) = -\rk (V)\).

Example 10.2.17 (The class of a vector bundle). Let \(X\) be a paracompact Hausdorff space and let \(E \to X\) be a rank \(n\) vector bundle, classified by a map \(f\colon X \to \Gr _n(\R ^{\infty })\). Combining the identification \(\Pi _{\infty }(\Gr _n(\R ^{\infty })) \iso \bB O(n)\) of Proposition 9.2.11 with the group-completion map \(\Vect _{\R }(\pt )^{\simeq }\to \BOP \), we obtain a virtual vector bundle \[ [E]\colon \Pi _{\infty }(X) \xrightarrow {\Pi _{\infty }(f)} \bB O(n) \subseteq \Vect _{\R }(\pt )^{\simeq } \longrightarrow \BOP \] of rank \(n\). For the trivial bundle \(\ul {\R ^N}\) this is the constant map with value \(\R ^N\).

This construction is compatible with direct sums. Indeed, under the identifications \(\Pi _{\infty }(\Gr _n(\R ^{\infty }))\cong \bB O(n)\), the Whitney-sum map corresponds to the map induced by the block-sum homomorphism \(O(n)\times O(m)\to O(n+m)\). To see this, observe that the orthonormal frame bundle of \(E\oplus E'\) is obtained from the product over \(X\) of the orthonormal frame bundles of \(E\) and \(E'\) by extension of structure group along this homomorphism. Since the addition on \(\Vect _{\R }(\pt )^{\simeq }\) is induced by block sum, it follows that \[ [E\oplus E']\cong [E]+[E']. \]

Construction 10.2.18 (Thom spectrum of a virtual vector bundle). Let \(V\) be a virtual vector bundle on an anima \(A\). Composing with the J-homomorphism gives a stable spherical fibration \[ \xi _V \quad := \quad J \circ V\colon A \longrightarrow \Pic (\Sp ). \] If \(V\) has rank \(d\), then so does \(\xi _V\), since \(J\) carries the class of \(\R \) to \(\S [1]\). We define the Thom spectrum of \(V\) as \[ \th (V) \quad := \quad \th (\xi _V) \quad = \quad \colim \big (J \circ V\colon A \to \Sp \big ). \]

Remark 10.2.19. As \(J\) is a homomorphism of commutative groups in animae, the assignment \(V \mapsto \xi _V\) carries sums to pointwise tensor products and inverses to pointwise inverses: \[ \xi _{V+W} \cong \xi _V \otimes \xi _W \qquad \text {and}\qquad \xi _{-V} \cong \xi _V^{-1}. \] For bundles over the same base, this does not in general give an isomorphism \(\th (V+W)\cong \th (V)\otimes \th (W)\): the tensor product on the right is computed by a colimit over \(A\times A\). External sums do satisfy this formula; see Chapterexercise 10.2.

We next show that this construction extends our previous definition of Thom spectra for vector bundles. We first identify the associated families of pointed spheres. As in Construction 10.1.1, a pointed action of a topological group \(G\) on a topological space \(Y\), together with a principal \(G\)-bundle \(P\to X\), gives an associated pointed bundle \(P\times _GY\to X\). Passing to underlying animae and applying pointed straightening produces a functor \(\Pi _{\infty }(X)\to \An _*\). For a universal principal bundle, we refer to the resulting functor \(\bB G\to \An _*\) as the functor classified by the pointed \(G\)-action on \(Y\).

Construction 10.2.20 (One-point compactification of vector spaces). By Proposition 10.2.12, the rank \(n\) component of \(\Vect _{\R }(\pt )^{\simeq }\) is isomorphic to \(\bB O(n)\). We denote the restriction of one-point compactification to this component by \[ J_n^{\mathrm {un}}\colon \bB O(n) \longrightarrow \An _*. \] It is the functor classified by the standard pointed action of \(O(n)\) on \(S^{\R ^n}\). Consequently, the restriction of \(J\) along the rank \(n\) component \(\bB O(n)\to \BOP \) is \(\Sigma ^\infty J_n^{\mathrm {un}}\). Its restriction along the stable map \[ \bB O(n)\longrightarrow \{0\}\times \bB O\subseteq \BOP , \qquad V\longmapsto V-\R ^n, \] is \(\Sigma ^\infty J_n^{\mathrm {un}}[-n]\).

Lemma 10.2.21. Let \(X\) be a paracompact Hausdorff space and let \(f\colon X \to \Gr _n(\R ^{\infty })\) be a continuous map. Let \(p\colon E \to X\) be the rank \(n\) vector bundle classified by \(f\). Then the map \(\xi _E\colon \Pi _{\infty }(X) \to \An _*\) is given by the composite \[ \Pi _{\infty }(X) \xrightarrow {\Pi _{\infty }(f)} \Pi _{\infty }(\Gr _n(\R ^{\infty })) \iso \bB O(n) \xrightarrow {J_n^{\mathrm {un}}} \An _*, \] where the middle isomorphism is the one from Proposition 9.2.11.

Proof. Since \(E\cong f^*\gamma _n\), naturality in Proposition 10.2.12 gives \(\xi _E\cong \xi _{\gamma _n}\circ \Pi _\infty (f)\). The universal bundle \(\gamma _n\) is associated to the universal principal \(O(n)\)-bundle \(V_n(\R ^\infty )\to \Gr _n(\R ^\infty )\), so its fiberwise one-point compactification is the associated pointed sphere bundle. Under \(\Pi _\infty (\Gr _n(\R ^\infty ))\cong \bB O(n)\), its straightening is therefore \(J_n^{\mathrm {un}}\), by the final assertion of that proposition. □

Applying suspension spectra and taking colimits now identifies the two Thom spectra. Subtracting a trivial bundle gives a desuspension, a fact we will use in the proof of Atiyah duality in Section 11.3.

Lemma 10.2.22. Let \(X\) be a paracompact Hausdorff space and let \(E \to X\) be a rank \(n\) vector bundle. Then there is an isomorphism \(\xi _{[E]} \cong \Sigma ^{\infty }(\xi _E)\) of stable spherical fibrations, and hence an isomorphism \(\th ([E]) \cong \th (E)\). More generally, for every \(N \geq 0\) we have \(\xi _{[E]-[\ul {\R ^N}]} \cong \Sigma ^{\infty }(\xi _E)[-N]\) and therefore \[ \th \big ([E]-[\ul {\R ^N}]\big ) \quad \cong \quad \th (E)[-N]. \] If \(X\) also has the homotopy type of a cell complex, this is isomorphic to \(\Sigma ^{\infty -N}\Pi _{\infty }\Th (E)\).

Proof. By Construction 10.2.20, the restriction of \(J\) to the rank \(n\) component is \(\Sigma ^{\infty }J_n^{\mathrm {un}}\). The first isomorphism is therefore given by Lemma 10.2.21, and passing to colimits gives \(\th ([E]) \cong \Sigma ^{\infty }\Th (\xi _E) \cong \th (E)\), since \(\Sigma ^{\infty }\) preserves colimits.

The virtual vector bundle \([\ul {\R ^N}]\) is constant with value \(\R ^N\), so \(\xi _{[\ul {\R ^N}]}\) is constant with value \(\Sigma ^{\infty }S^{\R ^N} \cong \S [N]\). By Remark 10.2.19 we thus obtain \[ \xi _{[E]-[\ul {\R ^N}]} \cong \Sigma ^{\infty }(\xi _E) \otimes \S [-N] \cong \Sigma ^{\infty }(\xi _E)[-N]. \] Passing to colimits gives the asserted shift of Thom spectra. Under the additional cell-complex hypothesis, Proposition 10.2.6 identifies \(\th (E)\) with \(\Sigma ^{\infty }\Pi _{\infty }\Th (E)\), giving the final assertion. □

10.2.5 Bordism spectra

We now apply the Thom-spectrum construction to bordism spectra. We start with \(\MO \), which comes from the virtual-rank-zero component of \(\BOP \), and relate it to the prespectrum \(\MO ^{\pre }\) constructed in Section 10.1.

Definition 10.2.23 (Unoriented bordism spectrum). We define the unoriented bordism spectrum \(\MO \) as the Thom spectrum of the restriction of \(J\) to the virtual-rank-zero component: \[ \MO \quad := \quad \th (J\vert _{\bB O}) \quad = \quad \colim \big (J\colon \bB O\to \Sp \big ). \]

Theorem 10.2.24. The spectrum associated to the prespectrum \(\MO ^{\pre }\) of Construction 10.1.11 via Proposition 4.3.29 is \(\MO \): \[ (\MO ^{\pre })^{\mathrm {sp}} \cong \MO . \]

Proof. We first identify the levels and structure maps of the classical prespectrum with the unstable Thom construction. We then pass to the colimit presentation of the virtual-rank-zero component of \(\BOP \).

Step 1: Consider the functor \(\xi _{\gamma _k} \colon \Pi _{\infty }(\Gr _k(\R ^{\infty })) \to \An _*\) associated to \(\gamma _k\). Applying Lemma 10.2.21 to the identity of \(\Gr _k(\R ^\infty )\) identifies \(\xi _{\gamma _k}\) with \(J_k^{\mathrm {un}}\). In particular, Proposition 10.2.6 provides an isomorphism \[ \MO ^{\pre }_k = \Pi _{\infty }(\Th (\gamma _k)) \cong \Th (J_k^{\mathrm {un}}) \] of pointed animae. Write \(i_k\) for the inclusion of Grassmannians used in Construction 10.1.11. The identification \(i_k^*\gamma _{k+1}\cong \ul {\R }\oplus \gamma _k\) gives, by the compatibility of one-point compactification with direct sums, an isomorphism \[ J_{k+1}^{\mathrm {un}}\vert _{\bB O(k)} \cong \Sigma J_k^{\mathrm {un}}. \] The isomorphisms of Proposition 10.2.6 are natural for pullback maps of bundles. Under fiberwise one-point compactification, adjoining a trivial line gives fiberwise suspension; taking colimits gives suspension of the Thom anima. Hence, under the direct-sum identifications of Construction 10.1.11, the adjoints \(\Sigma \MO ^{\pre }_k\to \MO ^{\pre }_{k+1}\) of the structure maps correspond to \[ \Sigma \Th (J_k^{\mathrm {un}}) \cong \Th \big (J_{k+1}^{\mathrm {un}}\vert _{\bB O(k)}\big ) \longrightarrow \Th (J_{k+1}^{\mathrm {un}}). \]

Step 2: In the presentation \(\bB O \simeq \colim _{k \in \N } \bB O(k)\) of Definition 10.2.13, the map from the \(k\)-th stage sends \(V\) to \(V-\R ^k\). The restriction of \(J\) to this stage is therefore \(\Sigma ^\infty J_k^{\mathrm {un}}[-k]\) by Construction 10.2.20. Applying Theorem 21.2.11 gives \[ \MO \cong \colim _k \Sigma ^{\infty }\Th (J_k^{\mathrm {un}})[-k]. \] By Step 1, the transition maps \(\Sigma ^{\infty }\Th (J_k^{\mathrm {un}})[-k] \to \Sigma ^{\infty }\Th (J_{k+1}^{\mathrm {un}})[-(k+1)]\) are induced by the structure maps of \(\MO ^{\pre }\) after applying suspension spectra and the indicated shifts. Thus Proposition 4.3.29 identifies this colimit with \((\MO ^{\pre })^{\mathrm {sp}}\). □

More generally, a map \(B\to \BOP \) defines a Thom spectrum.

Definition 10.2.25 (The Thom spectrum \(MB\)). Let \(\phi \colon B \to \BOP \) be a map of animae. We define its associated Thom spectrum \(MB\) by \[ MB := \colim \big (B\xrightarrow {\phi }\BOP \xrightarrow {J}\Sp \big ). \] Equivalently, \(\phi \) is a virtual vector bundle on \(B\), and \(MB=\th (\phi )\) in the notation of Construction 10.2.18. The notation suppresses \(\phi \), which will always be clear from the context. We allow \(B\) to be an arbitrary anima. For the Pontryagin–Thom theorem of Section 10.3, we will specialize to maps which land in the virtual-rank-zero component \(\bB O\subseteq \BOP \).

Construction 10.2.26 (Multiplicative structures on bordism spectra). Let \(B\) be a commutative monoid in animae and let \(\phi \colon B\longrightarrow \BOP \) be a morphism of commutative monoids. Since the J-homomorphism is symmetric monoidal, so is the composite \[ B\xrightarrow {\phi }\BOP \xrightarrow {J}\Pic (\Sp )\subseteq \Sp . \] Consequently, Construction 10.2.10 equips \(MB\) with a canonical commutative ring spectrum structure.

Example 10.2.27 (Classical bordism spectra). A continuous group homomorphism \(G \to O\) induces a map \(\bB G \to \bB O\), and we write \(MG\) for the resulting Thom spectrum. Taking \(B = \bB O\) with \(\phi \) the inclusion \(\bB O\hookrightarrow \BOP \) recovers \(\MO \). The composite \[ \bB U\longrightarrow \bB O\hookrightarrow \BOP , \] where the first map is induced by realification, defines the complex bordism spectrum \(\MU \). Further classical examples are \[ \MSO , \quad \mathrm {MSU}, \quad \mathrm {MSp}, \quad \text {and}\quad \mathrm {MSpin}. \] For \(\MO \) and \(\MU \), the defining maps to \(\BOP \) are restrictions of morphisms of commutative groups constructed above, so Construction 10.2.26 equips these Thom spectra with commutative ring structures. The other classical Thom spectra in the display also admit commutative ring structures, but their construction requires compatible infinite-loop refinements of the maps \(\bB G\to \bB O\), which we do not develop here.

Example 10.2.28 (Periodic bordism spectra). The full group completions give the periodic unoriented and complex bordism spectra \[ \MOP :=\th \big (J\colon \BOP \to \Sp \big ) \quad \qquadtext {and}\quad \MUP :=\th \big (\BUP \longrightarrow \BOP \xrightarrow {J}\Sp \big ), \] respectively. Both are commutative ring spectra by Construction 10.2.26. Restricting these defining diagrams to their virtual-rank-zero components recovers \(\MO \) and \(\MU \). Translation by \(r[\R ]\) and \(r[\C ]\) identifies the rank-zero components with the rank \(r\) components, while shifting the associated stable spherical fibrations by \(r\) and \(2r\), respectively. Taking colimits componentwise then gives isomorphisms of underlying spectra \[ \MOP \simeq \bigoplus _{r\in \Z }\MO [r] \quad \qquadtext {and}\quad \MUP \simeq \bigoplus _{r\in \Z }\MU [2r]. \] Reindexing these sums gives \[ \MOP \cong \MOP [1] \quad \qquadtext {and}\quad \MUP \cong \MUP [2], \] which explains the terminology.
