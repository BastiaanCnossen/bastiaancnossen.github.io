Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Part I · Chapter 10 · Section 10.5

Coherence for one-point compactification (online only)

← Previous chapterChapter 9 → Next chapterChapter 11 ← Previous sectionSection 10.4 → Next sectionSection 11.1

We now prove Proposition 10.2.12. We first construct fiberwise one-point compactification on the ordinary groupoids of vector bundles, compatibly with direct sums and pullback. We then pass to underlying animae, apply the construction over \(X\times \abs {\Delta ^q}\), and take the colimit defining the moduli anima. The passage to animae uses the pointed-operad formalism of Part II. This section may be skipped on a first reading.

10.5.1 Fiberwise compactification

Fix a topological space \(X\). Let \(\mathrm {Bun}(X)\) be the 1-category of locally trivial fiber bundles over \(X\) and continuous maps over \(X\). Its terminal object is \(X\to X\), and its finite products are fiber products over \(X\). Let \(\mathrm {Bun}_{*,\mathrm {c}}(X)\) be the 1-category of bundles with compact Hausdorff pointed fibers, locally trivial as pointed bundles, and pointed bundle maps. That is, the local trivializations carry the distinguished section to the basepoint of the fiber.

For \(K,L\in \mathrm {Bun}_{*,\mathrm {c}}(X)\) with sections \(s_K\) and \(s_L\), define the fiberwise wedge \[ K\vee _XL := \bigl (K\times _Xs_L(X)\bigr )\cup \bigl (s_K(X)\times _XL\bigr ) \subseteq K\times _XL. \] The fiberwise smash product is the pushout \[ K\wedge _XL:=(K\times _XL)\amalg _{K\vee _XL}X \] in topological spaces over \(X\), pointed by the last term. In each fiber this collapses the wedge to a point. Over a common pointed trivializing open subset \(U\subseteq X\), the quotient map is \[ \id _U\times q\colon U\times (K_0\times L_0)\longrightarrow U\times (K_0\wedge L_0). \] Indeed, \(q\) is a closed surjection between compact Hausdorff spaces, hence perfect; its product with \(U\) is again a quotient map. It follows that \(K\wedge _XL\) is locally trivial with compact Hausdorff pointed fiber \(K_0\wedge L_0\). The same argument applies to iterated smash products. Their universal properties give a symmetric monoidal structure on \(\mathrm {Bun}_{*,\mathrm {c}}(X)\) with unit \(X\times S^0\). These constructions commute with pullback, as is seen in the same local trivializations.

Lemma 10.5.1 (Fiberwise one-point compactification). Fiberwise one-point compactification defines a symmetric monoidal functor \[ \Vect _{\R }^{\disc }(X)^{\simeq } \longrightarrow \mathrm {Bun}_{*,\mathrm {c}}(X)^{\simeq }, \qquad E\longmapsto S^E, \] natural in \(X\) under pullback.

Proof. A linear isomorphism extends uniquely to a pointed homeomorphism of one-point compactifications. The local description of Construction 10.1.1 shows that this extension is continuous in families. It therefore gives a functor on groupoids of vector bundles, commuting with pullback.

The homeomorphisms \(S^{V\oplus W}\cong S^V\wedge S^W\) for vector spaces commute with linear isomorphisms. Applying them in bundle charts gives natural isomorphisms \[ S^{E\oplus F}\cong S^E\wedge _XS^F, \qquad S^{0_X}\cong X\times S^0, \] where \(0_X\) denotes the zero vector bundle on \(X\). To check the associativity, unit and symmetry diagrams, restrict to the complements of the infinity sections. There they are the corresponding diagrams for direct sums of vector bundles. All maps also carry the infinity section to the infinity section, so the diagrams commute everywhere. □

10.5.2 Passage to animae

We use the following consequence of the pointed-operad construction. A finite-product-preserving functor \(F\colon C\to D\) induces a morphism \[ (\Mm _{(C,\times )})_*\longrightarrow (\Mm _{(D,\times )})_* \] by Proposition 15.3.6, Theorem 18.4.12. Its functor on colors sends a pointed object \((*\to K)\) to \((*\to F(K))\). If \(D\) has finite colimits preserved separately by cartesian products, the target is the multimorphism operad of \((D_*,\wedge )\) by Lemma 18.4.10. This construction is functorial in \(F\): the cartesian symmetric monoidal refinement is functorial by the equivalence of Proposition 15.3.6, and \((-)_*\) is a functor. It supplies a lax symmetric monoidal functor whenever the source pointed operad is represented by a symmetric monoidal category.

For \(C=\mathrm {Bun}(X)\), we only need its full pointed suboperad on \(\mathrm {Bun}_{*,\mathrm {c}}(X)\). An operation from \(K_1,\ldots ,K_r\) to \(L\) is a bundle map \[ K_1\times _X\cdots \times _XK_r\longrightarrow L \] which sends a tuple with any coordinate in the distinguished section to the distinguished section of \(L\). Such maps factor uniquely through the fiberwise smash product. Nullary operations are sections of \(L\), equivalently pointed maps \(X\times S^0\to L\). Thus this full suboperad is represented by \((\mathrm {Bun}_{*,\mathrm {c}}(X),\wedge _X)\).

Lemma 10.5.2 (Realization of sphere bundles). Taking underlying animae of fiberwise one-point compactifications defines a natural transformation \[ \xi \colon \Vect _{\R }^{\disc }(-)^{\simeq } \Longrightarrow \Fun (\Pi _\infty (-),\An _*) \] of functors \(\Top \catop \to \CMon (\Cat _\infty )\). Its component at \(X\) sends \(E\) to the family \(\xi _E\), with fiber \(\Pi _\infty (S^{E_x})\) at \(x\).

Proof. Taking underlying animae of total spaces and projections gives \[ \Pi _{\infty ,X}\colon \mathrm {Bun}(X)\longrightarrow \An _{/\Pi _\infty (X)}. \] This functor preserves finite products: the terminal bundle maps to the terminal object, and for bundles \(K,L\) the canonical map \[ \Pi _\infty (K\times _XL) \longrightarrow \Pi _\infty (K)\times _{\Pi _\infty (X)}\Pi _\infty (L) \] is an isomorphism. Indeed, bundle projections are Serre fibrations by Theorem 2.3.13, so Proposition 2.3.19 applies.

These functors are natural under pullback. More precisely, the categories \(\mathrm {Bun}(X)\) form a cartesian fibration over \(\Top \). Taking underlying animae gives a functor from this fibration to the pullback along \(\Pi _\infty \) of the codomain fibration \(\Ar (\An )\to \An \). It preserves cartesian morphisms, since a pullback square along a bundle projection becomes a pullback square of animae. Cartesian straightening therefore gives a natural transformation of finite-product-preserving functors.

Pointing now gives lax symmetric monoidal functors on \(\mathrm {Bun}_{*,\mathrm {c}}(X)\), naturally in \(X\). Under straightening, the target identifies with \[ (\An _{/\Pi _\infty (X)})_* \simeq \Fun (\Pi _\infty (X),\An _*). \] The unpointed straightening equivalence preserves finite products. Applying the pointed-operad construction and Lemma 18.4.10 therefore identifies the smash products on the two sides. In the functor category, finite products and finite colimits are computed pointwise, so Lemma 16.3.4 identifies its smash product with pointwise smash product. Composing with Lemma 10.5.1 gives natural lax symmetric monoidal functors \(\xi _X\).

It remains to check that their structure maps are isomorphisms. This can be checked at each point \(x\in X\). Pullback to \(x\) identifies the fibers of the realized bundles with the underlying animae of their topological fibers, again because bundle projections are Serre fibrations. The binary structure map at \(x\) is consequently \[ \Pi _\infty (S^{E_x})\wedge \Pi _\infty (S^{F_x}) \longrightarrow \Pi _\infty (S^{E_x}\wedge S^{F_x}) \cong \Pi _\infty (S^{E_x\oplus F_x}). \] Choose coordinates identifying the two compactifications with \(S^m\) and \(S^n\). The inclusion \[ (S^m\times \{\infty \})\cup (\{\infty \}\times S^n) \hookrightarrow S^m\times S^n \] is a relative cell complex, as are the inclusions used to form the wedge. Thus Corollary 2.3.20, Proposition 2.3.9 identify the displayed structure map with an isomorphism. The unit structure map is \(S^0\cong \Pi _\infty (S^0)\). Hence the functors \(\xi _X\) are symmetric monoidal, and their naturality gives the asserted transformation. □

10.5.3 Passage to the moduli anima

We can now pass from ordinary bundle groupoids to the moduli anima. The projections \(X\times \abs {\Delta ^q}\to X\) induce isomorphisms on underlying animae. Their naturality in both \(X\) and \([q]\) lets us replace the target by a constant simplicial diagram.

Proof of Proposition 10.2.12. Put \[ D(X):=\Fun (\Pi _\infty (X),\An _*)^\simeq , \] regarded as a commutative monoid in animae under pointwise smash product. Since the source of \(\xi _X\) is a groupoid, Lemma 10.5.2 gives a natural map \[ \Vect _{\R }^{\disc }(X)^\simeq \longrightarrow D(X) \qquad \text {in }\CMon (\An ). \] Applying it over \(X\times \abs {\Delta ^q}\) gives a map of simplicial diagrams \[ \Vect _{\R }^{\disc }(X\times \abs {\Delta ^\bullet })^\simeq \longrightarrow D(X\times \abs {\Delta ^\bullet }). \] On the other hand, pullback along the projections gives \[ \const _{D(X)}\longrightarrow D(X\times \abs {\Delta ^\bullet }). \] Every component of this map is an isomorphism, since \(X\times \abs {\Delta ^q}\to X\) is a homotopy equivalence. Inverting it and composing with the preceding map gives a cocone in \(\CMon (\An )\). By Corollary 19.7.10, its colimit induces \[ S_X^{(-)}\colon \Vect _{\R }(X)^\simeq \longrightarrow D(X) \subseteq \Fun (\Pi _\infty (X),\An _*). \] In degree zero, this sends \(E\) to \(\xi _E\). Both maps of simplicial diagrams are natural in \(X\): they are transformations of functors on \(\Top \catop \times \simp \catop \). Their composite and its colimit are therefore natural in \(X\) as well.

Finally, the proof of Proposition 9.4.1, with \(\R \) in place of \(\C \), identifies the rank \(n\) component of \(\Vect _{\R }(\pt )^\simeq \) with \(\bB \GL _n(\R )\simeq \bB O(n)\), compatibly with block sums. In degree \(q\), an automorphism of the trivial bundle is a continuous map \(g\colon \abs {\Delta ^q}\to \GL _n(\R )\). Fiberwise compactification sends it to \[ (t,v)\longmapsto (t,g(t)v), \qquad (t,\infty )\longmapsto (t,\infty ). \] These formulas commute with the simplicial operators and composition. They identify the restriction to \(\bB \GL _n(\R )\) with the functor obtained by realizing the singular simplicial action on \(S^{\R ^n}\). Restricting along \(O(n)\to \GL _n(\R )\) gives the corresponding functor for the orthogonal action.

To relate this description to the associated-bundle definition, let \(P=V_n(\R ^\infty )\) and \(B=\Gr _n(\R ^\infty )\). The principal \(O(n)\)-bundle \(P\to B\) has contractible total space, and \[ S^{\gamma _n}\cong P\times _{O(n)}S^{\R ^n}. \] Over \(P\) this pointed bundle is trivial, and under \(P\times _B P\cong P\times O(n)\) its transition maps are the standard orthogonal action. By Lemma 9.2.10, Proposition 9.2.11, taking underlying animae identifies the entire Čech nerve with the bar construction for \(O(n)\). The transition maps therefore give the same action as the simplicial formulas above, including their compatibility with composition. Descent for animae (Theorem 5.2.5) identifies the resulting pointed object over \(\bB O(n)\) with the underlying pointed object of \(S^{\gamma _n}\to B\). Its straightening is, by definition, the functor classified by the pointed orthogonal action. This proves the final assertion of the proposition. □

Exercises

Exercise 10.1 (Homotopy invariance of Thom spaces). Let \(f\colon X'\to X\) be a homotopy equivalence between paracompact Hausdorff spaces and let \(E\to X\) be a real vector bundle. Prove part (2) of Lemma 10.1.8: the induced map \(\Th (f^*E)\to \Th (E)\) is a pointed homotopy equivalence. Hint: Use homotopy invariance of vector bundles over cylinders, and observe that it suffices for both composites of the induced Thom maps to be pointed homotopic to homeomorphisms.

Exercise 10.2 (External sums and Thom spectra). Let \(V\colon A\to \BOP \) and \(W\colon B\to \BOP \) be virtual vector bundles. Define their external sum as the composite \[ V\boxplus W\colon A\times B\xrightarrow {V\times W}\BOP \times \BOP \xrightarrow {+}\BOP . \] Show that there is a natural isomorphism \[ \th (V\boxplus W)\cong \th (V)\otimes \th (W). \]

Exercise 10.3 (The Möbius bundle). Let \(L\to S^1\) be the Möbius real line bundle.

(1)

Identify \(\Th (L)\) with \(\R \!\mathrm {P}^2\) as a pointed space.

(2)

Show that \(L\) is not \(H\Z \)-orientable.

(3)

Construct its canonical \(H\F _2\)-orientation. Writing \(a\in H^1(\R \!\mathrm {P}^2;\F _2)\) for the standard generator, show that the Thom class is \(a\) and that the Thom isomorphism sends the generators of \(H^0(S^1;\F _2)\) and \(H^1(S^1;\F _2)\) to \(a\) and \(a^2\), respectively.

Exercise 10.4 (Collapse of a framed point). Embed a point in \(\R ^n\) and equip its normal bundle with the standard framing. Write down the Pontryagin–Thom collapse map \[ S^n\longrightarrow \Th (\R ^n)\cong S^n. \] Show that, with the standard choices of orientation, this map has degree \(1\). Explain how reversing one vector in the framing changes the degree.

Exercise 10.5 (Low-dimensional unoriented bordism). Compute \(\Omega _0^O(*)\) and \(\Omega _1^O(*)\). Compare your answer with \(\pi _0(\MO )\) and \(\pi _1(\MO )\).

Exercise 10.6 (Thom isomorphism for the tautological line). Let \(\gamma ^1\to \R \!\mathrm {P}^n\) be the tautological real line bundle. Compute the reduced mod-\(2\) cohomology of \(\Th (\gamma ^1)\) as a graded \(\F _2\)-vector space. Identify the Thom class and its restrictions to the fibers.
