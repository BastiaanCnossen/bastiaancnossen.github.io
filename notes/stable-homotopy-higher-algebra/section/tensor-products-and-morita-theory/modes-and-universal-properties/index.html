Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Part II · Chapter 18 · Section 18.2

Modes and universal properties

← Previous chapterChapter 17 → Next chapterChapter 19 ← Previous sectionSection 18.1 → Next sectionSection 18.3

Recall from Chapter 16 the sequence of colimit-preserving symmetric monoidal functors \[ (\An ,\times ) \xrightarrow {(-)_+} (\An _*,\wedge ) \xrightarrow {F^{\CMon }} (\CMon (\An ),\otimes ) \xrightarrow {(-)^{\grp }} (\CGrp (\An ),\otimes ) \xhookrightarrow {\bB ^\infty } (\Sp ,\otimes ). \] We will show that tensoring a presentable \(\infty \)-category \(C\) with the four \(\infty \)-categories after \(\An \) gives \(C_*\), \(\CMon (C)\), \(\CGrp (C)\), and \(\Sp (C)\), respectively. These constructions freely impose pointedness, semiadditivity, additivity, and stability. Each leaves \(C\) unchanged when it already has the corresponding property, so applying the construction twice has the same effect as applying it once.

This idempotence comes from the commutative algebra structures on these \(\infty \)-categories. We first discuss it in an arbitrary symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category.

Definition 18.2.1 ([Lurie (2017), Definition 4.8.2.8]). Let \(E\) be a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category. A commutative algebra \(A\in \CAlg (E)\) is idempotent if its multiplication \(A\otimes A\to A\) is an isomorphism. An object \(X\in E\) is called \(A\)-local if the unit induces an isomorphism \(X\to A\otimes X\). We write \(E^{A\text {-}\mathrm {loc}}\subseteq E\) for the full subcategory of \(A\)-local objects.

Proposition 18.2.2 ([Lurie (2017), Propositions 4.8.2.4, 4.8.2.7 and 4.8.2.10]). Let \(A\) be an idempotent commutative algebra in a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category \(E\).

(1)

The forgetful functor \(\Mod _A(E)\to E\) is fully faithful, with essential image \(E^{A\text {-}\mathrm {loc}}\).

(2)

Tensoring with \(A\) defines a symmetric monoidal Bousfield localization \[ A\otimes -\colon E\longrightarrow E^{A\text {-}\mathrm {loc}}, \] where the target has its induced symmetric monoidal structure with unit \(A\).

Proof. Both unit insertions \(A\to A\otimes A\) are inverse to multiplication. Thus Proposition 22.7.9 identifies \(A\otimes -\) as a Bousfield localization onto the \(A\)-local objects. If \(A\otimes f\) is an isomorphism, then so is \(A\otimes (f\otimes Y)\) for every \(Y\). Hence Proposition 14.7.2 supplies the symmetric monoidal structure and refinement in (2).

For an \(A\)-module \(X\), the unit and action exhibit its underlying object as a retract of \(A\otimes X\). Since locality is closed under retracts, \(X\) is \(A\)-local, and the action map \(A \otimes X \to X\) is inverse to the unit. The counit of the free-module adjunction of Proposition 17.1.14 is therefore an isomorphism, proving full faithfulness in (1). Its image is exactly the local objects, since each local object \(X\) is isomorphic to the underlying object of its free module \(A \otimes X\). □

A localization given by tensoring with a fixed object is called smashing. We apply this construction to \(\infty \)-categories.

Definition 18.2.3 (Mode, [Carmeli et al. (2021)]). A mode is an idempotent commutative algebra in \(\PrL \).

Since \(\PrL \hookrightarrow \widehat {\Cat }_{\infty }^{\mathrm {colim}}\) is fully faithful and symmetric monoidal, a mode \(M\) is also an idempotent commutative algebra in the latter \(\infty \)-category. Thus a cocomplete \(\infty \)-category \(C\) is \(M\)-local if the unit induces an equivalence \(C\simeq \An \otimes C\to M\otimes C\). By Proposition 18.2.2, this is equivalent to admitting a unique \(M\)-module structure. Tensoring with \(M\) defines a symmetric monoidal Bousfield localization of \(\widehat {\Cat }_{\infty }^{\mathrm {colim}}\), which restricts to the corresponding localization of \(\PrL \).

Corollary 18.2.4. Let \(M\) be a mode and let \(C,D\) be cocomplete \(\infty \)-categories. If \(D\) is \(M\)-local, restriction along the unit \(C\to M\otimes C\) induces an equivalence \[ \Fun ^{\colim }(M\otimes C,D)\iso \Fun ^{\colim }(C,D). \]

Proof. The localization universal property gives an equivalence on groupoid cores. For every small \(\infty \)-category \(K\), the pointwise \(M\)-action equips \(\Fun (K,D)\) with an \(M\)-module structure, so it is again \(M\)-local. Applying the universal property to these targets and currying proves the equivalence of functor categories by Yoneda. □

The four constructions at the beginning of this section provide the following examples.

Theorem 18.2.5 (The basic modes, [Gepner et al. (2015), Sections 3--5]; see also [Lurie (2017), Section 4.8.2]). The presentably symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-categories \[ (\An _*,\wedge ), \qquad (\CMon (\An ),\otimes ), \qquad (\CGrp (\An ),\otimes ), \qquad (\Sp ,\otimes ) \] are modes. For every presentable \(\infty \)-category \(C\), there are natural equivalences \[ \begin {aligned} \An _*\otimes C &\simeq C_*, & \CMon (\An )\otimes C &\simeq \CMon (C), \\ \CGrp (\An )\otimes C &\simeq \CGrp (C), & \Sp \otimes C &\simeq \Sp (C). \end {aligned} \] Under these equivalences, the localization units are the functors adjoining a basepoint, forming the free commutative monoid or group, and taking the suspension spectrum, respectively.

Proof. Let \(F\) be one of \((-)_*\), \(\CMon \), \(\CGrp \), and \(\Sp \), and put \(M:=F(\An )\). These constructions are defined by diagrams satisfying limit conditions, so they commute with forming \(\infty \)-categories of limit-preserving functors. Thus Proposition 18.1.6, Corollary 23.2.6 give \[ C\otimes M \simeq \Fun ^{\lim }(C\catop ,F(\An )) \simeq F\bigl (\Fun ^{\lim }(C\catop ,\An )\bigr ) \simeq F(C). \] Indeed, limits in all four diagram categories are computed pointwise, and the defining conditions can be checked pointwise as well.

By Proposition 16.6.4, the unit \(\An \to M\) is the corresponding free functor, whose right adjoint is the forgetful or evaluation functor \(M\to \An \). The naturality assertion of Proposition 18.1.6 identifies the right adjoint of \(C\to C\otimes M\) with the usual forgetful or evaluation functor \(F(C)\to C\). Hence the localization units have the asserted descriptions.

The \(\infty \)-category \(M\) is pointed, semiadditive, additive, or stable, respectively, and in each case \(F(M)\to M\) is an equivalence; see Lemma 4.1.9, Proposition 5.3.20, Proposition 5.3.17, Theorem 4.3.14, Corollary 4.3.17. Consequently \(M\to M\otimes M\) is an equivalence, and its left inverse given by the existing multiplication is an equivalence as well. This proves that \(M\) is a mode. □

The functors in the sequence at the beginning of this section are morphisms of modes, meaning morphisms in \(\CAlg (\PrL )\): they are colimit-preserving and symmetric monoidal by Proposition 16.6.4.

We now identify the local cocomplete \(\infty \)-categories. Among presentable \(\infty \)-categories, this can directly be inferred from the tensor descriptions in Theorem 18.2.5. For the general case, we first compute colimit-preserving functors out of the four basic modes.

Lemma 18.2.6 (Nonmonoidal universal properties of the basic modes). Let \(M\) be one of \(\An _*\), \(\CMon (\An )\), \(\CGrp (\An )\), and \(\Sp \), and let \(D\) be a cocomplete \(\infty \)-category that is respectively pointed, semiadditive, additive, or stable. Evaluation at the monoidal unit of \(M\) induces an equivalence \[ \ev _{\unit _M}\colon \Fun ^{\colim }(M,D)\iso D. \]

Proof. The case \(M=\Sp \) is Theorem 4.4.14. For the other three cases, we claim that for every cocomplete \(D\) there are natural equivalences \[ \begin {aligned} \Fun ^{\colim }(\An _*,D)\quad &\simeq \quad \bigl ((D\catop )_*\bigr )\catop ,\\ \Fun ^{\colim }(\CMon (\An ),D) \quad &\simeq \quad \CMon (D\catop )\catop ,\\ \Fun ^{\colim }(\CGrp (\An ),D) \quad &\simeq \quad \CGrp (D\catop )\catop . \end {aligned} \] These follow from the presheaf universal property and the localization universal property of Theorem 23.2.2(4), which allows arbitrary cocomplete targets.

For the first equivalence, regard \(\An _*\) as the full subcategory of \(\Fun ([1],\An )=\PSh ([1]\catop )\) consisting of arrows whose source is terminal. If \(Y_0\) denotes the representable corresponding to \(0\), this is the localization at \(\emptyset \to Y_0\). A colimit-preserving functor out of this localization therefore corresponds to a functor \([1]\catop \to D\) whose value at \(0\) is initial. Such a functor is an arrow \(d\to \emptyset _D\), where \(\emptyset _D\) is initial in \(D\), or equivalently a pointed object of \(D\catop \), with the direction of natural transformations reversed.

For commutative monoids, we use the presentation in the proof of Proposition 23.2.7: \(\CMon (\An )\) is the localization of \(\Fun (\Span (\Fin ),\An )=\PSh (\Span (\Fin )\catop )\) imposing preservation of finite products. A colimit-preserving functor from this localization to \(D\) corresponds to a functor \(G\colon \Span (\Fin )\catop \to D\) carrying the corresponding finite coproduct diagrams to coproduct diagrams. This says exactly that \(G\catop \colon \Span (\Fin )\to D\catop \) preserves finite products. Localizing in addition at the shear maps imposes the grouplike condition, giving the equivalence for commutative groups.

Under these equivalences, evaluation at \(\unit _M\) corresponds to the underlying-object functor on the right: the unit is the reflection of the representable at \(1\in [1]\) or \(\lra {1}\in \Span (\Fin )\), respectively. In the pointed case this reflection is \(S^0\); in the other two cases it is the free commutative monoid or group on a point. If \(D\) is pointed, semiadditive, or additive, respectively, then so is \(D\catop \), and the underlying-object functor is an equivalence by Lemma 4.1.9, Proposition 5.3.17. This proves the claim. □

Proposition 18.2.7. Let \(M\) be one of \(\An _*\), \(\CMon (\An )\), \(\CGrp (\An )\), and \(\Sp \). A cocomplete \(\infty \)-category \(D\) is \(M\)-local if and only if it is respectively pointed, semiadditive, additive, or stable.

Proof. Suppose first that \(D\) has the corresponding property. The functor category \(\Fun ^{\colim }(M,D)\) is cocomplete, with colimits computed pointwise. For every other cocomplete \(\infty \)-category \(E\), currying identifies restriction along \(E\to M\otimes E\) with \[ \Fun ^{\colim }\bigl (E,\Fun ^{\colim }(M,D)\bigr ) \xrightarrow {\ (\ev _{\unit _M})_*\ } \Fun ^{\colim }(E,D). \] This is an equivalence by Lemma 18.2.6, so \(D\) is \(M\)-local.

Conversely, if \(D\) is \(M\)-local, then Corollary 18.2.4 with \(C=\An \) gives \(D\simeq \Fun ^{\colim }(M,D)\). For the first three choices of \(M\), the formulas in the preceding proof express the latter as the opposite of a pointed, semiadditive, or additive \(\infty \)-category, respectively; see Lemma 4.1.9, Proposition 5.3.20.

For \(M=\Sp \), the functor category \(\Fun ^{\colim }(\Sp ,D)\) is pointed by the dual of Corollary 4.1.12, with zero object the constant functor at the initial object of \(D\). Its colimits are computed pointwise, and the suspension of \(F\) is \(F\circ \Sigma _{\Sp }\), since \(F\) preserves suspension pushouts. Precomposition with \(\Sigma _{\Sp }\) is an equivalence, so Theorem 4.2.2 proves stability. □

We write \(\Fun ^{\colim ,\otimes }(C,D)\) for the \(\infty \)-category of colimit-preserving symmetric monoidal functors and monoidal natural transformations.

Proposition 18.2.8 (Multiplicative universal property of modes). Let \(M\) be a mode, and let \(C,D\in \CAlg (\widehat {\Cat }_{\infty }^{\mathrm {colim}})\). Equip \(M\otimes C\) with the symmetric monoidal structure obtained by applying the symmetric monoidal localization \(M\otimes -\) to \(C\). If \(D\) is \(M\)-local, restriction along \(C\to M\otimes C\) induces an equivalence \[ \Fun ^{\colim ,\otimes }(M\otimes C,D) \iso \Fun ^{\colim ,\otimes }(C,D). \]

Proof. Apply Proposition 18.2.2(2) in \(\widehat {\Cat }_{\infty }^{\mathrm {colim}}\). By Corollary 14.7.4, its symmetric monoidal localization induces a localization on commutative algebra objects, giving the asserted equivalence on groupoid cores.

For every small \(\infty \)-category \(K\), the pointwise \(M\)-action makes \(\Fun (K,D)\) \(M\)-local. Its colimits and tensor product are computed pointwise, so it satisfies the same hypotheses as \(D\). Applying the same argument with this target and currying gives the equivalence of functor categories by Yoneda. □

Remark 18.2.9. Let \(C\) be a presentably symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category. For \(M=\CMon (\An )\), the equivalence \(M\otimes C\simeq \CMon (C)\) of Theorem 18.2.5 identifies the induced symmetric monoidal structure with the Day convolution structure of Proposition 16.4.1. Indeed, the latter makes the free functor \(C\to \CMon (C)\) symmetric monoidal, and its target is semiadditive. By Proposition 18.2.8, this functor factors through a colimit-preserving symmetric monoidal functor \[ \CMon (\An )\otimes C\longrightarrow \CMon (C). \] Its underlying functor is the equivalence of Theorem 18.2.5, by the uniqueness in Corollary 18.2.4.

Taking \(C=\An \) gives the following initiality statement.

Corollary 18.2.10 (Multiplicative universal property). Every mode \(M\) is initial among \(M\)-local cocomplete symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-categories whose tensor products preserve colimits separately in each variable.

Proof. For \(M\)-local \(D\), Proposition 18.2.8 gives \[ \Fun ^{\colim ,\otimes }(M,D)\simeq \Fun ^{\colim ,\otimes }(\An ,D). \] Since \(\An \) is the monoidal unit of \(\widehat {\Cat }_{\infty }^{\mathrm {colim}}\), it is initial in its \(\infty \)-category of commutative algebras. Thus the groupoid core on the right is contractible. Applying this also with target \(\Fun (K,D)\) shows that the whole functor category is contractible. □

By Proposition 18.2.7, if \(M\) is one of the four basic modes and \(D\in \CAlg (\widehat {\Cat }_{\infty }^{\mathrm {colim}})\) has the corresponding property in Table 18.1, then \(\Fun ^{\colim ,\otimes }(M,D)\) is contractible. These universal properties characterize the four symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-categories and determine the transition functors.
