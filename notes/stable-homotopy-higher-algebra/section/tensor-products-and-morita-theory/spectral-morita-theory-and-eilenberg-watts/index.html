Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Part II · Chapter 18 · Section 18.3

Spectral Morita theory and Eilenberg–Watts

← Previous chapterChapter 17 → Next chapterChapter 19 ← Previous sectionSection 18.2 → Next sectionSection 19.1

A compact generator allows us to recover a presentable stable \(\infty \)-category from its endomorphism ring spectrum. We first construct this ring spectrum and the associated module-valued mapping functor. We then prove the Morita equivalence and identify colimit-preserving functors between module categories with bimodules.

Proposition 18.3.1 ([Lurie (2017), Remark 7.1.2.2]). Let \(D\) be a stable \(\infty \)-category and \(M\in D\).

(1)

The mapping spectrum \(A:=\hom _D(M,M)\) admits a preferred structure of an associative ring spectrum.

(2)

The mapping-spectrum functor \(\hom _D(M,-)\colon D\to \Sp \) admits a preferred lift \[ \hom _D(M,-)\colon D\longrightarrow \RMod _A, \] with right \(A\)-action given by precomposition.

If \(D\) is presentable, this lift fits into an adjunction \[ -\otimes _A M\colon \RMod _A\rightleftarrows D\noloc \hom _D(M,-). \]

Proof. Suppose first that \(D\) is presentable. By Theorem 18.2.5, Proposition 18.2.2, it carries a unique \(\Sp \)-module structure in \(\PrL \). The action functor \(-\otimes M\colon \Sp \to D\) preserves colimits and sends \(\S \) to \(M\), so Theorem 4.4.14 identifies it with the tensoring of Proposition 4.4.9. Its right adjoint is \(\hom _D(M,-)\). In particular, there are natural isomorphisms \[ \Hom _{\Sp }(E,A)\simeq \Hom _D(E\otimes M,M), \] so \(A\) is an endomorphism object of \(M\). By Corollary 17.3.3, it has a preferred associative algebra structure and acts on \(M\) from the left.

By Lemma 17.2.11, the relative tensor product \(-\otimes _A M\colon \RMod _A\to D\) preserves small colimits, so it has a right adjoint \(G\) by Theorem 23.2.5.

Let \(U\colon \RMod _A\to \Sp \) be the forgetful functor. The composite \(UG\) is right adjoint to \[ K\longmapsto (K\otimes A)\otimes _A M\simeq K\otimes M, \] hence \(UG\simeq \hom _D(M,-)\). Under this identification, the right \(A\)-action is precomposition: its adjoint is the composite of evaluations \[ \hom _D(M,X)\otimes A\otimes M \longrightarrow \hom _D(M,X)\otimes M \longrightarrow X. \] This proves both assertions and the stated adjunction in the presentable case.

For arbitrary \(D\), enlarge the universe so that \(D\) is small and use the fully faithful exact embedding \(D\hookrightarrow \Ind (D)\). Its target is presentable and stable by Proposition 23.3.6 and [Lurie (2017), Proposition 1.1.3.6]. The embedding preserves mapping spectra, so applying the presentable case in \(\Ind (D)\) and restricting its module-valued functor to \(D\) proves the result. □

Theorem 18.3.2 (Spectral Morita theorem, [Lurie (2017), Theorem 7.1.2.1]). Let \(D\) be a presentable stable \(\infty \)-category with a compact generator \(M\), and let \(A:=\hom _D(M,M)\). Then the adjunction of Proposition 18.3.1 is an equivalence: \[ -\otimes _A M\colon \RMod _A\rightleftarrows D\noloc \hom _D(M,-). \]

Proof. Since \(M\) is compact, \(\hom _D(M,-)\) preserves filtered colimits; being exact, it preserves finite colimits, and hence all colimits. The forgetful functor \(\RMod _A\to \Sp \) detects and preserves colimits, so the same holds for the module-valued functor.

The counit at \(M\) is the canonical isomorphism \(A\otimes _A M\to M\). The triangle identity then shows that the unit at \(A=\hom _D(M,M)\) is an isomorphism as well. Both adjoints are exact and preserve colimits, so the full subcategories on which the unit, respectively the counit, is an isomorphism are stable and closed under colimits. They contain the generators \(A\) of \(\RMod _A\) and \(M\) of \(D\) (Example 23.3.10), and are therefore the entire \(\infty \)-categories. □

Remark 18.3.3. In the setting of stable model categories, this result is due to Schwede and Shipley (2003), and it is often referred to as the Schwede–Shipley theorem.

Remark 18.3.4. Barr–Beck gives an alternative proof of Theorem 18.3.2. Consider the adjunction \[ -\otimes M\colon \Sp \rightleftarrows D\noloc \hom _D(M,-). \] The right adjoint is conservative because \(M\) is a generator, and it preserves all colimits by compactness and exactness. Barr–Beck therefore identifies \(D\) with the Eilenberg–Moore category of the associated monad on \(\Sp \).

The evaluation action of \(A=\hom _D(M,M)\) on \(M\) induces a morphism from the monad \(-\otimes A\) to the adjunction monad, with underlying natural transformation \[ -\otimes A\longrightarrow \hom _D(M,-\otimes M). \] The action unit and associativity give compatibility with the monad unit and multiplication. The underlying transformation is an isomorphism because both functors preserve colimits and it is an isomorphism on the sphere spectrum. Hence the Eilenberg–Moore category is \(\RMod _A\), and its comparison functor is the module-valued functor \(\hom _D(M,-)\).

When \(D\) is presentably symmetric monoidal and \(M\) is its tensor unit, the equivalence also recovers the tensor product.

Corollary 18.3.5 (Symmetric monoidal Morita equivalence, [Lurie (2017), Proposition 7.1.2.7]). Let \(C\) be a presentably symmetric monoidal stable \(\infty \)-category whose unit \(\unit \) is a compact generator. Then \(A:=\hom _C(\unit ,\unit )\) is canonically a commutative ring spectrum, and \(\hom _C(\unit ,-)\) lifts to a symmetric monoidal equivalence \[ \Phi \colon C\iso \Mod _A. \]

Proof. By Corollary 18.2.10, the functor \(-\otimes \unit \colon \Sp \to C\) is symmetric monoidal. Its right adjoint \(\hom _C(\unit ,-)\) is therefore lax symmetric monoidal by Corollary 4.4.10, Proposition 14.5.6, giving \(A\) its commutative ring structure and inducing a lax symmetric monoidal functor \[ \Phi \colon C\simeq \Mod _{\unit }(C)\longrightarrow \Mod _A. \] The evaluation action of \(A\) on \(\unit \) identifies its underlying associative algebra with the endomorphism algebra of \(\unit \) by Corollary 17.3.3, since the induced algebra map is the identity on underlying spectra. The lax structure maps are adjoint to tensor products of evaluation maps; thus the induced right \(A\)-action on \(\hom _C(\unit ,X)\) is the precomposition action of Proposition 18.3.1. Identifying \(\Mod _A\) with \(\RMod _A\) by Observation 17.2.5, we may therefore apply Theorem 18.3.2 to conclude that \(\Phi \) is an equivalence and preserves colimits.

It remains to show that \(\Phi \) is symmetric monoidal. Its unit map \(A\to \Phi (\unit )\) is an isomorphism. For fixed \(Y\), both sides of the lax structure map \[ \Phi (X)\otimes _A\Phi (Y)\longrightarrow \Phi (X\otimes Y) \] preserve colimits in \(X\) (Lemma 17.2.11), and hence are exact. The map is an isomorphism at \(X=\unit \) by unitality, so it is an isomorphism everywhere because \(\unit \) generates \(C\). Thus \(\Phi \) is a symmetric monoidal equivalence. □

We now describe colimit-preserving functors between module categories in terms of bimodules.

For associative ring spectra \(R\) and \(S\), the relative tensor product gives a functor \[ -\otimes _R-\colon \RMod _R\times {}_R\BMod _S\longrightarrow \RMod _S \] which preserves colimits separately in both variables by Lemma 17.2.11. Currying therefore gives a functor \[ {}_R\BMod _S\longrightarrow \FunL (\RMod _R,\RMod _S). \]

Theorem 18.3.6 (Spectral Eilenberg–Watts theorem, [Lurie (2017), Proposition 7.1.2.4]). For all associative ring spectra \(R\) and \(S\), the functor \[ {}_R\BMod _S\longrightarrow \FunL (\RMod _R,\RMod _S) \] is an equivalence.

Proof. We construct an inverse by sending \(F\) to \(F(R)\). By Theorem 18.2.5, Proposition 18.2.2, every colimit-preserving functor between presentable stable \(\infty \)-categories has a unique \(\Sp \)-linear structure. This also applies to all natural transformations: apply the same assertion with target \(\Fun (K,\RMod _S)\) for every small \(\infty \)-category \(K\) and curry, as in the proof of Proposition 18.2.8. Applying \(F\) to the regular bimodule \(R\), regarded as a left \(R\)-module in \(\RMod _R\), therefore gives an \((R,S)\)-bimodule \(F(R)\) by Theorem 17.2.6. Its left action is \[ R\otimes F(R)\simeq F(R\otimes R)\longrightarrow F(R), \] where the last map is induced by multiplication. This construction defines a functor \(F\mapsto F(R)\).

For a right \(R\)-module \(M\), the bar construction \(\Bar _R(M,R)_\bullet \) has terms \(M\otimes R^{\otimes n}\otimes R\) and realization \(M\otimes _RR\simeq M\). In the operadic construction of Remark 17.2.10, we may place the final input in \(\RMod _R\) and the preceding inputs in \(\Sp \). Since \(F\) is \(\Sp \)-linear, it preserves these operations and their cocartesian transport, identifying \(F(\Bar _R(M,R)_\bullet )\) with \(\Bar _R(M,F(R))_\bullet \). The last face map uses the left \(R\)-action just constructed; the other faces and degeneracies are induced by maps of spectra. Since \(F\) preserves geometric realizations, we obtain \[ F(M)\simeq \abs {\Bar _R(M,F(R))_\bullet } \simeq M\otimes _RF(R), \] naturally in \(M\) and \(F\).

Conversely, evaluating \(-\otimes _RB\) at \(R\) gives \(R\otimes _RB\simeq B\) as \((R,S)\)-bimodules. These natural isomorphisms show that the two functors are inverse. □

Associative ring spectra \(R\) and \(S\) are called Morita equivalent if \(\RMod _R\) and \(\RMod _S\) are equivalent. By Theorem 18.3.6, this amounts to the existence of an \((R,S)\)-bimodule \(B\) and an \((S,R)\)-bimodule \(C\) with isomorphisms \(B\otimes _SC\simeq R\) and \(C\otimes _RB\simeq S\) as bimodules; see Chapterexercise 18.3.

Exercises

Exercise 18.1 (Tensor products of presheaf categories). Let \(C\) and \(D\) be small \(\infty \)-categories. Show that the external-product functor \[ \PSh (C)\times \PSh (D)\longrightarrow \PSh (C\times D), \qquad (F,G)\longmapsto \bigl ((c,d)\longmapsto F(c)\times G(d)\bigr ), \] exhibits \(\PSh (C\times D)\) as the tensor product \(\PSh (C)\otimes \PSh (D)\) in \(\widehat {\Cat }_{\infty }^{\mathrm {colim}}\).

Exercise 18.2. Consider the sequence of modes \[ \An \longrightarrow \An _*\longrightarrow \CMon (\An )\longrightarrow \CGrp (\An )\longrightarrow \Sp \] from Theorem 18.2.5. Show that the tensor product of any two of these modes is the one which occurs further to the right in this sequence.

Exercise 18.3. Let \(R,S,T\) be associative ring spectra. For an \((R,S)\)-bimodule \(B\), write \(F_B:=-\otimes _RB\colon \RMod _R\to \RMod _S\).

(1)

Show that natural transformations \(F_B\to F_{B'}\) correspond to morphisms of \((R,S)\)-bimodules \(B\to B'\).

(2)

For an \((S,T)\)-bimodule \(C\), identify the \((R,T)\)-bimodule corresponding to \(F_C\circ F_B\).

(3)

Identify the bimodule corresponding to the identity functor of \(\RMod _R\).

(4)

Deduce that \(F_B\) is an equivalence if and only if there is an \((S,R)\)-bimodule \(C\) with isomorphisms \[ B\otimes _SC\simeq R \qquadtext {and} C\otimes _RB\simeq S \] of \((R,R)\)- and \((S,S)\)-bimodules, respectively.

Exercise 18.4 (Matrix Morita equivalence). Let \(R\) be an associative ring spectrum and let \(n\geq 1\). Put \(P:=R^{\oplus n}\in \RMod _R\) and let \[ A:=\hom _{\RMod _R}(P,P). \]

(1)

Show that \(P\) is a compact generator of \(\RMod _R\). Identify the underlying spectrum of \(A\) with \(\bigoplus _{i,j=1}^nR\) and show that composition gives matrix multiplication. In particular, deduce an isomorphism \(\pi _*A\cong M_n(\pi _*R)\) of graded rings. We call \(A\) the matrix ring spectrum \(M_n(R)\).

(2)

The construction of Proposition 18.3.1 gives an adjunction \[ -\otimes _AP\colon \RMod _A\rightleftarrows \RMod _R\noloc \hom _{\RMod _R}(P,-). \] Deduce from Theorem 18.3.2 that this adjunction is an equivalence.
