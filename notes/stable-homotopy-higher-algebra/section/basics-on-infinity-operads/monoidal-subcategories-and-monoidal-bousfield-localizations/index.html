Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Part II · Chapter 14 · Section 14.7

Monoidal subcategories and monoidal Bousfield localizations

← Previous chapterChapter 13 → Next chapterChapter 15 ← Previous sectionSection 14.6 → Next sectionSection 15.1

A full subcategory \(D\) of a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category \(C\) determines a full suboperad of \(\Mm _C\) by Construction 14.1.7. If \(D\) contains the unit and is closed under tensor products, this suboperad is itself symmetric monoidal. For a reflective subcategory, there is another possibility: tensor the objects in the ambient \(\infty \)-category and then apply the reflector. We will show that this defines a symmetric monoidal structure whenever tensoring preserves the morphisms inverted by the reflector.

Lemma 14.7.1. Let \((C, \otimes _C, \unit _C)\) be a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category. If \(D \subseteq C\) is a full subcategory whose collection of objects \(D^{\simeq } \subseteq C^{\simeq }\) contains \(\unit _C\) and is closed under \(\otimes _C\), then \(D\) inherits a symmetric monoidal structure \((D, \otimes _D, \unit _D)\) from \(C\) such that the inclusion \(i\colon D \hookrightarrow C\) is a (strong) symmetric monoidal functor.

Proof. The full suboperad on the colors of \(D\) is closed under cocartesian transport in \(C^{\otimes }\): by Observation 14.4.7, this transport is computed by finite tensor products, and closure under these follows from closure under the unit and binary tensor products. The cocartesian universal properties restrict by full faithfulness, so \(D^{\otimes }\to \Span (\Fin )\) is a cocartesian fibration and its inclusion preserves cocartesian morphisms. □

Recall that a Bousfield localization is a functor \(L\colon C \to D\) that admits a fully faithful right adjoint \(i \colon D \hookrightarrow C\). In this case a morphism \(x \to y\) is called an \(L\)-equivalence if the map \(L(x) \to L(y)\) in \(D\) is an isomorphism.

Proposition 14.7.2. Let \((C,\otimes _C,\unit _C)\) be a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category, and let \(i\colon D \hookrightarrow C\) be the inclusion of a full subcategory such that \(i\) admits a left adjoint \(L\colon C \to D\). Assume that for every \(L\)-equivalence \(f\colon x \to y\) in \(C\) and for every \(z \in C\), the morphism \(f \otimes _C \id _z\colon x \otimes z \to y \otimes z\) is also an \(L\)-equivalence. Then the following hold:

(1)

The full suboperad \(D^{\otimes }\) of \(C^{\otimes }\) defines a symmetric monoidal structure on \(D\), with \[ x\otimes _Dy\simeq L(ix\otimes _Ciy),\qquad \unit _D\simeq L(\unit _C). \]

(2)

The inclusion \(i^{\otimes }\colon D^{\otimes } \hookrightarrow C^{\otimes }\) admits a left adjoint \(L^{\otimes }\colon C^{\otimes } \to D^{\otimes }\) which is a strong symmetric monoidal refinement of the functor \(L\).

(3)

If \(C\) admits small colimits and its tensor product preserves them separately in both variables, then the same holds for \(D\).

Proof. We regard objects of \(D\) as objects of \(C\), omitting \(i\). For every span \(\alpha \colon I\leftarrow K\to J\), the transport functor \(\alpha _!\colon C^I\to C^J\) preserves componentwise \(L\)-equivalences. Indeed, it is computed by restriction and finite tensor products, and a tensor product of \(L\)-equivalences is an \(L\)-equivalence by changing one factor at a time, using symmetry and the hypothesis.

We first construct a left adjoint to \(i^{\otimes }\) by sending \(\{x_i\}_{i\in I}\) to \(\{Lx_i\}_{i\in I}\). For a tuple \(X=\{x_i\}_{i\in I}\) in \(C^{\otimes }\) and a tuple \(Y\) of local objects, the componentwise unit induces a map \[ \Hom _{D^{\otimes }}(\{Lx_i\},Y)\longrightarrow \Hom _{C^{\otimes }}(X,Y). \] On the fibers over a span \(\alpha \colon I\leftarrow K\to J\), cocartesian transport identifies this with \[ \prod _{j\in J}\Hom _C((\alpha _!\{Lx_i\})_j,Y_j) \longrightarrow \prod _{j\in J}\Hom _C((\alpha _!X)_j,Y_j). \] This is an isomorphism: \(\alpha _!\) preserves componentwise \(L\)-equivalences, and maps into the local objects \(Y_j\) invert these morphisms. The pointwise criterion for adjunctions therefore gives \(L^{\otimes }\dashv i^{\otimes }\). Its unit lies over identity spans, so the adjunction is relative to \(\Span (\Fin )\). Moreover, \(L^{\otimes }\) preserves inert projections, since it applies \(L\) componentwise. Hence it is a morphism of \(\infty \)-operads by Corollary 14.1.14.

For \(X\in D^{\otimes }\) and a span \(\alpha \), consider the composite \[ X\longrightarrow \alpha _!X\longrightarrow L^{\otimes }\alpha _!X. \] The first morphism is cocartesian in \(C^{\otimes }\) and the second is the unit. The relative adjunction identifies maps from its target to any \(Y\in D^{\otimes }\) with maps from \(\alpha _!X\) to \(Y\) in \(C^{\otimes }\), over the same spans. Thus the cocartesian universal property in \(C^{\otimes }\) proves that the composite is cocartesian in \(D^{\otimes }\). This establishes the symmetric monoidal structure on \(D\) and the stated tensor and unit formulas.

For arbitrary \(X\in C^{\otimes }\), the unit induces an isomorphism \[ L^{\otimes }\alpha _!X\iso L^{\otimes }\alpha _!L^{\otimes }X, \] since \(\alpha _!\) preserves componentwise \(L\)-equivalences. Under this identification, naturality of the unit identifies the image under \(L^{\otimes }\) of a cocartesian lift \(X\to \alpha _!X\) with the cocartesian lift starting at \(L^{\otimes }X\) just constructed. Hence \(L^{\otimes }\) is strong symmetric monoidal.

Finally, colimits in \(D\) are obtained by applying \(L\) to colimits in \(C\). For a small diagram \((y_j)\) in \(D\) and \(x\in D\), we obtain \begin {align*} x\otimes _D\colim _j^D y_j &\simeq L\bigl (x\otimes _C iL(\colim _j^C iy_j)\bigr )\\ &\simeq L\bigl (x\otimes _C\colim _j^C iy_j\bigr )\\ &\simeq \colim _j^D L(x\otimes _Ciy_j) \simeq \colim _j^D(x\otimes _Dy_j). \end {align*}

The second isomorphism uses that tensoring the unit of \(L\) with \(x\) is an \(L\)-equivalence. Symmetry gives preservation of colimits in the other variable. □

When \(C\) admits internal homs, the compatibility condition on \(L\) can equivalently be expressed as a closure property of the local objects.

Lemma 14.7.3. Let \(C\) be a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category in which each functor \(-\otimes z\) admits a right adjoint \(\iHom _C(z,-)\), and let \(L\colon C\rightleftarrows D\colon i\) be a Bousfield localization. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

Tensoring with any object of \(C\) preserves \(L\)-equivalences.

(2)

For every \(z\in C\) and every local object \(w\), the object \(\iHom _C(z,w)\) is local.

Under these conditions, \(D\) inherits a symmetric monoidal structure for which \(L\) is strong symmetric monoidal.

Proof. An object is local precisely when mapping into it inverts all \(L\)-equivalences, and a morphism is an \(L\)-equivalence precisely when mapping from it into every local object gives an isomorphism. Thus, for \(f\colon x\to y\) an \(L\)-equivalence, \(z\in C\), and \(w\) local, the tensor–hom adjunction identifies the conditions in (1) and (2) by identifying the maps \[ \Hom _C(y\otimes z,w)\longrightarrow \Hom _C(x\otimes z,w) \] with \[ \Hom _C(y,\iHom _C(z,w))\longrightarrow \Hom _C(x,\iHom _C(z,w)). \] The final assertion follows from Proposition 14.7.2. □

A symmetric monoidal localization descends to algebras.

Corollary 14.7.4 (cf. [Lurie (2017), Proposition 2.2.1.9]). Let \(L \colon C \rightleftarrows D \colon i\) be as in Proposition 14.7.2, and let \(\Oo \) be an \(\infty \)-operad. Then postcomposition induces a Bousfield localization \[ \Alg _{\Oo }(L)\colon \Alg _{\Oo }(C) \rightleftarrows \Alg _{\Oo }(D) \colon \Alg _{\Oo }(i) \] Its local objects are precisely the \(\Oo \)-algebras whose values on all colors lie in \(D\).

Proof. The symmetric monoidal adjunction \(L\dashv i\) induces the asserted adjunction on algebras by Lemma 14.5.9. Since \(D^{\otimes }\) is the full suboperad on the local colors, postcomposition with \(i^{\otimes }\) is fully faithful, with the stated image. The left adjoint applies \(L\) on each color, so it inverts precisely the maps whose underlying morphisms are \(L\)-equivalences. □

The reflector applies \(L\) on each color. Consequently, a morphism of \(\Oo \)-algebras is inverted precisely when its value on every color is an \(L\)-equivalence. In particular, a commutative algebra is local precisely when its underlying object is local, and its localization has underlying object \(L(A)\).

The preceding results concern reflective localizations inside an \(\infty \)-category. The complementary problem of constructing a monoidal structure on a Dwyer–Kan localization at a class of weak equivalences is treated later in Chapter 21.

Exercises

Exercise 14.1. Show that the following functors are examples of \(\infty \)-operads:

  • \(\Comm = (\Span (\Fin ),\id )\);
  • \(\Triv = (\Fin \catop ,\mathrm {inclusion})\);
  • \(\Triv _{\emptyset } = (*, \mathrm {inclusion})\);
  • \(\Ee _0 = (\Span (\Fin ,\all ,\inj ), \mathrm {inclusion})\).

In each case, compute the multimorphism animae and the corresponding composition of multimorphisms, show that these animae are discrete, and identify the resulting \(\infty \)-operads with their classical counterparts from Section 12.1, or with the empty-colored operad in the case of \(\Triv _{\emptyset }\).

Exercise 14.2.

(1)

Prove the converse of Lemma 14.4.9: if an \(\infty \)-operad \(\Oo \) is the multimorphism operad of a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category, then both hypotheses of that lemma are satisfied. Conclude that these two conditions characterize the essential image of \(\Mm \colon \Cat _{\infty }^{\otimes }\to \Op _{\infty }\) on objects.

(2)

Given symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-categories \(C\) and \(D\), show that an operad map \(f\colon \Mm _C \to \Mm _D\) lies in the image of the functor \(\Mm \) if and only if the map \(f^{\otimes }\colon C^{\otimes } \to D^{\otimes }\) preserves the cocartesian lifts \((x_1, \dots , x_n) \to x_1 \otimes \dots \otimes x_n\) of the span \(\lra {n} \xleftarrow {=} \lra {n} \to \lra {1}\) for every \(n \geq 0\). Use Observation 14.4.7 to pass from these one-output lifts to arbitrary active spans.

Exercise 14.3 (Nullary and binary operations suffice). Let \(\Oo \) be an \(\infty \)-operad. Suppose that for every family \(\{x_i\}_{i\in I}\) with \(|I|=0\) or \(|I|=2\), there is a multimorphism \(\varphi \colon \{x_i\}_{i\in I}\to y\) such that substitution induces an isomorphism \[ \Oo (\{y\}\sqcup \{z_j\}_{j\in J};w)\longrightarrow \Oo (\{x_i\}_{i\in I}\sqcup \{z_j\}_{j\in J};w) \] for every finite family of colors \(\{z_j\}_{j\in J}\) and every color \(w\).

(1)

Show that multimorphisms with this property are closed under operadic substitution, and deduce that every finite tuple admits such a multimorphism.

(2)

Apply Lemma 14.4.9 to show that \(\Oo \) arises from a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category.

Exercise 14.4. Equip \(\Ab \) with its tensor product and \(\Set \) with its cartesian monoidal structure. Show that the forgetful functor \(U\colon \Ab \to \Set \) is lax symmetric monoidal, with structure maps \[ U(A)\times U(B)\longrightarrow U(A\otimes B), \qquad (a,b)\longmapsto a\otimes b, \] and unit determined by \(1\in \Z \). Identify the associative and commutative algebra structures induced on the underlying set of a ring.

Exercise 14.5 (Algebras in an arrow category). Let \(C\) be a symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-category and equip \(\Fun ([1],C)\) with the pointwise symmetric monoidal structure. Show that an \(\Oo \)-algebra in \(\Fun ([1],C)\) is precisely a morphism of \(\Oo \)-algebras in \(C\). Specialize to \(\Oo =\Assoc \) and show that sources, targets, and composites of algebra morphisms are computed on the underlying objects of \(C\).

Exercise 14.6 (Arithmetic localization is monoidal). Let \(P\) be a set of primes and let \(L(X):=X[P^{-1}]\) be the smashing localization of spectra from Section 7.2. Show that the \(L\)-equivalences are stable under tensoring with arbitrary spectra. Construct the symmetric monoidal structure on the full subcategory of \(P\)-divisible spectra and describe the localization of associative and commutative ring spectra.
