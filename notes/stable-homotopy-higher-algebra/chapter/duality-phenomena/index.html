Stable Homotopy Theory and Higher Algebra · Part I · Chapter 11

Duality phenomena

← Previous chapterChapter 10 → Next chapterChapter 12 ← Previous sectionSection 10.5 → Next sectionSection 11.1

Poincaré duality identifies the cohomology of a closed oriented \(n\)-manifold \(M\) with its homology in complementary degrees: \[ H^k(M;\Z )\cong H_{n-k}(M;\Z ). \] In this chapter, we explain Poincaré duality from the viewpoint of stable homotopy theory and extend it to generalized homology and cohomology theories. We use the Thom spectra and orientations of Chapter 10.

Spanier and Whitehead (1955) developed a duality theory for finite polyhedra by studying their complements in spheres. Their construction lifts classical homological duality to a relation between stable homotopy classes of maps. In modern terms, every finite spectrum \(X\) has a dual spectrum \(DX\), and for every spectrum \(E\) there are natural isomorphisms \[ E^k(X)\cong E_{-k}(DX). \]

For a closed smooth manifold \(M\), we would like a geometric description of the dual of its suspension spectrum \(\S [M]=\Sigma ^\infty M_+\). Milnor and Spanier (1960) obtained such a description using the Thom space of the normal bundle of an embedding into Euclidean space. Atiyah (1961b) developed this further into a duality theorem for Thom complexes. Using virtual bundles, the resulting Atiyah duality takes the form \[ D(\S [M])\cong \th (-T_M). \] Indeed, if \(M\hookrightarrow \R ^N\) has normal bundle \(\nu \), then \([\nu ]-[\ul {\R ^N}]=-T_M\), so the desuspended normal Thom spectrum can be expressed in terms of the tangent bundle alone.

Atiyah duality on its own requires no orientation. Given a commutative ring spectrum \(R\) and an \(R\)-orientation of the tangent bundle, we may apply the Thom isomorphism of Theorem 10.4.6 and obtain an isomorphism \[ R^k(M)\cong R_{n-k}(M). \] For \(R=\Z \), this recovers classical Poincaré duality. For other ring spectra, the required orientation depends on the cohomology theory, as discussed in Section 10.4.

We develop these results using duality in symmetric monoidal \(\infty \)-categories, following the categorical approach to topological duality systematically developed by Dold and Puppe (1980). We begin with dualizable objects in Section 11.1 and their relation to finiteness in Section 11.2: for modules over a commutative ring spectrum, dualizability is equivalent to both compactness and perfectness. We specialize to spectra in Subsection 11.2.1, and in Section 11.3 we prove Atiyah duality by constructing evaluation and coevaluation maps using Pontryagin–Thom collapse maps. Finally, in Section 11.4, we deduce generalized Poincaré duality and identify its isomorphism with cap product by the fundamental class.

For a historical account of these developments, we refer to Becker and Gottlieb (1999).
