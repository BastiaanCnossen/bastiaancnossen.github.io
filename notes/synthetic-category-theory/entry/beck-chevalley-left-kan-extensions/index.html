Construction 9.6.1 (The Beck–Chevalley map under straightening). Consider a commutative square of small categories and a functor \(F\colon A \to \An\). This data determines the Beck–Chevalley map \[ \BC_! \colon f'_!u^*(F) \to v^*f_!(F) \] in \(\Fun(B',\An)\). The Beck–Chevalley map is defined as the composite \[ f'_!u^*(F) \xrightarrow{\eta} f'_!u^*f^*f_!(F) = f'_!{f'}^*v^*f_!(F) \xrightarrow{\varepsilon} v^*f_!(F), \] where \(\eta\colon \id_{\Fun(A,\An)} \to f^*f_!\) is the unit of \(f_! \dashv f^*\) and \(\varepsilon\colon f'_!{f'}^* \to \id_{\Fun(B',\An)}\) is the counit of \(f'_! \dashv {f'}^*\). Equivalently, \(\BC_!\) is adjoint to \[ u^*(F) \xrightarrow{\eta} u^*f^*f_!F = {f'}^*v^*f_!(F). \]

We next describe this map under straightening. Consider the pullback square where the right vertical map is the universal left fibration with small fibers. Choose a factorization of \(f \circ p\colon X \to B\) as \[ X \xrightarrow{j} Y \xrightarrow{q} B, \] where \(j\) is initial and \(q\) is a left fibration. In particular, \(q\) is given via a pullback square for some functor \(f_!(F)\). The commutative square on the left exhibits the unit \(F \to f^*f_!F\). Pulling back along \(v\) and \(u\), we obtain the commutative diagram Write \(X':=A'\times_A X\). We may factor \(f' \times_f j\) as \[ X' \xrightarrow{j'} Y' \xrightarrow{r} B' \times_B Y, \] where \(j'\) is initial and \(r\) is a left fibration. Then the composite \[ q' := v^*q \circ r \colon Y' \to B' \] is again a left fibration. These factorizations fit into the commutative diagram We have pullback squares and The map \(r\colon Y'\to B'\times_B Y\) over \(B'\) corresponds under directed univalence to a natural transformation \[ f'_!u^*(F)\to v^*f_!(F). \] Its adjunct along \(f'_!\dashv {f'}^*\) is represented by the composite \(rj'\colon X'\to B'\times_B Y\). This composite is induced by \(j\colon X\to Y\), which represents the unit \(F\to f^*f_!(F)\). Its adjunct is therefore the map \[ u^*(F)\to u^*f^*f_!(F) = {f'}^*v^*f_!(F) \] that defines the Beck–Chevalley map. Hence \(r\) corresponds to \(\BC_!\colon f'_!u^*(F) \to v^*f_!(F)\).
