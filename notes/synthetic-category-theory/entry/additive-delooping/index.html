Proposition 20.4.13. Let \(C\) be an admissible class of Waldhausen categories. We consider a finite products preserving functor \[ F\colon\WaldCat_C\to \An_{\bullet}\, . \] There is a canonical commutative square functorially in \(A\) and \(F\), that is cartesian whenever \(F\) is additive.

Proof

We observe that \(F(\Scons_0(A))\) is contractible for all \(A\) and thus, by virtue of Lemma 20.4.12 there is a canonical equivalence of the form \[ \underset{[n] : \Delta\catop}{\colim}\, F(\Scons_{n+1}(A))={*} \] The functorial embedding \(i_n\colon [n]\hookrightarrow [n+1]\) defined through the identification of \([n+1]\) with \([0]\star[n]\) defines a functor \[ i^*_n\colon\Scons_{n+1}(A)\to\Scons_n(A) \] functorially in \([n]\) and in \(A\). We obtain a canonical commutative square of the form where the vertical maps are induced by the functors \(i^*_n\) above and the horizontal maps are instances of the canonical map \[ t_0\to \underset{[n] : \Delta\catop}{\colim}\, t_n \] for any functor \(t\colon\Delta\catop\to\An_{\bullet}\).

It suffices now to prove that the commutative square above is cartesian whenever \(F\) is additive. By virtue of Lemma 13.4.4, it suffices to prove that, for any additive functor \(F\colon\WaldCat_C\to\An_{\bullet}\), and any map \(u\colon [m]\to [n]\) in \(\Delta\), the functor \(F\) sends the commutative square of Waldhausen categories to a pullback square. We conclude this proof by observing thanks to Corollary 20.4.11 that this commutative square is equivalent to the commutative square which is obviously cartesian.
