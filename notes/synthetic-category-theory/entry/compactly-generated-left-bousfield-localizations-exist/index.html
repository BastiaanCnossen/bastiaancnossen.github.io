Theorem 18.1.6. Let \(C\) be a locally small category with small colimits and \(S\hookrightarrow\Map([1],C_\omega)\) a small family of maps between compact objects of \(C\). Then the full embedding \(L_S(C) \hookrightarrow C\) has a left adjoint \[ \loc_S\colon C\to L_S(C) \] exhibiting \(L_S(C)\) as the localization of \(C\) by the family of \(S\)-equivalences. Furthermore, the functor \(\loc_S\) preserves compact objects.

Proof of Theorem 18.1.6 through the small object argument

By virtue of Lemma 18.1.11, we may assume that \(S\) is codiagonal-stable. We define a functor \[ L\colon C\to C \] as well as a natural transformation \(l\colon \id_C\to L\) as follows. There is a functor \[ \An\times C\to C \ , \quad (a,x)\mapsto a\otimes x \] defined by the functorial formula \[ \Hom_C((a\otimes x)\catop,y)=\Hom_\An(a\catop,\Hom_C(x\catop,y)) \] for \(a : \An\) and \(x,y : C\). More precisely, if \(a\) is an anima and \(x\) an object of \(C\), then \(a\otimes x\) is the \(a\)-indexed colimit of the constant diagram with value \(x\).

Given \(x : C\) and an object \(a\) of \(C\), there is thus a canonical map \[ c_{a,x}\colon \Hom_C(a,x)\otimes a\to x \] corresponding to the identity of \(\Hom_C(a,x)\), functorially in \(x\). We define \(A(x)\) as the colimit indexed by \(S\) of the diagram that assigns \(\Hom_C(a,x)\otimes a\) for each term \(u\colon a\to b\) of \(S\). Similarly, we define \(B(x)\) as the colimit indexed by \(S\) of the diagram that assigns \(\Hom_C(a,x)\otimes b\) for each term \(u\colon a\to b\) of \(S\). We define \[ \alpha_x\colon A(x)\to x \] as the map induced by the family \(c_{a,x}\) indexed by each term \(u\colon a\to b\) of \(S\). We form the pushout square where \(i_x\) is the \(S\)-indexed colimit of the maps \[ \id\otimes\, u\colon \Hom_C(a,x)\otimes a\to \Hom_C(a,x)\otimes b\, . \] We now define a sequence of functors \[ L^0\xrightarrow{l^1}L^1\xrightarrow{l^2}\cdots\to L^n\xrightarrow{l^{n+1}}L^{n+1}\to\cdots \] by induction through the formulas \[ L^0=\id_C\quad\text{and}\quad L^{n+1}=L\circ L^n\ , \quad l^{n+1}=l_{L^n}\, . \] We define \[ \lambda_x\colon x\to \loc_S(x) \] functorially in \(x\) by the formula \[ \loc_S(x)=\underset{n : \omega}{\colim}\, L^n(x) \] with \(\lambda_x\) the map induced by the component of the colimit cocone at \(0 : \N\). It follows from Lemma 18.1.9 that \(l_x\) is an \(S\)-equivalence for all \(x\). We can also see the map \(\lambda_x\) as the \(\omega\)-indexed colimit of the natural transformation in which the vertical maps are \(S\)-equivalences (this is proven by induction, as finite compositions of maps of the form \(l_y\) for various \(y\)’s). Therefore, Lemma 18.1.9 (ii) implies that \(\lambda_x\) is an \(S\)-equivalence for any \(x\).

We claim that \(\loc_S(x)\) is \(S\)-local. Indeed, for any \(u\colon a\to b\) in \(S\), and for any \(n : \N\), there is a canonical commutative diagram of the form in which the dashed arrow \(\lambda_n\) is induced by the map \(\beta_{L^n(x)}\). We can think of the collection of the maps \(\lambda_n\) as a morphism of sequences of morphisms of anima: Taking the associated Milnor colimits yields a morphism \[ \lambda'\colon\underset{n : \omega}{\colim}\, \Hom_C(a,L^n(x)) \to\underset{n : \omega}{\colim}\, \Hom_C(b,L^{n+1}(x))\, . \] Since \(\omega\) is filtered, the embedding \(\omega_{1/}\to\omega\) is terminal, hence we have \[ \underset{n : \omega}{\colim}\, \Hom_C(b,L^{n+1}(x))= \underset{n : \omega}{\colim}\, \Hom_C(b,L^{n}(x))\, . \] The map \(\lambda'\) thus yields a map \[ \lambda\colon\underset{n : \omega}{\colim}\, \Hom_C(a,L^n(x)) \to\underset{n : \omega}{\colim}\, \Hom_C(b,L^{n}(x))\, . \] Since \(\Hom_C(t,\loc_S(x))\) canonically is the colimit of the \(\Hom_C(t,L^n(x))\)’s for any compact object \(t\), the map \(\lambda\) determines a map \[ \bar{\lambda}\colon\Hom_C(a,\loc_S(x))\to\Hom_C(b,\loc_S(x)) \] that is a section of the map \[ u^*\colon\Hom_C(b,\loc_S(x))\to\Hom_C(a,\loc_S(x)) \] by construction. Lemma 18.1.8 thus proves that \(\loc_S(x)\) is \(S\)-local.

Consider now an \(S\)-local object \(y\) in \(C\). Then, for any object \(x : C^\simeq\), the \(S\)-equivalence \(\lambda_x\) induces an isomorphism \[ \lambda^*_x\colon\Hom_C(\loc_S(x),y)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Hom_C(x,y)\, . \] Since this equivalence is obviously functorial in \(y\), this shows that the full embedding of \(L_S(C)\) into \(C\) is a right adjoint by Theorem 12.3.20.

The characterization of \(L_S(C)\) as localization of \(C\) by \(S\)-equivalences follows from Proposition 4.4.16 and from Corollary 12.3.14 applied to \(L_S(C)\).

It remains to prove that the left adjoint \(\loc_S\) preserves compact objects. Let \(x : C^\simeq\) be a compact object. We consider a small filtered category \(I\) as well as an \(I\)-indexed functor \(i\mapsto y_i\) with values in \(L_S(C)\). We know from Proposition 18.1.5 that the colimit of the diagram of the \(y_i\)’s can be computed independently in \(L_S(C)\) or in \(C\) itself. In other words, we have \begin{align*} \underset{i : I}{\colim}\, \Hom_{L_S(C)}(\loc_S(x),y_i) &= \underset{i : I}{\colim}\, \Hom_{C}(x,y_i)\\ &= \Hom_{C}(x,\underset{i : I}{\colim}\, y_i)\\ &= \Hom_{L_S(C)}(\loc_S(x),\underset{i : I}{\colim}\, y_i)\, , \end{align*} thus proving that \(\loc_S(x)\) is a compact object of \(L_S(C)\).
