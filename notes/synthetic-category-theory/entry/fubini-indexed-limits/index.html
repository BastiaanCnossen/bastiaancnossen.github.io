Lemma 7.3.2 (Fubini for indexed limits). Let \(I\), \(J\), and \(C\) be categories. If \(C\) admits \(I\)-indexed and \(J\)-indexed limits, then it admits \((I\times J)\)-indexed limits. For every functor \(F\colon I\times J\to C\), there are canonical isomorphisms \[ \lim_{(i,j):I\times J}F(i,j) \simeq \lim_{i:I}\lim_{j:J}F(i,j) \simeq \lim_{j:J}\lim_{i:I}F(i,j), \] natural in \(F\). The analogous statement holds for colimits.

Proof

Under the exponential-law equivalence \[ \Fun(I\times J,C)\simeq\Fun(I,\Fun(J,C)), \] the constant-diagram functor \(C\to\Fun(I\times J,C)\) is the composite \[ C\xrightarrow{\const_J}\Fun(J,C) \xrightarrow{\const_I}\Fun(I,\Fun(J,C)). \] By the dual statement in Lemma 7.3.1, the second functor admits the pointwise \(I\)-indexed limit functor as a right adjoint, while the first admits \(\lim_J\) as a right adjoint. Their composite is therefore right adjoint to the constant \((I\times J)\)-diagram functor and computes \(\lim_J\lim_I\). Reversing \(I\) and \(J\) gives the other iterated limit. The displayed isomorphisms, including their naturality in \(F\), follow from uniqueness of right adjoints. The colimit statement is dual.
