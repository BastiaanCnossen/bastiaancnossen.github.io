Synthetic Category Theory · Chapter 15

Rezk nerves and univalent Segal animae

← Previous chapterChapter 14 → Next chapterChapter 16 ← Previous sectionSection 14.3 → Next sectionSection 15.1

A category \(C\) determines a simplicial anima \[ N(C)\colon\simp\catop\to\An,\qquad [n]\longmapsto\Map([n],C), \] called its Rezk nerve. Its first three terms record the objects, morphisms, and composition in \(C\). More generally, \(\Map([n],C)\) is the anima of strings of \(n\) composable morphisms, and the simplicial structure records their restrictions and composites. In this chapter we show that this information determines \(C\) completely, and characterize the simplicial animae that arise in this way.

The main result is that the Rezk nerve is fully faithful, with essential image the univalent Segal animae (Corollaries 15.1.13 and 15.5.5). The Segal condition expresses the existence and uniqueness of composition, while univalence identifies isomorphisms with identifications of objects. Together these conditions give a description of categories entirely in terms of animae and maps between them.

We first prove full faithfulness using an abstract nerve theorem, which recovers a category as a localization of its category of simplices. We then study Segal animae and their mapping animae. The final step is Rezk completion: every Segal anima has an associated category whose nerve has the same mapping animae, and every object of that category is isomorphic to one coming from the original Segal anima. This also identifies the category of small categories as the localization of small Segal animae at Dwyer–Kan equivalences (Corollary 15.5.7).

Definition 15.0.1 (Rezk nerve). Let \(i\colon \simp \hookrightarrow \Cat\) denote the inclusion. We define the Rezk nerve functor as the composite functor \[ N \colon \Cat \xrightarrow{h_{\Cat}} \PSh(\Cat) \xrightarrow{i^*} \PSh(\simp). \] By Theorem 12.8.1, this functor admits a left adjoint \[ \ac = Li_!\colon \PSh(\simp) \to \Cat \] satisfying \(\ac \Delta^n = \ac h_{\simp}([n]) = [n]\), functorially in \([n] \in \simp\). We refer to \(\ac\) as the associated category functor.
