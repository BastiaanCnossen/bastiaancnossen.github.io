Synthetic Category Theory · Chapter 14 · Section 14.2

Adjoint localizations and local objects

← Previous chapterChapter 13 → Next chapterChapter 15 ← Previous sectionSection 14.1 → Next sectionSection 14.3

There are two complementary directions. If a localization functor admits an adjoint, that adjoint is fully faithful. Conversely, a functor with a fully faithful adjoint is a Bousfield localization at the morphisms it inverts. Its essential image consists of the local objects. This description also explains how limits and colimits are computed in a reflective localization.

Proposition 14.2.1 (Adjoints of localizations). Let \(C\) be a category. If \(f\colon C\to D\) is a localization of \(C\) at a subcategory \(W\hookrightarrow C\) and admits a right (resp. left) adjoint \(g\colon D\to C\), then \(g\) is fully faithful.

Proof

We prove the case of right adjoints (the case of left adjoints is analogous, or follows from the case of right adjoints by applying the operator \((-)\catop\)). We choose a universe \(\Cat\) so that \(C\) and \(D\) are small. The universal property of a localization is invariant under passage to opposite categories, so \(f\catop\) is a localization at the opposite subcategory \(W\catop\). In particular, restriction \[ f^*\colon\PSh(D)\longrightarrow\PSh(C) \] is fully faithful. By Theorem 12.3.20, the adjunction \(f\dashv g\) gives an identification \[ f^*\iso g_!\, . \] Hence \(g_!\) is fully faithful. Applying Proposition 12.2.11 to \(g\catop\) shows that \(g\catop\), and therefore \(g\), is fully faithful.

Corollary 14.2.2 (Recognition of Bousfield localizations). Let \(f\colon C\to D\) have a fully faithful right adjoint \(g\colon D\to C\), and let \(W\) be the subcategory of morphisms inverted by \(f\). Then \(f\) is a localization at \(W\).

Proof

By Proposition 6.4.10, the adjunction exhibits \(g\) as a right adjoint section. Hence Proposition 4.4.16 shows that \(f\) is a localization at \(W\).

Under the hypotheses of the corollary, we call an object \(x\) of \(C\) \(W\)-local if the presheaf \(\Hom_C(-,x)\) sends every morphism in \(W\) to an isomorphism.

Proposition 14.2.3 (Local-object criterion). Let \(f\colon C\to D\) have a fully faithful right adjoint \(g\colon D\to C\), let \(W\) be the subcategory of morphisms inverted by \(f\), and let \(\eta\colon\id_C\to gf\) be the unit. An object \(x\) of \(C\) is \(W\)-local if and only if \(\eta_x\colon x\to gf(x)\) is an isomorphism. Consequently, \(g\) identifies \(D\) with the full subcategory of \(C\) spanned by the \(W\)-local objects.

Proof

By Corollary 14.2.2, the functor \(f\) is a localization at \(W\). Consider the adjunction \[ f_!\colon\PSh(C)\rightleftarrows \PSh(D)\colon f^*. \] Passing this localization to opposite categories shows that the right adjoint \(f^*\) is fully faithful, and its essential image consists of those presheaves on \(C\) that send every morphism in \(W\) to an isomorphism. Consequently, for a presheaf \(F\colon C\catop\to\An\), the unit \[ F\longrightarrow f^*f_!(F) \] is invertible precisely when \(F\) sends every morphism inverted by \(f\) to an isomorphism. For the representable presheaf \(F=\Hom_C(-,x)\), Proposition 12.3.18 and Theorem 12.3.20 identify this unit with the map \[ \Hom_C(-,x)\longrightarrow\Hom_D(f(-),f(x)) \iso\Hom_C(-,gf(x)) \] induced by the unit \(\eta_x\colon x\to gfx\). The full faithfulness of the Yoneda embedding, Proposition 12.3.10, therefore shows that the property that \(\Hom_C(-,x)\) sends every morphism inverted by \(f\) to an isomorphism is equivalent to the invertibility of \(\eta_x\colon x\to gfx\). Since the unit is invertible on every object in the image of \(g\), and every object on which the unit is invertible belongs to that image, the final assertion follows.

Proposition 14.2.4 (Limits and colimits in reflective localizations). Let \(f\colon C\to D\) be a functor with a fully faithful right adjoint \(g\colon D\to C\). Let \(I\) be a category and \(F\colon I\to D\) a functor. If \(c=\colim_{i:I}gF(i)\) exists in \(C\), then \[ \colim_{i:I}F(i)\simeq f(c). \] If \(\ell=\lim_{i:I}gF(i)\) exists in \(C\), then \(\ell\) is local with respect to the morphisms inverted by \(f\), and the unit \(\ell\to gf(\ell)\) is invertible. In particular, the limit of \(F\) exists and \[ g\left(\lim_{i:I}F(i)\right)\simeq\ell\, . \] Equivalently, \(f(\ell)\) is a limit of \(F\).

Proof

We may assume that there is a universe \(\Cat\) so that \(C\), \(D\) as well as \(I\) are all small. Assume first that the colimit \(c=\colim_{i:I}gF(i)\) exists in \(C\). For any object \(d\) of \(D\), the adjunction and the full faithfulness of \(g\) give \begin{align*} \Hom_D(f(c),d) &\simeq\Hom_C(c,g(d))\\ &\simeq\lim_{i:I\catop}\Hom_C(gF(i),g(d))\\ &\simeq\lim_{i:I\catop}\Hom_D(F(i),d). \end{align*} Thus \(f(c)\) is a colimit of \(F\).

Assume now that the limit \(\ell=\lim_{i:I}gF(i)\) exists in \(C\). For every \(i\), the presheaf \(\Hom_C(-,gF(i))\) sends the morphisms inverted by \(f\) to isomorphisms, by Proposition 14.2.3. The same is therefore true of its limit \[ \Hom_C(-,\ell)\simeq\lim_{i:I}\Hom_C(-,gF(i)). \] Another application of that remark shows that the unit at \(\ell\) is invertible, and hence gives an isomorphism \[ \ell\iso gf(\ell). \] Consequently, for every object \(d\) of \(D\), \begin{align*} \Hom_D(d,f(\ell)) &\simeq\Hom_C(g(d),gf(\ell))\\ &\simeq\Hom_C(g(d),\ell)\\ &\simeq\lim_{i:I}\Hom_C(g(d),gF(i))\\ &\simeq\lim_{i:I}\Hom_D(d,F(i)). \end{align*} Thus \(f(\ell)\) is a limit of \(F\).

Corollary 14.2.5 (Existence of limits and colimits). Let \(f\colon C\to D\) be a functor with a fully faithful right adjoint \(g\colon D\to C\). Let \(I\) be a category. If \(C\) admits \(I\)-indexed colimits, then so does \(D\). If \(C\) admits \(I\)-indexed limits, then so does \(D\).

Thus colimits are formed in the ambient category and then reflected, whereas limits of local objects are already local. We now turn from such formal consequences of localization to an explicit description of its mapping animae.
