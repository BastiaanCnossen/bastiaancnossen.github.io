Synthetic Category Theory · Chapter 5 · Section 5.2

Dependent products and sums

← Previous chapterChapter 4 → Next chapterChapter 6 ← Previous sectionSection 5.1 → Next sectionSection 5.3

Fix an anima \(A\). We introduce two constructions that assign an absolute category to every category in context \(A\): the dependent product and the dependent sum. We start with the former.

Axiom K.4 (Dependent product). For a category \(B\) in context \(A\), there is an absolute category \(\Pi_AB\), equipped with an evaluation functor \[ \varepsilon_B\colon W_A(\Pi_AB)\longrightarrow B \] in context \(A\). For an absolute functor \(g\colon T\to\Pi_AB\), define \(g^u:=\varepsilon_B\circ W_Ag\). We require:

  1. (1) For every absolute category \(T\) and functor \(f\colon W_AT\to B\) in context \(A\), there is an absolute functor \(f_c\colon T\to\Pi_AB\), equipped with an identification \(f=(f_c)^u\) in context \(A\).

  2. (2) If \(B\) is an anima in context \(A\), then \(\Pi_AB\) is an anima.

  3. (3) For any \(h,k\colon T\to\Pi_AB\), the functor \[ (h=k)\longrightarrow\Pi_A(h^u=k^u)_A \] is an equivalence. This functor is obtained by currying the functor \(W_A(h=k)\to(h^u=k^u)_A\) induced by weakening and postwhiskering with evaluation.

We call \(\Pi_AB\) the dependent product, or category of sections, of \(B\) over \(A\). We also write \(\prod_{a:A}B(a)\). Its absolute objects correspond to functors \(1_A\to B\) in context \(A\), using the equivalence \(W_A(*)\simeq1_A\). The second clause guarantees that both sides in the last clause are animae in the absolute context.

Together, the first and third clause specify a currying/uncurrying correspondence between functors \(W_AT \to B\) in context \(A\) and absolute functors \(T \to \Pi_AB\). Semantically, we think of \(\Pi_A\) as a right adjoint to the weakening operation \(W_A\).

Construction 5.2.1 (Functoriality of dependent products). A functor \(u\colon B\to C\) in context \(A\) gives a functor \[ \Pi_Au\colon\Pi_AB\to\Pi_AC \] by currying the composite \(W_A(\Pi_AB)\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_B}B\xrightarrow{u}C\). By construction, there is a commutative square The uniqueness clause of currying shows that this construction is compatible with identities and composites. Moreover, identifications \(\alpha\colon f = g\) between functors in context \(A\) induce identifications \(\Pi_A \alpha \colon \Pi_A f = \Pi_A g\) between their dependent products.

Lemma 5.2.2. Dependent products preserve terminal categories, products, and pullbacks.

Proof

Consider \(B\to D\leftarrow C\) in context \(A\). Using the projections, we obtain a functor \[ \Pi_A(B\times_D C)\longrightarrow \Pi_AB\times_{\Pi_AD}\Pi_AC. \] To construct an inverse, write \(P := \Pi_AB\times_{\Pi_AD}\Pi_AC\). We first construct a functor \(W_AP\to B\times_D C\). The projections of \(P\) induce functors \(W_AP \to W_A\Pi_AB\) and \(W_AP \to W_A\Pi_AC\), and composing them with the evaluations give functors \(W_AP\to B\) and \(W_AP\to C\). Their composites to \(D\) are identified by the defining identification of \(P\) and the compatibility identifications of evaluation. This results in a functor \(W_AP \to B \times_D C\), which in turn curries to a functor \(P \to \Pi_A(B \times_D C)\). The fact that this is an inverse follows similarly by uncurrying and using the respective pullback properties; we leave the details to the interested reader. The proofs for products and the terminal category are the same, with the corresponding universal properties.

We now introduce the dependent sum, or ‘total category’, of a contextual category. Its universal property is dual to that of the dependent product.

Axiom K.5 (Dependent sum). For a category \(B\) in context \(A\), there is an absolute category \(\Sigma_AB\), equipped with a pair functor \[ \eta_B\colon B\longrightarrow W_A(\Sigma_AB) \] in context \(A\). For an absolute functor \(k\colon\Sigma_AB\to D\), define \(k^\flat:=W_Ak\circ\eta_B\).

  1. (1) For every absolute category \(D\) and functor \(f\colon B\to W_AD\) in context \(A\), there is an absolute functor \(\bar f\colon\Sigma_AB\to D\), equipped with an identification \(f=(\bar f)^\flat\) in context \(A\).

  2. (2) For functors \(k,h\colon\Sigma_AB\to D\), the functor \[ (k=h)\longrightarrow\Pi_A(k^\flat=h^\flat)_A \] is an equivalence. It is obtained by currying the functor on identification animae induced by weakening and prewhiskering with \(\eta_B\).

We also write \(\sum_{a:A}B(a)\) for \(\Sigma_AB\) and \((a,-)\) for \(\eta_B\). The two universal properties express that \(\Sigma_A\) is left adjoint to weakening.

Construction 5.2.3 (Functoriality of dependent sums). For a functor \(u\colon B\to C\) in context \(A\), applying the sum axiom to \(\eta_C\circ u\) gives a functor \(\Sigma_Au\colon\Sigma_AB\to\Sigma_AC\) and a commutative square The functors \(\Sigma_Au\) are compatible with identities, composites, and identifications. For an absolute category \(D\), extending \(\id_{W_AD}\) gives a functor \[ d_D\colon\Sigma_AW_AD\longrightarrow D \] equipped with an identification \(W_Ad_D\circ\eta_{W_AD}=\id_{W_AD}\).

Proposition 5.2.4 (Mapping animae for dependent products and sums). For absolute categories \(T,D\) and a category \(B\) in context \(A\), evaluation and restriction along the pair functor induce equivalences of absolute animae \[ \Map(T,\Pi_AB)\simeq\Pi_A\Map_A(W_AT,B), \qquad \Map(\Sigma_AB,D)\simeq\Pi_A\Map_A(B,W_AD). \]

Proof

For the first map, weaken the evaluation functor \(\Map(T,\Pi_AB)\times T\to\Pi_AB\) and compose with \(\varepsilon_B\) to obtain a functor \(W_A\Map(T,\Pi_AB) \times W_AT \to B\). Currying first in the variable \(W_AT\) and then in the \(A\)-variable produces the functor. Conversely, if we denote \(Q := \Pi_A\Map_A(W_AT,B)\), then evaluation provides a functor \(W_A(Q \times T) \simeq W_AQ\times W_AT\to B\). Currying along \(A\) gives \(Q\times T\to\Pi_AB\), which in turn curries to \(Q\to\Map(T,\Pi_AB)\).

For the second map, we weaken the evaluation functor \(\Map(\Sigma_AB,D)\times\Sigma_AB\to D\) and restrict its second input along \(\eta_B\) to obtain \(W_A\Map(\Sigma_AB,D) \times B \to W_AD\). Currying in \(B\) and then along \(A\) produces the functor. For its inverse, write \(Q := \Pi_A\Map_A(B,W_AD)\). Evaluation provides a functor \(W_AQ\times B\longrightarrow W_AD\). Currying in \(W_AQ\) and using preservation of functor categories gives \[ B\longrightarrow\Fun_A(W_AQ,W_AD)\simeq W_A\Fun(Q,D), \] and the axiom for dependent sums extends this to \(\Sigma_AB\to\Fun(Q,D)\). Uncurrying gives \(\Sigma_AB \times Q \to D\), and then currying in \(\Sigma_AB\), with anima parameter \(Q\), gives \(Q\to\Map(\Sigma_AB,D)\).

In each case, the computation identifications for these successive currying operations identify the two composites with the identity after evaluation, which suffices to obtain the identifications at the level of the displayed mapping animae.

Proposition 5.2.5. For a category \(B\) in context \(A\) and an absolute category \(C\), the functor induced by taking the sum of the first projection and extending the second projection is an equivalence \[ \Sigma_A(B\times W_AC)\iso\Sigma_AB\times C. \]

Proof

For every absolute category \(D\), the preceding proposition, currying, and preservation of functor categories give a chain of equivalences \begin{align*} \Map(\Sigma_A(B\times W_AC),D) &\simeq\Pi_A\Map_A(B\times W_AC,W_AD)\\ &\simeq\Pi_A\Map_A(B,\Fun_A(W_AC,W_AD))\\ &\simeq\Pi_A\Map_A(B,W_A\Fun(C,D))\\ &\simeq\Map(\Sigma_AB,\Fun(C,D))\\ &\simeq\Map(\Sigma_AB\times C,D). \end{align*} The composite equivalence can be identified with precomposition with the functor in the proposition statement. We may now obtain an inverse to the given functor by taking \(D\) to be its source and its target, respectively.

Remark 5.2.6 (Compatibility with change of theory). Let \(S\) and \(T\) be two instances of the abstract theory, and let \(W\colon S \to T\) be a change of theory, as detailed in Section 5.1.1. Considering an anima \(A\) in theory \(S\), we then obtain a theory \(S^A\) of categories of \(S\) in context \(A\), and a theory \(T^{WA}\) of categories of \(T\) in context \(WA\).

We assume that \(W\) comes equipped with a change of theory \(W^{[A]}\) from \(S^A\) to \(T^{WA}\). This change of theory is compatible with weakening, in that we have \(W^{[A]}(W_AC) \iso W_{WA}(WC)\) for every category \(C\) in \(S\), compatibly with functors and specified identifications. Moreover, we require compatibility with dependent products and sums: the canonical functors \[ W(\Pi_AB)\to\Pi_{WA}(W^{[A]}B), \qquad \Sigma_{WA}(W^{[A]}B) \to W(\Sigma_AB) \] are required to be equivalences, respecting the evaluation and pair functors and their specified identifications. Since \(W^{[A]}\) is itself a change of theory, the same requirements apply in further anima contexts.
