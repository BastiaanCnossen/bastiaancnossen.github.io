Synthetic Category Theory · Chapter 21 · Section 21.1

Accessibility axiom

← Previous chapterChapter 20 ← Previous sectionSection 20.8 → Next sectionSection 21.2

Definition 21.1.1. A universe \(\Cat\) is accessible if, for any small anima \(\Gamma\) and any family of small categories indexed by \(\Gamma\) \[ x\colon\Gamma\mapsto C_x\colon\Cat \] there is an elementary small subuniverse \(\kappa\) in \(\Cat\) such that each \(C_x\) belongs to \(\kappa\).

We formulate now an extra axiom for synthetic category theory.

Axiom O (accessibility axiom). Any universe is contained in an accessible universe.

Remark 21.1.2. Many theorems of synthetic category theory do not need this axiom. We could compare it as a weak form of the axiom of choice in set theory (it is a good replacement of transfinite induction). The comparison holds in many ways: using the accessibility axiom is arguably less constructive then the others. Moreover, we will see later that the ordinary axiom of choice implies the accessibility axiom (this explains why the theory of accessible categories and the theory of presentable categories are formulated in classical category theory through the language of cardinals). However, the accessibility axiom is much more robust than the axiom of choice. More precisely, it allows to develop the theory of presentable categories in full, then the theory of Grothendieck-Lurie topoi. For each topos \(X\), we will see that univalent Segal objects of \(X\) yield a semantic interpretation of synthetic category theory in which the accessibility axiom holds as well.

From now on, we fix an accessible universe \(\Cat\), the objects of which will be called small categories and a larger universe \(\Cat'\), whose objects will be called large categories, such that \(\Cat\) is large and any small category is large.

Remark 21.1.3. We can see \(\Cat\) as an elementary subuniverse of \(\Cat'\). Indeed, for any elementary subuniverse \(\kappa\) in \(\Cat\), we can see \(\kappa\) as an elementary subuniverse of \(\Cat'\). Therefore, any (large) category with \(\Cat\)-small colimits having \(\kappa\)-small ones, it is \(\kappa\)-filtered for any elementary subuniverse \(\kappa\) of \(\Cat\). This implies that such a category is \(\Cat\)-filtered. Since \(\N\) is small, any such category is idempotent complete. We can thus identify the category of large categories with small colimits with the one of \(\Cat\)-presentable categories. This means that we can see the category of large categories with small filtered colimits (and small colimits preserving functors between them) as a (very large) \(\Cat\)-presentable category – this is a particular case of Theorem 17.2.24 with \(D=\Cat'\) and \(\kappa=\Cat\). If \(\An'\) denotes the category of large animae, we can thus see large categories as those presheaves of the form \[ \Cat\catop\to\An' \] that preserve small limits. A large category is \(\Cat\)-compact if and only if it is small.

We denote by \(\CAT\) the category of large categories with small colimits and small colimits preserving functors between them.
