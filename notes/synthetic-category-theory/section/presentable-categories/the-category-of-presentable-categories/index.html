Synthetic Category Theory · Chapter 21 · Section 21.2

The category of presentable categories

← Previous chapterChapter 20 ← Previous sectionSection 21.1

Definition 21.2.1. A (large) category \(C\) is presentable if there exists an elementary subuniverse \(\kappa\) in \(\Cat\) such that \(C\) is \(\kappa\)-presentable.

We define the category \(\PrL\) as the full subcategory of \(\CAT\) whose objects are the presentable categories, with morphisms the functors that preserve small colimits.

Lemma 21.2.2. Let \(\kappa\) be an elementary subuniverse of \(\Cat\) and \(C\) a \(\kappa\)-presentable category. For any elementary subuniverse \(\lambda\) in \(\Cat\) containing \(\kappa\) (i.e. such that any strictly \(\kappa\)-small category if \(\lambda\)-small), the category \(C\) is \(\lambda\)-small. Furthermore, given any small family \(F\) of objects of \(C\), there exists an elementary subuniverse of \(\Cat\) such that all objects in \(S\) are \(\lambda\)-small.

Proof

The first assertion is obvious. We can choose an elementary universe \(\lambda\) such that each \(C^\kappa_{/x}\) for \(x\colon F\) is \(\lambda\)-small. Then each object \(x\) in \(F\) is \(\lambda\)-compact.

Definition 21.2.3. Let \(C\) be a locally small category and \(S\) a family of morphisms in \(C\). We denote by \(L_S(C)\) the full subcategory of \(S\)-local objects of \(C\), i.e. of those objects \(x\colon C^\simeq\) such that the presheaf \[ \Hom_C(-,x)\colon C\catop\to\An \] takes any term of \(S\) to an isomorphism of \(\An\). We say that the left Bousfield localization of \(C\) exists if the full embedding \[ L_S(C)\hookrightarrow C \] has a left adjoint.

Example 21.2.4. If \(C\) has small colimits and if \(S\) is a small family of maps between compact objects, then the left Bousfield localization exists, by Theorem 18.1.6.

Proposition 21.2.5. Let \(\kappa\) be a localizing subunivserse of \(\Cat\). If \(C\) is \(\kappa\)-presentable, then, for any small family \(S\) of morphisms of \(C\) between \(\kappa\)-small objects, the left Bousfield localization exists. Furthermore, the category \(L_S(C)\) of \(S\)-local objects is \(\kappa\)-presentable and the full embedding \[ L_S(C)\hookrightarrow C \] is a right adjoint that commutes with \(\kappa\)-filtered colimits.

Proof

Let \(C^\kappa\) be the full subcategory of \(\kappa\)-compact objects of \(C\). We may see \(S\) as a small family of morphisms in \(C^\kappa\), hence as a functor \[ \coprod_{f\colon S}\Delta^1\to C^\kappa\, . \] Recall that, for any small category \(C\), there is the free small category with \(\kappa\)-small colimits \(\langle C\rangle_\kappa\) associated to \(C\) by Theorem 17.3.3. Furthermore, since the subuniverse \(\kappa\) is localizing, the category \(\RexCat^\kappa\) of small categories with \(\kappa\)-small colimits has small colimits by Proposition 17.3.8.

The family of maps \(S\) thus defines functor preserving \(\kappa\)-small colimits of the form \[ \Big\langle \coprod_{f\colon S}\Delta^1\Big\rangle_\kappa\to C^\kappa\, . \] We can then form the pushout in \(\RexCat^\kappa\) of the form bellow. This commutative square remains a pushout in the category of large categories with \(\kappa\)-small colimits. We will prove that \[ L_S(C)=\Ind_\kappa(S^{-1}C^\kappa)\, . \] The functor \(\gamma\) induces an adjunction \[ \gamma_!\colon\PSh(C^\kappa)\rightleftarrows\PSh(S^{-1}C^\kappa)\colon \gamma^* \] with \(\gamma^*\) fully faithful that restrict to an adjunction of the form \[ \gamma_!\colon\Ind_\kappa(C^\kappa)\rightleftarrows\Ind_\kappa(S^{-1}C^\kappa)\colon \gamma^* \] by Proposition 17.2.21. Moreover, we have \[ \Ind_\kappa(C^\kappa)=C \] by Theorem 17.2.24 and Corollary 17.4.5. By construction, the full subcategory \(\Ind_\kappa(S^{-1}C^\kappa)\) is contained in the full subcategory \(L_S(C)\) of \(S\)-local objects. Conversely, any \(S\)-local object belongs to the essential image of \(\gamma^*\). Indeed, since \(C^\kappa\hookrightarrow C\) preserves \(\kappa\)-small colimits, given an \(S\)-local object \(x\) in \(C\), the restricted Hom functor \[ \Phi:=\Hom_C(-,x)\catop_{|_{C^\kappa}}\colon C^\kappa\to\An\catop \] preserves \(\kappa\)-small colimits and sends maps of \(S\) to isomorphisms, hence determines a commutative square of the form and thus a unique functor \[ \Psi\colon S^{-1}C^\kappa\to\An\catop \] such that \(\gamma\circ\Psi=\Phi\). In other words, if \(F=\Psi\catop\), we see that \(x\) is isomorphic to \(\gamma^*(F)\) through the identification of \(C\) with \(\Ind_\kappa(C^\kappa)\). We have thus proved that \[ \Ind_\kappa(S^{-1}C^\kappa)=L_S(C) \] so that the embedding \(L_S(C)\hookrightarrow C\) corresponds to \(\gamma^*\). The left adjoint of \(\gamma^*\) thus proves that the left Bousfield localization of \(C\) by \(S\) exists. The fact that \(S\)-local objects are stable under \(\kappa\)-filtered colimits can be checked directly or can be seen as a particular case of the last assertion of Proposition 17.2.21. Finally, Remark 17.2.27 proves that \(L_S(C)\) is \(\kappa\)-presentable.

Remark 21.2.6. The proof of the preceding proposition does not use the accessibility axiom. However, the latter is used to obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 21.2.7. If \(C\) is presentable, then, for any small family of maps \(S\) in \(C\), the left Bousfield localization of \(C\) by \(S\) exists. Furthermore, the category \(L_S(C)\) of \(S\)-local objects is presentable.

Proof

We may assume that there is an elementary subuniverse \(\kappa\) in \(\Cat\) such that \(C\) is \(\kappa\)-presentable (more precisely, it suffices to prove the corollary in the context whose terms are the elementary subuniverses \(\kappa\) such that \(C\) is \(\kappa\)-presentable). We may choose \(\kappa\) such that \(S\) consists of maps between \(\kappa\)-small objects by Lemma 21.2.2. We can now apply Proposition 21.2.5 directly and this achieves the proof.

Lemma 21.2.8. For any presentable category \(C\), there is a small category \(A\) and a small family of maps \(S\) in \(\PSh(A)\) such that \(C\) is the left Bousfield localization of \(\PSh(A)\) by \(S\).

Proof

Let \(\kappa\) be an elementary subuniverse of \(\Cat\) such that \[C=\Ind_\kappa(C^\kappa)\, .\] We define \(A=C^\kappa\) and let \(S\) be the family of maps obtained throught the following procedure. Given any strictly \(\kappa\)-small category \(E\) and any functor \[ F\colon E\to A \] we consider the comparison map \[ \colim\, h\circ F\to h(\colim \, F) \] where \(h\colon C^\kappa\hookrightarrow\PSh(A)\) is the Yoneda embedding. A direct application of the Yoneda lemma shows that the \(S\)-local objects precisely are the functors from \(A\catop\) to \(\An\) that preserve \(\kappa\)-small limits. In other words, we have \[ L_S(\PSh(A))=\Ind_\kappa(C^\kappa) \] by Corollary 17.2.20.

Lemma 21.2.9. The functor \[ \Cat\to\CAT\ , \quad A\mapsto\PSh(A) \] commutes with small colimits and takes values in \(\PrL\).

Proof

The forgetful functor \[ \CAT\hookrightarrow\Cat' \] has a left adjoint \[ C\mapsto\langle C\rangle_\Cat \] by Corollary 17.2.35 for \(\kappa=\Cat\). In particular, the latter preserves small colimits. On the other hand, for \(C\) small, Corollary 12.8.5 tells us that \[ \langle C\rangle_\Cat=\PSh(C)\, . \] Since the embedding \(\Cat\hookrightarrow\Cat'\) preserves small colimits, and since small colimits preserving functors are stable under composition, this proves the lemma.

Lemma 21.2.10. For any elementary subuniverse \(\kappa\) and finite poset \(P\), the category \(\Fun(P\catop,\An^\kappa)\) agrees with the category of \(\kappa\)-compact objects of \(\PSh(P)\) and we habe \[ \langle P\rangle_\Cat=\Fun(P\catop,\An^\kappa)\, . \]

Proof

The first assertion is an easy consequence of Lemma 18.6.12. The second assertion follows from the first and from the very construction of \(\langle P\rangle_\Cat\).

Lemma 21.2.11. The category \(\PSh(A)\) is \(\Cat\)-compact in \(\CAT\) for any small category \(A\).

Proof

The forgetful functor \[ \CAT\hookrightarrow\Cat' \] commutes with \(\Cat\)-filtered colimits and any finite poset is compact in \(\Cat'\). Therefore, the previous lemma for \(\kappa=\Cat\) implies that \(\PSh(P)\) is a \(\Cat\)-compact object of \(\CAT\) for any finite poset \(P\). Since any small category is a small colimit of finite posets, Lemma 21.2.9 and Remark 17.2.23 achieve the proof.

Lemma 21.2.12. The category \(\PrL\) is stable under small colimits in \(\CAT\).

Proof

Let \(i\mapsto C_i\) a functor indexed by a small category \(I\) and taking values in \(\PrL\). For each \(i\colon I^\simeq\), there is an elementary subuniverse \(\kappa(i)\) in \(\Cat\) such that \(C_i\) is \(\kappa(i)\)-presentable. We may assume given an elementary subuniverse \(\kappa\) of \(\Cat\) such that \(I\) as well as each \((C_i)^{\kappa(i)}\) are \(\kappa\)-small. We may also choose \(\kappa\) so that any small category that is \(\kappa(i)\)-small for some \(i\) is \(\kappa\)-small. In this case, \(C_i\) is \(\kappa\)-presentable for all \(i\). Possibly after enlarging \(\kappa\), we may assume that the diagram \(i\mapsto C_i\) takes its values in \(\PrL_\kappa\). Let \(C\) be the colimit of the diagram \(i\mapsto C_i\) in \(\PrL_\kappa\). We may also assume that \(\N\) is \(\kappa\)-small, so that any category with \(\kappa\)-small colimits is idempotent-complete as well: \[ \RexCat_\kappa=\PrL_\kappa\, . \] Let \(\RexCat^\prime_\kappa\) be the category of large categories with \(\kappa\)-small colimits. The explicit description of colimits in \(\RexCat^\prime_\kappa\) given in the proof of Lemma 17.3.6 show that both embeddings \[ \RexCat_\kappa\hookrightarrow\RexCat^\prime_\kappa\quad\text{and}\quad \RexCat_\kappa^\prime\hookrightarrow\CAT \] commute with small colimits. Therefore \(C\) is the colimit of \(i\mapsto C_i\) in \(\CAT\).

Theorem 21.2.13. Let \(C\) be a (large) category. The following conditions are equivalent.

  • (i) The category \(C\) is presentable.

  • (ii) There is a small category \(A\) as well as a small family of maps \(S\) between presheaves on \(A\) with values in small animae and an equivalence between \(C\) and the left Bousfield localization of \(\PSh(A)\) by \(S\).

  • (iii) The category \(C\) is \(\Cat\)-compact as an object of \(\CAT\).

Proof

The equivalence between assertions (i) and (ii) follows from Corollary 21.2.7 and Lemma 21.2.8. Since \(\Cat\)-compact objects ar stable under small colimits, in particular under pushouts, it follows from Lemma 21.2.11 and Lemma 21.2.12 that any left Bousfield localization of a category of presheaves on a small category is \(\Cat\)-compact. Therefore, any presentable category is \(\Cat\)-compact. The full subcategory of \(\Cat\)-compact objects of the \(\Cat\)-presentable (very large) category \(\CAT\) is the smallest full subcategory containing \(\PSh([1])=\langle [1]\rangle_\Cat\) stable under small colimits. Therefore, Lemma 21.2.12 and Lemma 21.2.11 also imply that any \(\Cat\)-compact object of \(\CAT\) is a presentable category. This shows that assertions (ii) and (iii) are equivalent and thus ends the proof of the theorem.

Corollary 21.2.14. The category \(\PrL\) is the full subcategory of \(\Cat\)-compact objects in the \(\Cat\)-presentable category \(\CAT\) of large categories with small colimits and small colimits preserving functors between them. In particular, \(\PrL\) has small colimits and the embedding functor \[ \PrL\hookrightarrow\CAT \] commutes with them.

Corollary 21.2.15. Any large category with small colimits is a large \(\Cat\)-filtered colimit of presentable categories with cocontinuous transition maps.

Proposition 21.2.16. Let \(C\) be a presentable category and \(D\) a locally small category with small colimits. A functor \(\varphi\colon C\to D\) has a right adjoint if and only if it commutes with small colimits.

Proof

It is clear that \(\varphi\) commutes with colimits if it has a right adjoint. Conversely, let us assume that \(\varphi\) commutes with small colimits. By virtue of Theorem 11.2.13 and of Lemma 21.2.8 it suffices to prove the proposition in a context where we can choose a small category \(A\) and a small family of morphisms \(S\) in \(\PSh(A)\) such that \(C\) is the left Bousfield localization of the category of presheaves on \(A\) by \(S\). We identify \(C\) with the full subcategory of \(S\)-local objects in the category of presheaves on \(A\). We denote by \[ \gamma\colon\PSh(A)\to C \] be the left adjoint of the obvious full embedding. A particular case of Lemma 17.3.6 with \(\kappa=\Cat\) tells us that the functor \(f:=\varphi\circ\gamma\) commutes with small colimits. Therefore, by virtue of Corollary 12.8.5, the functor \(f\) has a right adjoint \(g\). We claim that the functor \(g\) takes values in \(S\)-local objects: for any map \(u\colon x\to y\) in \(S\) and any object \(z\) of \(D\) we have the map \[ u^*\colon\Hom(y,g(z))\to\Hom(x,g(z)) \] is isomorphic to the map \[ f(u)^*\colon\Hom(f(y),z)\to\Hom(f(x),z) \] hence is an isomorphism since \(f\) factorizes through the functor \(\gamma\) and thus sends terms of \(S\) to isomorphisms of \(C\). Therefore, there is a unique functor \[ \psi\colon D\to C \] whose composition with the full embedding \(i\colon C\hookrightarrow\PSh(A)\) yields \(g\). For \(x\) in \(C\) and \(y\) in \(D\) we thus have \begin{align*} \Hom(\varphi(x),y) &=\Hom(f(i(x)),y)\\ &=\Hom(i(x),g(y))\\ &=\Hom(i(x),i(\psi(y)))\\ &=\Hom(x,\psi(y)) \end{align*}
