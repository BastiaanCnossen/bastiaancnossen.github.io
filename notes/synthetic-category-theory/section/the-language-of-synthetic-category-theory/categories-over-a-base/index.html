Synthetic Category Theory · Chapter 1 · Section 1.9

Categories over a base

← Previous chapterOverview of the axioms → Next chapterChapter 2 ← Previous sectionSection 1.8 → Next sectionSection 1.10

We will frequently consider categories equipped with a functor to a fixed category \(S\). We introduce the corresponding notions of functors and natural transformations over \(S\).

Definition 1.9.1 (Categories and functors over a base). A category over \(S\) is a category \(C\) equipped with a functor \(p\colon C\to S\). A functor over \(S\) from \(p\colon C\to S\) to \(q\colon D\to S\) consists of a functor \(f\colon C\to D\) and a specified identification \(qf=p\). We may sometimes also refer to such a functor as a relative functor if we wish to leave the base implicit. We will generally display such relative functors as commutative triangles

Definition 1.9.2. Let \(S\) be a category and let \(p\colon C \to S\) and \(q\colon D \to S\) be categories over \(S\). We define the category \(\Fun_S(C,D)\) of relative functors over \(S\) as the following pullback: The objects of \(\Fun_S(C,D)\) are precisely the relative functors from \(C\) to \(D\) over \(S\).

We write \(\Map_S(C,D)\) for the groupoid core of \(\Fun_S(C,D)\), which fits into a pullback square of the form We may sometimes also write \(\Map_{/S}(C,D)\) for this anima.

A natural transformation over \(S\) is a morphism in \(\Fun_S(C,D)\). Given two relative functors \(f,g\colon C\to D\), with structural identifications \(\alpha:qf=p\) and \(\beta:qg=p\), such a relative natural transformation consists of a natural transformation \(\eta\colon f\to g\) together with the identification \[ \beta\circ(q\eta)=\alpha. \] Similarly, identifications over \(S\) are identifications in the anima \(\Map_S(C,D)\).

Definition 1.9.3. A functor over \(S\) is an equivalence over \(S\) if it admits an inverse over \(S\), with identifications of the two composites with the identity functors over \(S\).

Identity functors over \(S\) use the unitality of composition, while composition of functors over \(S\) uses the associativity of composition. Whiskering and composition of natural transformations likewise preserve their identifications over \(S\). Unitality and associativity of functor composition of \(S\) is a consequence of the triangle and pentagon laws for composition of absolute functors. In particular, note that by passing to categories over \(S\), we loose some amount of coherences.

Given a functor \(p\colon S\to T\) and a category \(C\to T\), its base change along \(p\) is the pullback \(p^*C=C\times_T S\to S\). This operation can be extended to functors and natural transformations over \(T\):

Construction 1.9.4. Let \(p\colon S \to T\) be a functor. For categories \(C\) and \(D\) over \(T\), we obtain an induced functor \[ p^*\colon \Fun_T(C,D) \to \Fun_{S}(C \times_T S, D \times_T S) \] by applying pullback universality to a functor \(h\colon C\to D\) together with its specified identification over \(T\). This gives a functor \(h\times_T S\colon C\times_T S\to D\times_T S\) over \(S\). Applying the same construction to the universal functor parameterized by \(\Fun_T(C,D)\) and currying gives the displayed functor, including its action on natural transformations and their identifications.

The category \(S\xrightarrow{\id_S}S\) is terminal among categories over \(S\), in the sense that we have \[ \Fun_S(C,S) \iso * \] for every category \(C\) over \(S\). Products over \(S\) are computed by the corresponding absolute pullbacks: for categories \(C,D\) over \(S\) and any \(X\to S\), the universal property of pullbacks implies that the two projections induce an equivalence \[ \Fun_S(X,C\times_S D) \iso \Fun_S(X,C)\times\Fun_S(X,D). \] Indeed, a term on either side consists of two functors out of \(X\) into \(C\) and \(D\) with their specified identifications to its structural functor \(X\to S\); the same pullback description applies to natural transformations and their identifications.
