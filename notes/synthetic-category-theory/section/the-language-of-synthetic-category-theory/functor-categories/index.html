Functor categories

← Previous chapterOverview of the axioms → Next chapterChapter 2 ← Previous sectionSection 1.6 → Next sectionSection 1.8

Given two categories \(C\) and \(D\), we are interested in forming the functor category \(\Fun(C,D)\) whose objects are precisely the functors from \(C\) to \(D\).

Given any functor \(e \colon F \times C\to D\) and any category \(T\), we can form a functor \[ \mathit{u}_T\colon \Map(T,F)\to\Map(T\times C,D) \] by currying the composite \[ \Map(T,F)\times T\times C \xrightarrow{\ev_{\Map} \times \id_C} F\times C\xrightarrow{e} D. \] It satisfies the property that it evaluations on an absolute object of \(\Map(T,F)\), corresponding to a functor \(g\colon T\to F\), as \[ \mathit{u}_T(g)= e\circ(g\times\id_C). \]

Definition 1.7.1. A functor category from \(C\) to \(D\) is a category \(F\) equipped with a functor \(e\colon F\times C\to D\) such that, for any category \(T\), the induced map \[ \mathit{u}_T\colon \Map(T,F)\iso\Map(T\times C,D) \] is an equivalence. We then call \(F\) the category of functors from \(C\) to \(D\) and \(e\) the evaluation functor.

Proposition 1.7.2. If \(F\) and \(F'\) both are categories of functors from \(C\) to \(D\) with evaluation functors \(e\) and \(e'\) respectively, then there is an equivalence \(\varphi\colon F'\iso F\) as well as \(\sigma\colon e\circ (\varphi\times\id_C)= e'\).

Proof

Let \(v\) be an inverse of the equivalence \(u_{F'}\colon\Map(F',F)\iso\Map(F'\times C,D)\) and define \(\varphi:=v(e')\colon F'\to F\). By Construction 1.4.1, the identification \(u_{F'}\circ v=\id\) and the beta comparison for \(u_{F'}\) provide \[ \sigma\colon e\circ(\varphi\times\id_C)= e'. \] Exchanging the roles of \((F,e)\) and \((F',e')\), we similarly obtain a functor \(\psi\colon F\to F'\) together with \(e'\circ(\psi\times\id_C)= e\). Combining the two, the uncurried functors associated to \(\varphi\circ\psi\) and \(\id_F\) are identified: \[ e\circ((\varphi\circ\psi)\times\id_C) = e'\circ(\psi\times\id_C) = e = e\circ(\id_F\times\id_C). \] Since \(u_F\) is an equivalence, it reflects this identification and gives \(\varphi\circ\psi=\id_F\). The same argument shows \(\psi\circ\varphi=\id_{F'}\), so \(\varphi\) is an equivalence with inverse \(\psi\).

Axiom B.8 (Functor category). For any two categories \(C\) and \(D\), there is a category \(\Fun(C, D)\) equipped with an evaluation functor \(\ev\colon \Fun(C,D) \times C \to D\) that exhibits it as a functor category from \(C\) to \(D\).

Definition 1.7.3. Given another category \(T\), choose an inverse \(v_T\) of the equivalence \(\mathit{u}_T\). For a functor \(f\colon T\times C\to D\), define its currying by applying \(v_T\): \[ f_c:=v_T(f)\colon T\longrightarrow\Fun(C,D). \] It comes equipped with a commutative diagram as follows:

Conversely, given a functor \(g\colon T \to \Fun(C,D)\), we call the composite \[ T \times C \xrightarrow{g \times \id_C} \Fun(C,D) \times C \xrightarrow{\ev} D \] the uncurrying of \(g\) and denote it by \(g^u\). The discussion before shows it is identified with \(\mathit{u}_T(g)\). Similarly, an identification \(\beta\colon g= h\) induces an identification \(\beta^u\colon g^u= h^u\). At the level of mapping animae, uncurrying is the equivalence \[ (-)^u \colon \Map (T,\Fun(C,D))\iso\Map(T\times C,D) \] with inverse defined by currying.

For parallel functors \(g,h\colon T\to\Fun(C,D)\), the induced functor \[ (g= h)\longrightarrow(g^u= h^u) \] is an equivalence. Indeed, represent \(g,h\) by points of \(\Map(T,\Fun(C,D))\), apply Lemma 1.3.10 to \(\mathit{u}_T\), and use Construction 1.4.1 at both ends.

Notation 1.7.4. In term notation, an expression \(x:T,y:C\vdash f(x,y):D\) may therefore be curried to \(x:T\vdash(y\mapsto f(x,y)):\Fun(C,D)\). Conversely, evaluation of \(x:T\vdash F(x):\Fun(C,D)\) and \(x:T\vdash c(x):C\) is formed by pairing, giving \(x:T\vdash F(x)(c(x)):D\). These conventions also apply to identifications of the whole functors, with the beta and eta comparisons above.

Remark 1.7.5. By considering the terminal category, we observe that absolute objects \(*\to\Fun(C,D)\) correspond to functors \(C\simeq *\times C\to D\), justifying the notation and terminology of \(\Fun(C,D)\).

Construction 1.7.6 (Evaluation functor). Given categories \(C\) and \(D\), and \(x \colon *\to C\), the evaluation functor at \(x\) is defined as the composite \[ \ev_x\colon \Fun(C,D) \simeq \Fun(C,D) \times * \xrightarrow{\id \times x} \Fun(C,D) \times C \xrightarrow{\ev} D. \]

The functoriality of the product naturally provides functoriality for the functor categories:

Given a functor \(g\colon D \to D'\), we define the functor \(g \circ -\colon \Fun(C,D) \to \Fun(C,D')\) as the currying of the composite \[ \Fun(C, D) \times C \xrightarrow{\ev} D \xrightarrow{g} D'. \] By assumption we obtain a commutative square as follows: Similarly, given a functor \(f\colon C' \to C\), we define the functor \(- \circ f\colon \Fun(C,D) \to \Fun(C',D)\) as the currying of the composite \[ \Fun(C,D) \times C' \xrightarrow{\id \times f} \Fun(C,D) \times C \xrightarrow{\ev} D. \] It satisfies the property that it makes the following square commute: In a completely analogous way, every identification \(\beta\colon g = g'\) of functors \(D \to D'\) induces an identification \((\beta \circ -) \colon (g \circ -) = (g' \circ -)\) of functors \(\Fun(C,D) \to \Fun(C, D')\), and similarly for the construction \(- \circ f\).

Exercise 1.7.8 (Exercise 1.10.6). Formulate and prove that the assignments \(g \mapsto (g \circ -)\) and \(f \mapsto (- \circ f)\) are functorial, in the sense that they respect identity functors and composition of functors.

1.7.1 Properties of functor categories

The universal property of functor categories allows us to deduce their compatibilities with the terminal category, the initial category, products, coproducts and pullbacks from the corresponding properties of mapping animae. We will use Proposition 1.4.11 to test the comparison functors after applying \(\Map(T,-)\).

Lemma 1.7.9. For every category \(C\) the functor category \(\Fun(C,*)\) is contractible.

Proof

For every category \(T\), the universal property of the functor category gives \[ \Map(T,\Fun(C,*))\simeq\Map(T\times C,*)\simeq *, \] where the last equivalence is Lemma 1.4.4. Thus \(\Fun(C,*)\to *\) induces an equivalence on mapping animae for every \(T\), and is an equivalence by Proposition 1.4.11.

Lemma 1.7.10. Let \(C\), \(D\) and \(E\) be categories. Then the functor \[ (\pr_1 \circ \blank, \pr_2 \circ \blank)\colon \Fun(C, D \times E) \to \Fun(C, D) \times \Fun(C,E) \] is an equivalence.

Proof

For every category \(T\), the universal property of functor categories and Proposition 1.4.5 give equivalences \[ \begin{aligned} \Map(T,\Fun(C,D\times E)) &\simeq\Map(T\times C,D\times E)\\ &\simeq\Map(T\times C,D)\times\Map(T\times C,E)\\ &\simeq\Map(T,\Fun(C,D))\times\Map(T,\Fun(C,E))\\ &\simeq\Map(T,\Fun(C,D)\times\Fun(C,E)). \end{aligned} \] This composite is induced by the functor in the statement: after uncurrying, its two components are postcomposition with \(\pr_1\) and \(\pr_2\), by Lemma 1.7.7. The result follows from Proposition 1.4.11.

Lemma 1.7.11. Let \(T\) be a category and let \(f\colon C \to E\) and \(g\colon D \to E\) be functors. Then the functor \[ (\pr_1 \circ \blank, \pr_2 \circ \blank)\colon \Fun(T,C \times_{E} D) \to \Fun(T,C) \times_{\Fun(T,E)} \Fun(T,D) \] induced by the identification \((f \circ \blank) \circ (\pr_1 \circ \blank) = (g \circ \blank) \circ (\pr_2 \circ \blank)\) is an equivalence.

Proof

By Proposition 1.6.3, it suffices to show that the pullback square defining \(C \times_E D\) remains a pullback square after applying \(\Map(S,\Fun(T,-))\) for every category \(S\). Under uncurrying, its four corners are identified with \[ \Map(S\times T,C\times_E D),\quad \Map(S\times T,C),\quad \Map(S\times T,D),\quad \Map(S\times T,E). \] By Lemma 1.7.7, the four functors become postcomposition with the original projections, \(f\), and \(g\). These comparisons respect the specified commutativity identification, as follows by uncurrying its defining postcomposition identification. We conclude that this square agrees with the one obtained by applying \(\Map(S \times T, -)\) to the original pullback square, including its commutativity data. So the resulting square is a pullback by Proposition 1.6.3.

Lemma 1.7.12. For categories \(C\), \(D\) and \(E\), there is a preferred equivalence \[ \Fun(C,\Fun(D,E)) \iso \Fun(C \times D, E)\, . \]

Proof

We define the preferred functor as the curried functor of the following composite: \[ \Fun(C,\Fun(D,E)) \times C \times D \xrightarrow{\ev \times \id_{D}} \Fun(D,E) \times D \xrightarrow{\ev} E. \] To construct an inverse, it will by currying suffice to construct a functor \(\Fun(C \times D,E) \times C \to \Fun(D,E)\), which we may take to be the curried functor of the following composite \[ \Fun(C \times D,E) \times C \times D \xrightarrow{\ev} E. \] By construction, these two functors are compatible with the chain of equivalences \begin{align*} \Map(T,\Fun(C,\Fun(D,E))) &\simeq \Map(T\times C,\Fun(D,E))\\ &\simeq \Map((T\times C)\times D,E)\\ &\simeq \Map(T\times (C\times D),E)\\ &\simeq \Map(T,\Fun(C\times D,E))\, . \end{align*} This shows that applying \(\Map(T,-)\) to the preferred functor is an equivalence for all \(T\), hence that the preferred functor is an equivalence.

Lemma 1.7.13. For a category \(C\), the functor \(\ev_{*}\colon \Fun(*,C) \to C\) is an equivalence.

Proof

We leave it to the reader to verify that an inverse is given by currying the equivalence \(C \times * \iso C\).

Lemma 1.7.14. For a category \(C\), the functor category \(\Fun(\emptyset,C)\) is contractible, i.e. \(\Fun(\emptyset,C) \iso *\).

Proof

For every category \(T\), there are equivalences \[ \Map(T,\Fun(\emptyset,C)) \simeq\Map(T\times\emptyset,C) \simeq\Map(\emptyset,C) \simeq *, \] using Lemma 1.5.1 and the initial-category axiom. Hence \(\Fun(\emptyset,C)\to *\) induces an equivalence on mapping animae for every \(T\), and Proposition 1.4.11 applies.

Lemma 1.7.15. For categories \(C\), \(D\) and \(E\), restriction along the inclusions \(i_C\colon C \hookrightarrow C \sqcup D\) and \(i_D\colon D \hookrightarrow C \sqcup D\) induces an equivalence \[ (i_C^*,i_D^*)\colon \Fun(C\sqcup D, E) \iso \Fun(C,E) \times \Fun(D,E). \]

Proof

For every category \(T\), there are equivalences \[ \begin{aligned} \Map(T,\Fun(C\sqcup D,E)) &\simeq\Map(T\times(C\sqcup D),E)\\ &\simeq\Map((T\times C)\sqcup(T\times D),E)\\ &\simeq\Map(T\times C,E)\times\Map(T\times D,E)\\ &\simeq\Map(T,\Fun(C,E))\times\Map(T,\Fun(D,E))\\ &\simeq\Map(T,\Fun(C,E)\times\Fun(D,E)), \end{aligned} \] using distributivity, the coproduct axiom, and Proposition 1.4.5. This composite is induced by \((i_C^*,i_D^*)\): after uncurrying, its components are restriction along \(T\times i_C\) and \(T\times i_D\), which correspond under Corollary 1.6.17 to the two coproduct inclusions. The result follows from Proposition 1.4.11.

1.7.2 Mapping animae and functor categories

We now compare the mapping anima with the functor category. Currying mapping-anima evaluation defines a canonical functor \[ \imath_{C,D}:=(\ev_{\Map})_c\colon\Map(C,D)\longrightarrow\Fun(C,D). \] The following result identifies \(\Map(C,D)\) with the collection of objects of \(\Fun(C,D)\).

Proposition 1.7.16. For any categories \(C\) and \(D\), there is a preferred equivalence \[ \Map(C,D)\iso\Fun(C,D)^\simeq. \] Under this equivalence, the canonical functor \(\imath_{\Fun(C,D)}\colon\Fun(C,D)^\simeq\to\Fun(C,D)\) is identified with \(\imath_{C,D}\).

Proof

The universal property of functor categories gives equivalences \[ \Fun(C,D)^\simeq =\Map(*,\Fun(C,D)) \simeq\Map(*\times C,D) \simeq\Map(C,D). \] The inverse of their composite is the desired equivalence. To check compatibility with the canonical functors, it suffices to uncurry. Both resulting functors \(\Map(C,D)\times C\to D\) are identified with mapping-anima evaluation.

Proposition 1.7.17. For any anima \(X\), postcomposition with \(\imath_{C,D}\) induces an equivalence \[ (\imath_{C,D})_*\colon \Map(X,\Map(C,D))\iso\Map(X,\Fun(C,D)). \]

Proof

By the preceding proposition, this is the universal property of the groupoid core of \(\Fun(C,D)\) from Corollary 1.4.13.
