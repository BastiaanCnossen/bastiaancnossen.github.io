Synthetic Category Theory · Chapter 1 · Section 1.3

Equivalences of categories

← Previous chapterOverview of the axioms → Next chapterChapter 2 ← Previous sectionSection 1.2 → Next sectionSection 1.4

We now develop some basic properties of the equivalences introduced in Definition 1.1.5.

Proposition 1.3.1. Let \(f\colon C\to D\) be a functor. For \(f\) to be an equivalence, it suffices that the following properties hold for every category \(T\):

  1. (1) For \(a,b\colon T\to C\), any identification \(fa= fb\) gives an identification \(a= b\).

  2. (2) For any \(d\colon T\to D\), there is a functor \(c\colon T\to C\) equipped with an identification \(fc= d\).

Proof

Apply the second property to \(\id_D\) to obtain \(g\colon D\to C\) and \(fg=\id_D\). The associator, this identification, and the unitors identify \(f(gf)\) with \(f\id_C\). The first property, with \(T=C\), gives \(gf=\id_C\).

Definition 1.3.2 (Section/retraction). Consider a functor \(f\colon C \to D\). A section of \(f\) is a functor \(s\colon D \to C\) equipped with an identification \(fs = \id_D\). A retraction of \(f\) is a functor \(r\colon D \to C\) equipped with an identification \(\id_C = rf\).

Lemma 1.3.3. A functor \(f\colon C \to D\) is an equivalence if and only if it admits both a section \(s\) and a retraction \(r\). In this case there exists an identification \(s = r\) and \(s\) is an inverse of \(f\).

Proof

If \(f\) is an equivalence with inverse \(g\colon D \to C\), then clearly \(g\) is both a section and a retraction of \(f\). Conversely, given a section \(s\colon D \to C\), \(fs = \id_D\) and a retraction \(r\colon D \to C\), \(\id_C = rf\), we obtain an identification \(s = r\) by composing the following chain of identifications: \[ s = \id_C \circ s = (r \circ f) \circ s = r \circ (f \circ s) = r \circ \id_D = r. \] It follows that \(f\) is an equivalence with inverse \(s\), since there are identifications \(fs = \id_{D}\) and \(sf = rf = \id_{C}\).

Corollary 1.3.4. Any two inverses \(g\) and \(h\) of an equivalence \(f\) are identified.

Lemma 1.3.5. If \(f\colon C \to D\) and \(f'\colon D \to E\) are equivalences with inverses \(g\colon D \to C\) and \(g'\colon E \to D\), then also the composite \(f'f\colon C \to E\) is an equivalence with inverse \(gg'\colon E \to C\).

Proof

We have \(gg'f'f = g\id_{D}f = gf = \id_{C}\) and \(f'fgg' = f'\id_{D}g'= f'g' = \id_{E}\).

Lemma 1.3.6. Equivalences of categories satisfy the 2-out-of-3 property: given functors \(f\colon C \to D\) and \(g\colon D \to E\), if two of the three functors \(f\), \(g\) and \(gf\) are equivalences, then so is the third.

Proof

If \(g\) and \(f\) are equivalences, then so is \(gf\) by Lemma 1.3.5. If \(f\) and \(gf\) are equivalences, then we may rewrite \(g\) as the composite \(g = g \circ \id_{D} = g \circ f \circ f^{-1} = gf \circ f^{-1}\), so that \(g\) is the composite of two equivalences and hence itself an equivalence. A similar argument shows that \(f\) is an equivalence whenever \(g\) and \(gf\) are equivalences.

Lemma 1.3.7. Equivalences of categories satisfy the 2-out-of-6 property: given functors \(f\colon C \to D\), \(g\colon D \to E\) and \(h\colon E \to F\) such that \(gf\) and \(hg\) are equivalences, also the functors \(f\), \(g\), \(h\) and \(hgf\) are equivalences.

Proof

Consider the functors \(f' := f \circ (gf)^{-1}\colon E \to D\) and \(h' := (hg)^{-1} \circ h \colon E \to D\). Then there are identifications \[ g \circ f' = g \circ f \circ (gf)^{-1} = gf \circ (gf)^{-1} = \id_{E} \] and \[ h' \circ g = (hg)^{-1} \circ h \circ g = (hg)^{-1} \circ hg = \id_{D}. \] Hence it follows from Lemma 1.3.3 that \(g\) is an equivalence. In light of Lemma 1.3.6, we then get that also \(f\) and \(h\) are equivalences, and then so is \(hgf\).

Definition 1.3.8. We say that a functor \(f\colon C \to D\) is a retract of a functor \(f'\colon C' \to D'\) if there exists a commutative diagram of the form together with identifications \(\alpha\colon \id_{C} = h \circ g\) and \(\beta\colon j \circ k = \id_{D}\), and an identification between \(\id_f\colon f= f\) and the composite identification \[ f = f \circ \id_C \overset{\alpha}{=} f \circ h \circ g = j \circ f' \circ g = j \circ k \circ f \overset{\beta}{=} \id_D \circ f = f. \]

Lemma 1.3.9. Assume that \(f\colon C \to D\) is a retract of \(f'\colon C' \to D'\). If \(f'\) is an equivalence, then so is \(f\).

Proof

Let \((f')^{-1}\colon D' \to C'\) be an inverse of \(f'\). We claim that the composite \(w := h \circ (f')^{-1} \circ k\colon D \to C\) is inverse to \(f\). Indeed, we have identifications \[ f \circ w = f \circ h \circ (f')^{-1} \circ k = j \circ f' \circ (f')^{-1} \circ k = j \circ k = \id_{D} \] and \[ w \circ f = h \circ (f')^{-1} \circ k \circ f = h \circ (f')^{-1} \circ f' \circ g = h \circ g = \id_{C}. \] This finishes the proof.

Lemma 1.3.10. Prewhiskering or postwhiskering by an equivalence induces equivalences of animae of identifications.

Proof

Composition with a fixed identification is an equivalence, with inverse given by composition with its inverse. This follows from the vertical unit, associativity, and inverse laws.

Let \(F\colon C\to D\) have inverse \(G\), and consider two functors \(a,b\colon T\to C\). We wish to show that the first functor \(F \star -\) in the following diagram is an equivalence: \[ (a= b)\xrightarrow{F\star-}(Fa= Fb) \xrightarrow{G\star-}(GFa= GFb) \xrightarrow{F\star-}(FGFa= FGFb). \] Interchange with \(GF=\id_C\) identifies the composite of the first two functors with transport along the identifications \(GFa = a\) and \(b = GFb\), and so that composite is an equivalence. The composite of the last two is an equivalence for the same reason, using \(FG=\id_D\), and so the claim follows from the 2-out-of-6 property. Prewhiskering is treated in the same way.

Consequently an equivalence lifts a specified identification, together with an identification of its image with that identification. Applying the same result to functors into an anima of identifications also lifts identifications between identifications.

From the universal property of products, we may directly deduce that products are commutative, unital, and associative. For commutativity of products, one argues as follows:

Lemma 1.3.11. For categories \(C\) and \(D\), the functor \((\pr_2,\pr_1)\colon C \times D \to D \times C\) is an equivalence, with inverse given by \((\pr_2,\pr_1)\colon D \times C \to C \times D\).

Proof

We need to provide identifications \((\pr_2,\pr_1) \circ (\pr_2,\pr_1) = \id_{C \times D}\) and \((\pr_2,\pr_1) \circ (\pr_2,\pr_1) = \id_{D \times C}\). By symmetry, it will suffice to produce the first identification. For this, it will in turn suffice to produce identifications after composing with the functors \(\pr_1\) and \(\pr_2\). For the composition with \(\pr_1\) we compute that \[ \pr_1 \circ ((\pr_2,\pr_1) \circ (\pr_2,\pr_1)) = (\pr_1 \circ (\pr_2,\pr_1)) \circ (\pr_2,\pr_1) = \pr_2 \circ (\pr_2,\pr_1) = \pr_1, \] which is indeed identified with \(\pr_1 \circ \id_{C \times D}\). The case for composition with \(\pr_2\) is analogous. This finishes the proof.

Unitality may be argued similarly:

Lemma 1.3.12. For every category \(C\), the projection functor \(\pr_1\colon C \times * \to C\) is an equivalence, with inverse given by the functor \((\id_{C}, p_{C})\colon C \to C \times *\).

Proof

The composite \(\pr_1 \circ (\id_{C}, p_{C})\colon C \to C\) is identified with \(\id_{C}\) by the defining property of \((\id_{C}, p_{C})\). To show that the composite \((\id_{C}, p_{C}) \circ \pr_1\colon C \times * \to C \times *\) is identified with \(\id_{C \times *}\), it will suffice to do so after composing with \(\pr_1\) and with \(\pr_2\). The case for \(\pr_2\) is clear, since any two functors \(C \times * \to *\) are identified. In the case of \(\pr_1\) we compute \[ \pr_1 \circ ((\id_{C}, p_{C}) \circ \pr_1) = (\pr_1 \circ (\id_{C}, p_{C})) \circ \pr_1 = \id_{C} \circ \pr_1 = \pr_1 = \pr_1 \circ \id_{C \times *}, \] where the first identification is associativity, the second is the relation proved in the first paragraph, and the third and fourth are unitality. This finishes the proof.

Remark 1.3.13. In the previous proofs, we have been very explicit about the usage of associativity and naturality identifications. In the remainder of this book, we will often leave the usage of such identifications implicit in order to make the proofs more concise.

Exercise 1.3.14 (Exercise 1.10.1). Formulate and prove the associativity of the product of categories.

Construction 1.3.15 (Pairing of identifications). Given functors \(f,f'\colon T \to C\) and \(g,g'\colon T \to D\), pairing defines a functor \[ (-,-)\colon(f=f')\times(g=g')\longrightarrow((f,g)=(f',g')). \] For \(\varphi:f=f'\) and \(\psi:g=g'\), its value \((\varphi,\psi)\) is equipped with identifications \begin{align*} \beta^1_{f',g'}\circ\bigl(\pr_1\star(\varphi,\psi)\bigr) &=\varphi\circ\beta^1_{f,g},\\ \beta^2_{f',g'}\circ\bigl(\pr_2\star(\varphi,\psi)\bigr) &=\psi\circ\beta^2_{f,g}. \end{align*} There are identifications \[ (\id_f,\id_g)=\id_{(f,g)},\qquad (\varphi'\circ\varphi,\psi'\circ\psi) =(\varphi',\psi')\circ(\varphi,\psi), \] where \(\varphi':f'=f''\) and \(\psi':g'=g''\).

Proof

Under the identifications \(\beta^1_{f,g}:\pr_1(f,g)=f\) and \(\beta^2_{f,g}:\pr_2(f,g)=g\), \(\phi\) and \(\psi\) correspond to two absolute objects of the two factors of the following product: \[ \bigl(\pr_1(f,g)=\pr_1(f',g')\bigr) \times \bigl(\pr_2(f,g)=\pr_2(f',g')\bigr). \] Since this product is equivalent to the anima \((f,g) = (f',g')\) by Axiom B.2, this provides the identification \((\phi,\psi)\) with the two claimed identification.

We leave the identity law and composition law as exercises for the reader.

Lemma 1.3.16 (Pairing and precomposition). Let \(f\colon T\to C\), \(g\colon T\to D\), and \(\sigma\colon S\to T\) be functors. Pairing commutes with precomposition: there is an identification \[ (f,g)\circ\sigma=(f\circ\sigma,g\circ\sigma). \] This comparison is compatible with identifications of the input functors, with successive precomposition, and with precomposition by an identity functor, using the associativity and unitality identifications.

Proof

After composition with \(\pr_1\) and \(\pr_2\), both sides identify with \(f\circ\sigma\) and \(g\circ\sigma\), respectively. The product axiom therefore supplies the comparison, with these prescribed projections. Its compatibilities can likewise be checked after applying both projections, where they reduce to the coherence laws for composition and identifications. For compatibility with identity functors, one also uses \begin{equation} \rho_{ab}=(a\star\rho_b)\circ\alpha_{\id,b,a} \tag{1.1}\end{equation} for composable functors \(b,a\). This follows by prewhiskering with the identity, applying the pentagon for \(a,b,\id,\id\) and the triangle identities, and cancelling by Lemma 1.3.10. We leave the remaining checks to the reader.

Construction 1.3.17. The formation of products of categories is functorial: given \(f\colon C\to C'\) and \(g\colon D\to D'\), we obtain a functor \[ f\times g:=(f\pr_1,g\pr_2):C\times D\to C'\times D'. \] For identifications \(\varphi : f = f'\) and \(\psi : g = g'\) between functors with these same sources and targets, we define \[ \varphi\times\psi:=(\varphi\star\pr_1,\psi\star\pr_2). \] This gives a functor on the product of the two animae of identifications. It preserves identity identifications and vertical composition by Construction 1.3.15 and the corresponding laws for whiskering.

Exercise 1.3.18 (Exercise 1.10.3). Show that there are identifications \begin{align*} \id_C\times\id_D&=\id_{C\times D},\\ (f'\times g')\circ(f\times g)&=(f'f)\times(g'g) \end{align*} for \(f\colon C\to C'\), \(f'\colon C'\to C''\), \(g\colon D\to D'\), and \(g'\colon D'\to D''\).
