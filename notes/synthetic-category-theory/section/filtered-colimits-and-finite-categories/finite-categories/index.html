Synthetic Category Theory · Chapter 16 · Section 16.4

Finite categories

← Previous chapterChapter 15 → Next chapterChapter 17 ← Previous sectionSection 16.3 → Next sectionSection 17.1

We will construct the universe of finite categories. We fix a universe \(\Cat\).

Construction 16.4.1. Given a full embedding \(i\colon C\hookrightarrow\Cat\), we consider the functor \[ \colim_{\pushout}\colon\Fun({\pushout},\Cat)\to\Cat \] and define \(E(C)^\simeq\hookrightarrow\Cat^\simeq\) as the image of the induced map \[ \Fun(\pushout,C)^\simeq\xrightarrow{i^\simeq}\Fun(\pushout,\Cat)^\simeq \xrightarrow{\colim^\simeq_{\pushout}}\Cat^\simeq\, . \] We define \(E(C)\) as the full subcategory of \(\Cat\) with collection of objects \(E(C)^\simeq\), with corresponding embedding \[ i_{E(C)}\colon E(C)\hookrightarrow\Cat\, . \] Since the pushout of any diagram of the form \(x\xleftarrow{\id_x}x\xrightarrow{\id_x}x\) is \(x\) itself, the functor \(\const_{\pushout}\colon C\to\Fun({\pushout},C)\) induces a canonical functor \[ e_C\colon C\to E(C) \] such that \(i_{E(C)}\circ e_C\simeq i\). We observe that, by virtue of Proposition 11.2.18, if \(C\) is small, then so is \(E(C)\) (as full subcategory of a locally small category with a small collection of objects).

We define \(C_0\) as the full subcategory of \(\Cat\) with objects \(\emptyset\), \([0]\) and \([1]\), with \[ i_0\colon C_0\hookrightarrow\Cat \] the embedding. We define for any \(n : \N\) \[ e_n\colon C_n\hookrightarrow C_{n+1} \] through \(C_{n+1}=E(C_n)\) and \(e_n=e_{C_n}\). There is also a full embedding \[ i_{n+1}\colon C_{n+1}\to\Cat \] defined by \(i_{n+1}=i_{E(C_n)}\). The sequence of maps \(e_n\), \(n : \N\), define a functor \(C_\bullet\colon \omega\to\Cat\).

Definition 16.4.2. We define the category \(\Catfin\) of finite categories as the colimit \[ \Catfin=\underset{n : \omega}{\colim\, } C_n\, . \]

Remark 16.4.3. It follows from Corollary 16.3.12 that \(\Catfin\) is the full subcategory of \(\Cat\) induced by the embedding \[ \underset{n : \omega}{\colim\, } C_n^\simeq\hookrightarrow\Cat^\simeq\, . \] Since \(\omega\) as well as each \(C_n\) are small, so is \(\Catfin\).

Lemma 16.4.4. The category \(\pushout\) is a finite category.

Proof

We know that \([n]\) belongs to \(C_0\) for \(n=0\) and \(n=1\) and we have a pushout square of the form below. This shows that \(\pushout\) belongs to \(C_1\).

Proposition 16.4.5. Any finite category is compact in \(\Cat\).

Proof

The generators \([0]\) and \([1]\) are compact by Theorem 16.3.9. The initial category is compact because filtered categories are weakly contractible. By Proposition 16.3.8, induction shows that every object of \(C_n\) is compact. Any object of \(\Catfin\) belongs to some \(C_n\) after base change along a cover (Corollary 13.2.2). Compactness is a proposition, so it descends along this cover.

Lemma 16.4.6. The category \(\Catfin\) is the smallest full subcategory of \(\Cat\) stable under pushouts containing the objects \(\emptyset\), \([0]\), and \([1]\). In other words, given a full subcategory \(C\) of \(\Cat\), if the embedding \(C\hookrightarrow\Cat\) is a right exact functor, then the following conditions are equivalent.

  • (i) The embedding \(\Catfin\hookrightarrow\Cat\) factors through the embedding \(C\hookrightarrow\Cat\).

  • (ii) The embedding \(\Catfin^\simeq\hookrightarrow\Cat^\simeq\) factors through the embedding \(C^\simeq\hookrightarrow\Cat^\simeq\).

  • (iii) The objects \([0]\) and \([1]\) belong to \(C\).

Proof

By Lemma 16.4.4 and Proposition 16.4.5, \(\pushout\) is compact in \(\Cat\). Hence there is an equivalence \[ \underset{n : \omega}{\colim\ }\Fun(\pushout,C_n)^\simeq \iso \Fun(\pushout,\Catfin)^\simeq. \] Thus, after base change along the canonical cover, any functor \(F\colon\pushout\to\Catfin\) factors through some \(C_n\). In that context, the pushout of \(F\) in \(\Cat\) belongs to \(C_{n+1}\), hence to \(\Catfin\). Since belonging to the full subcategory \(\Catfin\hookrightarrow\Cat\) is a proposition, this cover-local conclusion descends. Therefore, \(\Catfin\) is stable under pushouts in \(\Cat\).

Conditions (i) and (ii) are equivalent by the universal property of full embeddings, and (i) implies (iii). Conversely, right exactness and (iii) ensure that \(C\) contains \(C_0\). Since \(C\) is closed under pushouts, induction gives \(C_n\subset C\) for all \(n\). Taking the colimit of these full embeddings gives \(\Catfin\hookrightarrow C\) by Corollary 16.3.12 because \(\colim_\omega C = C\) by weak contractibility of \(\omega\) (Corollary 16.1.6).

Proposition 16.4.7. The opposite of a finite category is finite: for any \(C : \Catfin\), we have \(C\catop : \Catfin\).

Proof

The functor \((-)\catop\) preserves pushouts and carries each of \(\emptyset\), \([0]\), and \([1]\) to an equivalent category. Hence the full subcategory of categories whose opposites are finite contains these generators and is closed under pushouts. Apply Lemma 16.4.6.

Proposition 16.4.8. If \(A\) and \(B\) are finite categories, then so is \(A\times B\).

Proof

Products preserve colimits in each variable. Applying Lemma 16.4.6 successively in the two variables therefore reduces the assertion to products of \(\emptyset\), \([0]\), and \([1]\) with one another. Only \([1]\times[1]\) needs consideration: it is the pushout \[ [2]\sqcup_{[1]}[2], \] where both maps \([1]\to[2]\) select the long edge. Moreover, \([2]\) is finite because \([2]=[1]\sqcup_{[0]}[1]\). Thus all products of generators are finite, as required.

Corollary 16.4.9. For any finite category \(A\), the functor \[ \Fun(A,-)\colon\Cat\to\Cat \] commutes with small filtered colimits.

Proof

Let \(I\) be a small filtered category and \(C\colon I\to\Cat\) a functor. In order to prove that the canonical functor \[ \underset{i : I}{\colim\ }\Fun(A,C_i)\to\Fun(A,\underset{i : I}{\colim\ }C_i) \] is an equivalence, it suffices to check that the induced map \[ \Hom_\Cat([1],\underset{i : I}{\colim\ }\Fun(A,C_i))\to \Hom_\Cat([1],\Fun(A,\underset{i : I}{\colim\ }C_i)) \] is an equivalence (by Corollary 6.3.9, taking into account the correspondence between \(\Map\) and \(\Hom_\Cat\) through directed univalence). Since \([1]\) and \([1]\times A\) are compact, we have: \begin{align*} \Hom_\Cat([1],\underset{i : I}{\colim\ }\Fun(A,C_i)) &=\underset{i : I}{\colim\ }\Hom_\Cat([1],\Fun(A,C_i))\\ &=\underset{i : I}{\colim\ }\Hom_\Cat([1]\times A,C_i)\\ &=\Hom_\Cat([1]\times A,\underset{i : I}{\colim\ }C_i)\\ &=\Hom_\Cat([1],\Fun(A,\underset{i : I}{\colim\ }C_i))\, . \end{align*}

Proposition 16.4.10. The embedding \(\Catfin\hookrightarrow\Cat\) commutes with pushouts.

Proof

By Lemma 16.4.6, a pushout in \(\Cat\) of finite categories is finite. Since \(\Catfin\) is a full subcategory, this is also a pushout in \(\Catfin\).

Corollary 16.4.11. If \(A\) and \(B\) are finite categories, then so is \(A\star B\).

Proof

There is a pushout of the form below. The previous proposition together with Proposition 16.4.8 thus imply that \(A\star B\) is finite.

Proposition 16.4.12. Let \(C\) be a category. If \(C\) has an initial (terminal) object as well as pushouts (pullbacks), then \(C\) has colimits (limits, resp.) of type \(A\) for any finite category \(A\).

Proof

Since \(C\) has an initial object, it has colimits of type \(\emptyset\). It also has colimits of type \([0]\) and \([1]\) (more generally, of type \(A\) for any \(A\) with a terminal object). The full subcategory of \(\Cat\) spanned by categories \(A\) such that \(C\) has \(A\)-indexed colimits is stable under pushouts, by Corollary 13.3.2. This full subcategory also contains \(C_0\). Therefore, it contains \(\Catfin\) as well, by Lemma 16.4.6.

Proposition 16.4.13. Let \(f\colon C\to D\) be a functor. We assume that \(C\) and \(D\) both have initial (terminal) objects and pushouts (pullbacks). If \(f\) is right (left) exact then it commutes with colimits (limits) of type \(A\) for any finite category \(A\).

Proof

We will consider the case of finite colimits (the case of finite limits will follow by replacing \(C\) by \(C\catop\)).

The full subcategory \(T\) of \(\Cat\) spanned by categories \(A\) such that \(f\) commutes with \(A\)-indexed colimits is stable under pushouts. Indeed, if \(A=A_1\sqcup_{A_0} A_2\) and \(f\) commutes with colimits indexed by \(A_0\), \(A_1\) or \(A_2\), for any functor \(X\colon A\to C\), if we denote by \(X_{|E}\) the composition of \(X\) with the structural functor \(E\to A\) for \(E=A_0,A_1,A_2\), then we have canonical isomorphisms \[ \colim_E\ fX_{|E}\iso f(\colim_E\ X_{|E})\, . \] If \(f\) is right exact, a double application of Corollary 13.3.2 yields isomorphisms \begin{align*} \colim_A fX &\simeq(\colim_{A_1}\ fX_{|A_1})\sqcup_{(\colim_{A_0}\ fX_{|A_0})}(\colim_{A_2}\ fX_{|A_2})\\ &\simeq f(\colim_{A_1}\ X_{|A_1})\sqcup_{f(\colim_{A_0}\ X_{|A_0})}f(\colim_{A_2}\ X_{|A_2})\\ &\simeq f\big((\colim_{A_1}\ X_{|A_1})\sqcup_{(\colim_{A_0}\ X_{|A_0})}(\colim_{A_2}\ X_{|A_2})\big)\\ &\simeq f(\colim_A\ X)\, . \end{align*} If \(f\) preserves initial objects, this full subcategory \(T\) contains \(\emptyset\). It clearly contains \([0]\) and \([1]\). Therefore, by Lemma 16.4.6, \(T\) contains all finite categories.

Recall that an anima \(X\) is finitely covered if there merely exists a natural number \(n\) and a cover \(\{1,\dots, n\}\to X\). If \(X\) is a set we also also call such choice a finite enumeration (see Definition 11.6.2).

Definition 16.4.14. Starting from \(A_0\subseteq \An\) the full subcategory spanned by \(\emptyset\) and \([0]\), define by \(A_{n+1}\subseteq \An\) to be full subcategory of those animae that can merely be obtained by forming a pushout in \(A_n\) (analogously to Construction 16.4.1).

An anima \(A\) is called finite if lies in the full subcategory \(\FinAn\coloneq \colim_{n:\omega}A_n\).

Lemma 16.4.15. Let \(C\) be a finite category.

  1. (1) The anima \(\Pi_\infty A\) is a finite anima.

  2. (2) The anima \(C^\simeq\) is finitely covered.

Warning 16.4.16. For a finite category \(C\), the anima \(C^\simeq\) need not be finite again. Here is the sketch of an example.

Consider a finitely presented group \(G=\langle g_1,\dots,g_p \mid r_1,\dots, r_q \rangle\) with a finitely generated but not finitely presented subgroup \(H=\langle h_1,\dots,h_n \rangle\triangleleft G\). From this data, build the following finite category.

  • • Start with the walking retract \(R=\{s:0\triangleleft 1 : r\}\), i.e., the pushout (where \(s\) is the image of \(0\to 1\) and \(r\) the image of \(1\to 2=0\)) Denote by \(e\coloneqq sr \colon 1\to 1\) the nontrivial idempotent.

  • • For each generator \(g_i\), attach an automorphism \(g_i\colon 1\iso1\), and for each relation \(r_j\) an identification between the corresponding composites.

  • • For each generator \(h_k\) of \(H\) attach an identification \(u_k\colon h_ke=eh_k\).

The resulting category \(C\) is finite by construction. However, the core \(C^\simeq\) is not a finite anima. Indeed, every finite anima has finitely presented fundamental groups, but one can compute that \(\pi_1(C^\simeq, 0)\cong H\), which was not finitely presented by assumption.

Proof

  1. (1) By induction on \(n\) we see that the geometric realization functor \(\Pi_\infty \colon \Cat \to \An\) restricts to a functor \(\Pi_\infty\colon C_n \to A_n\). Indeed, it fixes \(\emptyset\) and \([0]\) and sends \([1]\mapsto [0]\) and preserves pushouts. Taking colimits, it restricts to a functor \(\Pi_\infty\colon \Catfin\to \FinAn\).

  2. (2) Clearly the cores of \(\emptyset\), \([0]\) and \([1]\) are finitely covered (even finite) sets. Hence by Lemma 16.4.6 it suffices to show that categories with finitely covered cores are closed under pushouts. Consider a functor \(X\colon \pushout\to \Cat\) corresponding to a diagram \(C\leftarrow B\to D\) of categories and assume that we have finite covers \(S\to B\), \(T\to C\) and \(U\to D\) (we may choose them because we are proving a mere existence statement). By the computation of colimits of categories (Theorem 13.2.1), we can compute the pushout \(C\amalg_B D\) as a localization of the category \(\int_{\pushout} X\) whose core is covered by the finite set \(S\amalg T \amalg D\). Since the core of a category always covers any of its localizations (Corollary 11.2.16), we see that \(C\amalg_B D\) is finitely covered.

Corollary 16.4.17. An anima \(A\) is finite (as an anima) if and only if it is a finite category, i.e., we have \(\FinAn =\Catfin\cap \An\) as full subcategories of \(\Cat\).

Proof

“Only if” is clear because \(A_n\subseteq C_n\) for each \(n\), and “if” follows from Lemma 16.4.15 because \(A=\Pi_\infty A\).

Definition 16.4.18. Let \(P\) be a poset. We call \(P\) a finite poset if the following hold:

  1. (1) There merely exists a finite enumeration of the set \(P^\simeq\);

  2. (2) Its order is decidable, in the sense that there is a term of \[ \prod_{x,y : P^\simeq}\bigl(P(x,y)\sqcup\neg P(x,y)\bigr)\, . \]

Remark 16.4.19. By Proposition 11.6.3, a set \(S\) is finite (in the sense of Definition 11.6.1) if and only if \(S\) is finite as a poset.

Lemma 16.4.20. If \(P\) is a finite poset, then its core \(P^\simeq\) is a finite set.

Proof

The order on \(P\) being decidable, equality in \(P^\simeq\) is decidable as well: antisymmetry identifies \(x=y\) with \(P(x,y)\times P(y,x)\); in other words, \(P^\simeq\) is a finite poset. The claim thus follows from Remark 16.4.19 applied to the set \(P^\simeq\).

Lemma 16.4.21. Finite posets are closed under pushouts of posets.

Proof

Consider a diagram \(Q\xleftarrow{f} P\xrightarrow{g} R\) of finite posets. We aim to show that the pushout \(Q\amalg_P R\) (in posets) is finite again.

Let us temporarily fix cardinal presentations of \(P^\simeq,Q^\simeq,R^\simeq\) of size \(l,m,n : \N\), respectively. Using these presentations, we perform the following construction steps.

  • • Define a reflexive and transitive relation \(\leq\) (preorder) on the set \(X\coloneq P^\simeq\amalg Q^\simeq\amalg R^\simeq\) by starting with the coproduct order inherited from \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\) and imposing \[p\leq f(p)\leq g(p) \leq p\] for each \(p : P^\simeq\).

  • • Define the equivalence relation on \(X\) generated by \((x\sim y) \coloneq (x\leq y) \times (y\leq x)\).

  • • Both the preorder \(\leq\) and the equivalence relation \(\sim\) can be decided by a bounded number of equalities and/or order relations in \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\), each of which is decidable; hence both \(\leq\) and \(\sim\) are decidable.

  • • Using the chosen cardinal presentations we can construct a decidable subset \(Y\subseteq X\) which intersects each \(\sim\)-equivalence class exactly once. For example, one could use the bijection \(X\cong \{1,\dots,l\}\amalg \{1,\dots m\}\amalg\{1,\dots n\}\cong \{1,\dots l+m+n\}\) to define a decidable linear order \(o\) on \(X\) and then define \(Y\) to consist of those elements of \(X\) that are \(o\)-minimal in their \(\sim\)-equivalence class.

  • • Denote by \(u\colon X\to Y\) the map of sets that sends each \(x \in X\) to the unique \(y\in Y\) with \(x\sim y\). Moreover, we have a square of sets which commutes because \(f(p)\sim p \sim g(p)\) for all \(p\in P\) and \(u\) identifies \(\sim\)-related terms by construction.

  • • By construction, the preorder \(\leq\) is antisymmetric when restricted to \(Y\); hence we have a partial order on \(Y\). Passing through Theorem 10.3.10, this yields a poset \(T\) with \((T^\simeq,\leq)=(Y,\leq)\).

  • • By construction of the order and Lemma 10.3.7, the commutative square above has a unique upgrade to a commutative square of posets Showing that this square is a pushout of posets is a straightforward computation involving maps of underlying sets and the order relation.

  • • By construction, the poset \(T\) has again a finite set of objects (\(Y\) was defined as a decidable subset of \(P^\simeq \amalg Q^\simeq\amalg R^\simeq\)) and decidable order.

  • • Since the pushout \(Q\amalg_P R\) (in posets) is isomorphic to \(T\), it too is a finite poset.

Of course the construction of \(T\) itself heavily depends on the chosen cardinal presentations of \(P^\simeq\), \(Q^\simeq\) and \(R^\simeq\). But the final conclusion, namely that \(Q\amalg_P R\) is finite, is a proposition; hence we may discharge the propositional truncation, and conclude the proof.

Corollary 16.4.22.

  • • The poset truncation \(\posetrunc{C}\) of a finite category \(C\) is a finite poset.

  • • The set truncation \(\setrunc{A}\) of a finite anima \(A\) is a finite set.

Proof

The first statement follows from Lemma 16.4.21 because \(\emptyset\), \([0]\) and \([1]\) are finite posets and \(\posetrunc{-}\) sends pushouts of categories to pushouts of posets.

The second statement follows from the first, because the poset truncation of an anima is its set truncation.

Proposition 16.4.23. Let \(P\) be a poset. Then \(P\) is finite as a poset if and only if it is a finite category.

Proof

The statement “if” follows directly from Corollary 16.4.22, so it remains to show “only if”. Since the assertion that \(P\) is a finite category is a proposition, we may fix an isomorphism \(\{1,\dots, n\}\cong P^\simeq\) to do so.

We first observe, by induction on \(n\), that every nonempty poset with decidable order and \(n\) objects has a maximal object. Indeed, remove one object \(p\). If no object remains, then \(p\) is maximal. Otherwise, choose by induction a maximal object \(m\) of the remaining poset and decide whether \(m\leq p\). If \(m\leq p\), then \(p\) is maximal; if not, then \(m\) remains maximal after adjoining \(p\). For the first assertion, an object of the remaining poset above \(p\) would also be above \(m\), and hence would equal \(m\); antisymmetry would then identify it with \(p\). The second assertion follows because the only new possible object above \(m\) is \(p\).

We now prove the proposition by strong induction on \(n\). The case \(n=0\) is clear. Suppose that \(P\) has \(n+1\) objects and choose a maximal object \(m\). Let \(Q\) be the full subposet on the objects distinct from \(m\), let \(P_{\leq m}\) be the full subposet on the objects \(x\leq m\), and put \(P_{<m}=Q\cap P_{\leq m}\). The poset \(Q\) has \(n\) objects. Since the predicate \(x\leq m\) is decidable, filtering a cardinal presentation of \(Q\) gives a cardinal presentation of \(P_{<m}\) with at most \(n\) objects. Both \(Q\) and \(P_{<m}\) are therefore finite categories by the induction hypothesis. Moreover, \[ P_{\leq m}=P_{<m}\star[0]\, , \] so \(P_{\leq m}\) is finite by Corollary 16.4.11.

It remains to verify that the square is a pushout in \(\Cat\). Every chain \([k]\to P\) lies in \(Q\) or in \(P_{\leq m}\): if it contains \(m\), maximality forces all its vertices to lie below \(m\). A chain lies in both subposets precisely when it lies in \(P_{<m}\). Since equality with \(m\) is decidable, this exhibits each nerve level as a union of two subsets with the stated intersection. Applying the Rezk nerve therefore gives a levelwise pushout square. Mapping this levelwise pushout into the nerve of an arbitrary category, and using the full faithfulness of the nerve from Corollary 15.1.13, gives the pushout universal property in \(\Cat\). Since finite categories are closed under pushouts by Lemma 16.4.6, \(P\) is finite, as required.
