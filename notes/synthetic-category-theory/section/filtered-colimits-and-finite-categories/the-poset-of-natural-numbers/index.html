Synthetic Category Theory · Chapter 16 · Section 16.1

The poset of natural numbers

← Previous chapterChapter 15 → Next chapterChapter 17 ← Previous sectionSection 15.5 → Next sectionSection 16.2

In Chapter 10, we defined the set of natural numbers. We now augment the set \(\N\) with an order to define the ordinal \(\omega\), i.e., the poset of natural numbers.

Construction 16.1.1. We define the function \begin{equation} (-\leq-) \colon \N\times \N \to \Prop \tag{16.1}\end{equation} by double induction as follows: \begin{equation} (0\leq m) \coloneq * \quad\text{and}\quad (n+1\leq 0)\coloneq \emptyset \quad\text{and}\quad (n+1\leq m+1) \coloneq (n\leq m) \tag{16.2}\end{equation} Furthermore, we define \((n<m)\coloneq (n\leq m)\times (n\neq m)\)

Exercise 16.1.2. Show that for all \(n,m\in \N\) we have \begin{equation} (n \leq m) = (x : \N) \times (n+x=m). \tag{16.3}\end{equation}

Lemma 16.1.3. The relation \(\leq\) is a partial order on the set \(\N\) which is trichotomous, i.e., for all \(n,m \in \N\) we have \begin{equation} \left( (n < m) \amalg (n=m) \amalg (n>m) \right) \, =\,* \tag{16.4}\end{equation} In particular \(\leq\) is decidable.

Proof

All statements are straightforward to prove by induction using observational equality of natural numbers (Proposition 10.2.19).

Definition 16.1.4. We define \(\omega\) to be the unique poset with \(\omega^\simeq=\N\) and \(\omega(-,-)=(-\leq -)\). In other words, \(\omega\) is the poset associated to the partially ordered set \((\N,\leq)\) through the equivalence Theorem 10.3.10.

For each \(n,m\in \N\) we denote by \([n,m]\subseteq \omega\) the full subcategory spanned by \(\{x\in \N \mid n\leq x\leq m\}\).

Remark 16.1.5. Note that for each \(n\leq m\) we have \([n,m]\star[0]=[n,m+1]\) because we have \([n,m+1]^\simeq =[n,m]^\simeq\amalg \{m+1\}\), where the object \(m+1\) is terminal in \([n,m+1]\), the inclusion \([n,m]\subseteq [n,m+1]\) is fully faithful and there are no arrows going from \(m+1\) to any object in \([n,m]\). In particular, for each \(n\in \N\) there is an isomorphism \([n]\cong [0,n]\) (which is unique by Theorem 10.4.3).

Corollary 16.1.6. The object \(0\in\omega\) is initial, hence \(\omega\) is weakly contractible.

Proof

Immediate, because \(0\leq n\) for all \(n\in \N\).

Lemma 16.1.7. The successor function \(\succ\colon \N\to\N\) defines a poset map \(\omega\to \omega\).

Proof

By definition.

Lemma 16.1.8. [Binary coproducts in \(\omega\)] The poset \(\omega\) has binary products and coproducts, namely the minimum and maximum.

Proof

This follows from the fact that \((\N,\leq)\) is trichotomous. Explicitly, \begin{equation} \min(n,m)\coloneq \begin{cases} n\text{, if } n\leq m \\ m\text{, if } n>m \end{cases} \tag{16.5}\end{equation} and the maximum is defined analogously.

Lemma 16.1.9. For any \(n : \N\) the functor \[ \succ^n\colon \omega\to \omega, \quad x\mapsto x+n \] has a left adjoint given by \[ \pred^n\colon x\mapsto \begin{cases} x-n,\quad\text{if } x\geq n \\ 0,\quad\text{if } x\leq n \end{cases} \] respectively. In particular, it is terminal.

Proof

To construct \(\pred^n\) as a map of posets, it suffices to show the following to statements, both of which are straightforward by induction:

  • • For each \(x\geq n\) there is a unique \(k\in \N\) with \(n+k=x\), call it \(x-n\coloneq k\). This shows that \(\pred^n\) is well-defined as a set map.

  • • If \(x'\geq x \geq n\) then \(x'-n \geq x-n\) hence \(\pred^n\) preserves the order (there is nothing to check in the case \(x\leq n\) because \(0\leq -\) is always true).

To establish the adjunction one verifies the inequalities \(x\leq \pred^n(x)+n\) (with equality precisely when \(x\geq n\)) and \(\pred^n(x+n)=x\). These are the unit and counit of the adjunction; the triangle identities are vacuous because \(\omega\) is a poset.

Lemma 16.1.10. For any \(n : \N\), the functor \[ \succ^n\colon \omega\to \omega \] is a fully faithful left fibration and induces an equivalence \[ \omega\iso\omega_{\geq n}=\omega_{n/}, \]

Proof

Since the counit of the adjunction of Lemma 16.1.9 is the identity, \(\succ^n\) is fully faithful and the adjunction restricts to the equivalence \begin{equation} \pred^n\colon \omega_{\geq n} \xleftrightarrow{\simeq} \omega : \succ^n \tag{16.6}\end{equation} because \(\omega_{\geq n}\subseteq \omega\) is exactly the full subposet where the unit is invertible. Since \(\succ^n\colon \omega\to\omega\) is thereby identified with the slice projection \(\omega_{\geq n}=\omega_{n/}\to \omega\), it is a left fibration.

Theorem 16.1.11 (Milnor presentation of \(\omega\)). The canonical square of posets (defined by sending each element to the corresponding element with the same name) is a pushout square in \(\Cat\).

To prove Theorem 16.1.11 we make repeated use of the following general technique:

Lemma 16.1.12 (one-point sink/source attachment). Let \(C\) be a category and \(c\in C\) an object. Consider the pushout We say that \(D\) arises from \(C\) by a one-point sink attachment (at \(c\in C\)). Then taking cores yields \(C^\simeq \amalg \{1\}\simeq D^\simeq\) and the hom-animae in \(D\) are characterized under this identification by

  1. (1) \(C\to D\) is fully faithful, i.e. \(C(x,y)\xrightarrow{\simeq}D(x,y)\) for \(x,y\in C\);

  2. (2) \(D(1,1)=*\);

  3. (3) \(C(x,c)\xrightarrow[=]{\alpha\circ -}D(x,1)\) for \(x\in C\);

  4. (4) \(D(1,x)=\emptyset\) for \(x\in C\).

In particular, if \(C\) is a poset, then so is \(D\).

The dual construction is called a one-point source attachment.

Proof

Define the functor \(D\to [1]\) which is constantly \(0\) on \(C\) and the identity on \([1]\). Since the two inclusions \(0,1\colon *\to [1]\) are exponentiable (because they are a right and left fibration, respectively), the square (16.8) remains a pushout when passing to fibers over \(0\) and \(1\). In either case, the pushout degenerates and yields \begin{equation} C\iso D_0 \quad\text{and}\quad \{1\}\iso D_1, \tag{16.9}\end{equation} respectively. Since \([1]^\simeq=\{0\}\amalg \{1\}\), we obtain an identification \(D^\simeq\simeq C^\simeq \amalg \{1\}\), and the statements 1 and 2; we also get 4 because there are no arrows \(1\to 0\) in \([1]\).

Finally, note that \(\{0\}\to [1]\) has a right adjoint retraction that sends \(1\mapsto 0\), hence \(C\to D\) has a right adjoint retraction (by Proposition 4.4.17) that sends \(1\mapsto c\) and whose counit at \(1\in D\) is \(\alpha\colon c\to 1\) (by direct inspection of the construction). The equivalence 3 is precisely the equivalence on hom-animae induced by this adjunction.

Proof of Theorem 16.1.11

It is easy to show that the square (16.7) is a pushout of partially ordered sets:

  • • The square is tautologically a pushout on cores (a.k.a., underlying sets)

  • • For any partially ordered set \((S,\leq)\), a set map \(\N\to S\) preserves the order if and only if it preserves the order when restricted to each \(\{n,n+1\} \subseteq \N\).

In other words, if we form the actual pushout of categories, then we have \(\posetrunc{P}=\omega\) after poset truncation; it remains to show that \(P\) is already a poset.

For each \(a \in \N\) and \(b\in \N\star \{\infty\}\), denote by \(P[a,b]\) the same pushout but retaining only those coproduct components that are indexed by \(a\leq n< b\) on the left side and \(a\leq n\leq b\) in the top right corner *Note that despite the notation suggesting a closed interval, there is no object named \(\infty\) appearing anywhere in \(P[a,\infty]\).. For each finite \(b\), the analogous pushouts formed in posets yield the posets \([a,b]\); hence we have truncation maps \(P[a,b]\to [a,b]\) which are natural with respect to inclusions of intervals.

Observe that for \(b=a\) the pushout (16.10) degenerates to \[ P[a,a]=\{a\}\amalg_\emptyset \emptyset = [0] \] and for \(b=a+1\) the pushout (16.10) degenerates to \[ P[a,a+1]=[a,a+1]\amalg_{\{a,a+1\}}(\{a\}\amalg \{a+1\})=[1]. \]

An elementary rearranging of pushouts and coproducts shows that for each \(a\leq b<\infty\) and \(b\leq c\leq \infty\) we have a pushout squares of the following form In particular:

  • • Choosing \(c=b+1\) exhibits \(P[a,b+1]\) as a one-point sink attachment of \(P[a,b]\) at the last vertex \(b\). Hence, inductively for all \(a\leq b<\infty\), the category \(P[a,b]\) is a poset, hence \(P[a,b]=[a,b]\).

  • • Choosing \(a=0\), \(b=1\) and \(c=\infty\) exhibits \(P\) a one-point source attachment of \(P[1,\infty]\). In particular, the inclusion \([0]\to P\) is fully faithful.

  • • For each \(n\in \N\), choosing \(a=0\), \(b=n\) and \(c=\infty\) yields the pushout The top map is identified with \(\{0\}\to P[0,\infty]\) by relabeling all coproduct components along the bijection \(\succ^n\colon \N\to \N_{\geq n}\). Since the top map is fully faithful by the previous step, so is the bottom one by Corollary 12.2.12.

If we take cores of the defining pushout (16.10), then the left vertical map becomes an equivalence. Thus it follows from Corollary 13.2.2 that the map \(\N\to P^\simeq\) is a cover.

We can now show that \(P\) is a poset. For any \(x,y\in P\) we have to show that \(P(x,y)\) is a proposition. This statement itself is a proposition, so we may choose \(i,j \in \N\) with \(i\mapsto x\) and \(j\mapsto y\) (because \(\N\to P^\simeq\) is a cover). Now take \(n\coloneq max(i,j)\) so that \(x,y\) are both in the image of the map \([n]\to P\). Since we have shown this map to be fully faithful, we conclude that \(P(x,y)\) is identified with \([n](i,j)\) which is a proposition.

Definition 16.1.14. Let \(C\) be a category. We define the category \(\Seq_{\omega}(C)\) of \(\omega\)-indexed sequences in \(C\) as the following pullback: where the functor \(p\) is defined by \(p((f_n)_{n \in \N})_i := (t(f_i), s(f_{i+1}))\). Dually, we define the category \(\Seq_{\omega\catop}(C)\) of \(\omega\catop\)-indexed sequences in \(C\) as \[ \Seq_{\omega\catop}(C) := \Seq_{\omega}(C\catop)\catop. \]

Explicitly, an object of \(\Seq_{\omega}(C)\) consists of a collection of objects \((X_n)_{n \in \N}\) of \(C\) together with a sequence of morphisms \(f_n\colon X_n \to X_{n+1}\) between them: \[ X_0 \xrightarrow{f_0} X_1 \xrightarrow{f_1} X_2 \xrightarrow{f_2} \cdots \] We will denote such an object by \((X_n,f_n)_{n \in \N}\). Dually, an object of \(\Seq_{\omega\catop}(C)\) consists of a collection of objects \((X_n)_{n \in \N}\) with morphisms \(f_n \colon X_{n+1} \to X_n\) going in the other direction.

Corollary 16.1.15. For each category \(C\), the left pushout square of Remark 16.1.13 induces equivalences \begin{equation} \Seq_\omega(C) \simeq \Fun(\omega,C) \quad\text{and}\quad \Seq_{\omega^\op}(C) \simeq \Fun(\omega^\op,C) \tag{16.16}\end{equation} of categories.

Proof

The square (16.15) arises from Milnor presentation (16.13) of \(\omega\) by applying \(\Fun(-,C)\) which sends pushouts to pullbacks.

Definition 16.1.16 (Milnor (co)limit). Let \((X_n,f_n)_{n \in \N\catop}\) be an \(\omega\catop\)-indexed sequence in a category \(C\) with pullbacks as well as countable products (i.e. \(\N\)-indexed limits). We define the Milnor limit \(\Millim{n} X_n\) of the sequence above as the pullback where for \(i \in \N\) the \(i\)-th component of the map \(q\) is given by the map \((\pr_{i}, f_i \circ \pr_{i+1})\colon \prod_{n} X_n \to X_{i} \times X_{i}\).

Dually, if \((X_n,f_n)_{n \in \N}\) is an \(\omega\)-indexed sequence in a category \(C\) with colimits, we define its Milnor colimit \(\Milcolim{n} X_n\) as the Milnor limit of the associated sequence in \(C\catop\).

Corollary 16.1.17. Under the identification of Corollary 16.1.15, the Milnor colimit (or limit) of an \(\omega\) (or \(\omega^\op\)) sequence agrees with its usual colimit (or limit).

Proof

This follows from the Milnor presentation of \(\omega\) and the computation of (co)limits indexed by colimits (Corollary 13.3.2).

Remark 16.1.18. Under the identification of Corollary 16.1.15, the functor \(\succ^*\colon \Fun(\omega, C)\to \Fun(\omega,C)\) has the following explicit description. It sends a sequence of the form \[ X_0\overset{f_0}{\to} X_1\overset{f_1}{\to}\cdots \to X_n\overset{f_n}{\to} X_{n+1}\to\cdots \] to the sequence \[ X_1\overset{f_1}{\to} X_2\overset{f_2}{\to}\cdots \to X_{n+1}\overset{f_{n+1}}{\to} X_{n+2}\to\cdots \] By Lemma 16.1.9, the functor \(\succ^*\) above has a right adjoint \[ \succ_*\colon\Seq_{\omega}(C)\to\Seq_{\omega}(C) \] which explicitly sends a sequence of the form \[ X_0\overset{f_0}{\to} X_1\overset{f_1}{\to}\cdots \to X_n\overset{f_n}{\to} X_{n+1}\to\cdots \] to the sequence \[ X_0\overset{\id_{X_0}}{\to}X_0\overset{f_0}{\to} X_1\overset{f_1}{\to}\cdots \to X_n\overset{f_n}{\to} X_{n+1}\to\cdots \] The counit of this adjunction is the identity \(\succ^*\succ_*=\id\), so that, in particular, the functor \(\succ_*\) is fully faithful.

Proposition 16.1.19. For any \(m,n : \N\) with maximum \(k=\max(m,n)\), the functor \(\succ^k\) induces an equivalence \[ \omega\iso\omega_{m/}\times_\omega\omega_{n/}\, . \]

Proof

There is a canonical equivalence of full subcategories of \(\omega\): \[ \omega_{k/}\simeq\omega_{m/}\times_\omega\omega_{n/}\, . \] We conclude with Lemma 16.1.10.
