Proposition 19.6.1. Let \(C\) be a stable category and \(W\) a family of maps in \(C\) with the \(2\)-out-of-\(3\) property and stable under pullbacks. We also assume that one of the following conditions is verified:

  • (i) \(W\) is also stable under pushouts;

  • (ii) \(W\) is stable under the suspension functor \(\Sigma\colon C\to C\).

The localization \(L(C)\) of \(C\) by \(W\) is then stable and the localization functor \(\gamma\colon C\to L(C)\) is exact.

Proof

We know that \(L(C)\) is pointed left exact and that the localization functor \[ \gamma\colon C\to L(C) \] is left exact by Theorem 17.1.5.

Under condition (i), we can apply dually Corollary 17.1.8 and see that \(L(C)\) has pushouts and that \(\gamma\) is right exact (Proposition 14.1.1 and its dual version ensure that \(L(C)\) has a terminal object that is also initial and that \(\gamma\) preserves those). The proof of Theorem 17.1.5 (iv) tells us that the collection of pullback squares of \(C\) covers the collection of pullback squares of \(L(C)\). Since all pullback squares of \(C\) also are pushout squares, and since \(\gamma\) is exact, this implies that all pullback squares of \(L(C)\) are pushouts. Applying the previous argument to \(C\catop\) shows that all pushout squares of \(L(C)\) are pullback squares as well.

Under condition (ii), Lemma 14.1.5 tells us that the adjunction equivalence \[ \Sigma\colon C\rightleftarrows C\colon \Omega \] induces an adjunction equivalence after localization, which implies right away that \(\Omega\colon L(C)\to L(C)\) is an equivalence of categories. Therefore, \(L(C)\catop\) is stable, and thus so is \(L(C)\) itself.

Lemma 19.6.4. In a stable category \(C\), for any morphism \(u\colon x\to y\) in \(C\), the cofiber of \(u\) is canonically isomorphic to the suspension of its fiber.

Proof

This follows right away from the fact that in the commutative diagram both squares are pullbacks as well as pushouts.

Lemma 19.6.5. Let \(C\) be a stable category and a pullback (hence also a pushout) square in \(C\). Then \(u\) is an isomorphism if and only if \(u'\) is an isomorphism.

Proof

In a pullback square as in the lemma, if \(u\) is invertible, so is \(u'\). The dual statement for pushouts implies the converse.

Lemma 19.6.6. Let \(u\colon x\to y\) be a morphism in a stable category \(C\). The following assertions are equivalent.

  • (i) The morphism \(u\) is an isomorphism.

  • (ii) The fiber of \(u\) is isomorphic to \(0\).

  • (iii) The cofiber of \(u\) is isomorphic to \(0\).

Proof

It follows immediately from Lemma 19.6.4 that conditions (ii) and (iii) are equivalent. We deduce right away from Lemma 19.6.5 that conditions (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

Proposition 19.6.7. Let \(i\colon A\to B\) be a fully faithful exact functor between stable categories. The family \(W\) of morphisms of \(B\) with fibers in \(A\) agrees with the family of morphisms of \(B\) with cofibers in \(A\). Moreover \(W\) is stable under pullbacks as well as under pushouts, and the resulting localization of \(B\) by \(W\) is nothing else than the Verdier quotient \(B/A\).

Proof

The first assertion follows right away from Lemma 19.6.4. The stability of \(W\) under pullbacks is obvious, since fibers remain constant through pullbacks. Stability under pushouts follows by a dual argument. For composable morphisms \(f\) and \(g\), the cofiber sequence \(\cofib(f)\to\cofib(gf)\to\cofib(g)\) shows that \(W\) has the \(2\)-out-of-\(3\) property, since \(A\) is a full stable subcategory. Thus Proposition 19.6.1 shows that \(L(B)\) is stable and the localization functor \(\gamma\colon B\to L(B)\) is exact. By Corollary 17.3.7, precomposition with \(\gamma\) therefore identifies exact functors \(L(B)\to C\) with exact functors \(B\to C\) inverting \(W\), for every stable category \(C\).

Given an exact functor \(\varphi\colon B\to C\), using Lemma 19.6.6, we see that the property for \(\varphi\) to send any map in \(W\) to an isomorphism in \(C\) is equivalent to the property of sending any object of \(A\) to \(0\) in \(C\). Consequently, there is a pullback square of the form which identifies \(L(B)\) with the pushout defining \(B/A\).

Corollary 19.6.8. Let \(i\colon A\to B\) be an exact full embedding between small stable categories with corresponding Verdier quotient \(p\colon B\to B/A\). The induced functor \[ p^*\colon\Lex((B/A)\catop,\Sp)\to\Lex(B\catop,\Sp) \] is fully faithful. Given an exact functor of the form \[ F\colon B\catop\to\Sp\, , \] the following assertions are equivalent.

  • (i) The functor \(F\) belongs to the essential image of \(p^*\).

  • (ii) The functor \(i^*(F)=F\circ i\catop\) is constant with value \(0\).

  • (iii) We have \(F(i(a)) = 0\) in \(\Sp\) for any object \(a\) of \(A\).

  • (iv) We have \(\Omega^\infty(F(i(a)))\simeq*\) in \(\An_\bullet\) for any object \(a\) of \(A\).

Proof

The fact that the functor \(p^*\) is fully faithful with essential image described by condition (ii) is a direct consequence of the description of the universal property exhibiting \(B/A\) as a localization provided by Proposition 19.6.7, using Remark 18.5.6 and the left exact version of Corollary 17.3.7. Conditions (ii) and (iii) obviously are equivalent. Since \[ \Omega^\infty\colon\Lex(C\catop,\Sp)\to\Lex(C\catop,\An_{\bullet}) \] is an equivalence for any small stable category \(C\), all of the above may be repeated replacing \(\Sp\) by \(\An_{\bullet}\) and this implies that conditions (iii) and (iv) are equivalent.

Remark 19.6.9. Let \(i\colon A\to B\) be an exact fully faithful functor between small stable categories, with Verdier quotient. Let \(W\) be the family of maps with fibers in \(A\).

Using the Yoneda embedding \[ B\hookrightarrow\Ind(B)=\Lex(B\catop,\Sp) \] we may see \(W\) as a small family of maps in \(\Ind(B)\). The \(W\)-local objects are thus those presheaves satisfying condition (iii) of Corollary 19.6.8. More precisely, if \(L_W\Ind(B)\) denotes the full subcategory of \(W\)-local objects in \(\Ind(B)\), the functor \(p^*\) induces a fully faithful and essentially surjective functor from \(\Ind(B/A)\) to \(L_W\Ind(B)\). Since \(p^*\) corresponds to the full embedding of \(L_W\Ind(B)\) to \(\Ind(B)\), its left adjoint \[ p_!\colon\Ind(B/A)\to\Ind(B) \] correspond to the \(W\)-localization functor \[ \loc_W\colon L_W\Ind(B)\to\Ind(B)\, . \] One can then use Proposition 18.4.2 to identify the localization of \(B\) by \(W\) with the smallest full subcategory of \(\Ind(B/A)\) stable under pushouts and containing objects of the form \(p(b)\) for \(b : B^\simeq\). Let \(\gamma\colon B\to L(B)\) be this localization.

We also get a Verdier quotient of the form since \(i^*\) is a localization (having a fully faithful left adjoint \(i_!\)) and that the objects of \(\Ind(B)\) that are sent to \(0\) through \(i^*\) precisely are the objects in the essential image of \(p^*\) by Corollary 19.6.8, so that we can apply Proposition 19.6.7 (for large stable categories, i.e. seeing \(\Ind(B)\) as a stable category in larger universe).

Definition 19.6.10. A six gluing functors datum is a diagram of stable categories of the form in which all functors are exact, with the following properties.

  • (i) We have \(i^*\circ j_! = 0\).

  • (ii) Both functors \(j_!\) and \(i_*\) are fully faithful.

  • (iii) The functor \(j^*\) is right adjoint to \(j_!\) and left adjoint to \(j_*\).

  • (iv) The functor \(i^*\) is left adjoint to \(i_*\).

  • (v) The pair \((j^*,i^*)\) is jointly conservative: for any object \(x : X^\simeq\), we have \(x= 0\) if and only if \(j^*(x)=0\) and \(i^*(x)=0\).

Theorem 19.6.15 (Thomason). Let \(i\colon A\to B\) be a fully faithful exact functor between small stable categories, and let \(B/A\) be the associated Verdier quotient. By passing to idempotent completions, we obtain a cartesian square in \(\StabCat\) of the form with \(i'\) fully faithful, and the induced functor \[ B'/A'\to (B/A)' \] is an exact fully faithful functor that induces an equivalence after idempotent completion \[ (B'/A')'\simeq(B/A)'\, . \] Moreover, the induced commutative square of (large) stable categories has the property that \(i_!\) is fully faithful and that \(p_!\) induces an equivalence \[ \Ind(B)/\Ind(A)\simeq\Ind(B/A)\, . \]

Proof

Since \(i\) is fully faithful, the induced functor \[ i_!\colon\PSh(A)\to\PSh(B) \] is fully faithful, hence its restriction \[ i_!\colon\Ind(A)\to\Ind(B) \] is fully faithful as well. Restricting further to compact objects thus yields an exact fully faithful functor \[ i'\colon A'\to B'\, . \] Since \(\Ind(C)=\Ind(C')\) for any small stable category \(C\), Remark 19.6.9 implies right away that the comparison functor \[ B/A\to B'/A' \] is fully faithful and induces an equivalence \[ \Ind(B/A)=\Ind(B'/A')\, . \] Passing to compact objects, this proves that \(B/A\) and \(B'/A'\) have the same idempotent completion.

The only non-trivial property to be checked is the fact that \(i^*\) and \(p_!\) form a jointly conservative family of functors. In other words, we have to check that, for an object \(X\) of \(\Ind(B)\) with \(i^*(X)=0\) and \(p_!(X)=0\) we must have \(X=0\). But by Corollary 19.6.8, the condition that \(i^*(X)=0\) is equivalent to the condition that \(X\) is in the essential image of \(p^*\). Definition 19.6.16. Let \(B\) be a stable category. A thick subcategory of \(B\) is a fully faithful exact functor \(i\colon A\to B\) with the property that, for any projector of the form \(p\colon x\to x\) in \(A\), if \(i(p)\) has an image in \(B\), then \(p\) has an image in \(A\). We will then say by a slight abuse that \(A\) is a thick subcategory of \(B\).

Proposition 19.6.17. Let \(i\colon A\to B\) be a fully faithful exact functor between stable categories. We denote by \(\gamma\colon B\to B/A\) the associated Verdier quotient. The following conditions are equivalent.

  • (i) The commutative square is a pullback square.

  • (ii) \(A\) is a thick subcategory of \(B\).

Proof

If (i) holds and \(p\colon x\to x\) is a projector in \(A\) that has an image \(y\) in \(B\), then \(\gamma(y)=0\) because \(\gamma(y)\) is the image of a projector on \(\gamma(i(p))=0\). Therefore, \(y\) is in the essential image of \(i\). Conversely, if we denote by \(C'\) the idempotent completion of any stable category \(C\), we know from the previous theorem that we have a pullback square of the form Since \(B/A\to B'/A'\) is fully faithful, this readily implies that the full subcategory of \(B\) whose objects are sent to \(0\) in \(B/A\) is precisely the intersection of \(A'\) with \(B\). The explicit description of idempotent completions obtained from Proposition 17.4.4 shows that condition (ii) follows from condition (i).
