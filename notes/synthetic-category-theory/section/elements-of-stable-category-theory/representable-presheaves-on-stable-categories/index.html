Representable presheaves on stable categories

Construction 19.4.1. Let \(C\) be a small stable category. Recall from Corollary 19.2.8 that \[ \Ind(C)=\Lex(C\catop,\Sp)\, . \] The Yoneda embedding \(C\hookrightarrow\Ind(C)\) thus yields a fully faithful exact functor \[ h^\Sp_C\colon C\to \Lex(C\catop,\Sp) \] hence an exact functor of the form \[ (h^\Sp_C)\catop\colon C\catop\to\Rex(C,\Sp\catop) \] corresponding to a functor \[ \SpHom_C\colon C\catop\times C\to\Sp\, \] that is (right) exact in each variable. For \(x\) in \(C\catop\) and \(y\) in \(C\) there is a functorial identification \[ \Omega^\infty(\SpHom_C(x,y))=\Hom_C(x,y)\, . \] Therefore, if \(C\) is locally small, \(\SpHom_C(x,y)\) belongs to the category \(\Sp\) of small spectra.

Lemma 19.4.2. If \(C\) is a stable locally small category, then \(\SpHom_C\) takes its values in \(\Sp\). Moreover, the induced functor \[ \SpHom_C\colon C\catop\times C\to\Sp \] preserves small limits in each variable.

Proof

We can choose a larger universe for which \(C\) is small, with corresponding category of large spectra \(\Sp'\) and thus a functor \[ \SpHom_C\colon C\catop\times C\to \Sp'\, . \] An object of \(\Sp'\) is by definition an infinite sequence of possibly large pointed animae \(x_n\) equipped with isomorphisms \(x_n\simeq\Omega(x_{n+1})\) for all \(n : \N\). In the case of \(\SpHom_C(a,b)\) with \(a\) and \(b\) objects of \(C\), by construction, this sequence may be obtained through \[ x_n=\Hom_C(a,\Sigma^n(b)) \] together with the isomorphisms \begin{align*} \Hom_C(a,\Sigma^n(b)) &=\Hom_C(a,\Omega(\Sigma^{n+1}(b)))\\ &=\Omega( \Hom_C(a,\Sigma^{n+1}(b)))\, . \end{align*} The last assertion of the lemma follows from the analogous property for the ordinary Hom functor \(\Hom_C\).

Theorem 19.4.3. Let \(f\colon C\to D\) be an exact functor between locally small stable categories. Then \(f\) has a right adjoint \(g\colon D\to C\) if and only if there is a functorial isomorphism of spectra \[ \SpHom_D(f(x),y)=\SpHom_C(x,g(y)) \] for any \(x : C\) and any \(y : D\).

Proof

This is clearly a sufficient condition since one can recover \(\Hom\) from \(\SpHom\) by applying \(\Omega^\infty\).

For the converse, we may choose to work in a universe for which both \(C\) and \(D\) are small. The property that \(g\) is right adjoint to \(f\) is that \(g_!=f^*\), where \[ f^*\colon\PSh(D)\to\PSh(C) \] is the functor defined by precomposition by \(f\catop\) whereas \[ g_!\colon\PSh(D)\to\PSh(C) \] extends \(g\) by small colimits along the Yoneda embedding. By virtue of Theorem 18.2.8, these identifications remain true when we restrict ourselves to ind-objects. We thus get identifications of the form \(g_!=f^*\) where this time we consider functors \[ f^*, g_!\colon\Fun(D\catop,\Sp)\to\Fun(C\catop,\Sp)\,. \] Since we have a canonical commutative square of the form we must have \[ g_!(\SpHom_D(-,y))=\SpHom_C(-,g(y)) \] for all \(y : D\). Since \[ f^*(\SpHom_D(-,y))=\SpHom_D(f(-),y) \] holds by definition, this proves the theorem.

Proposition 19.4.4. Let \(C\) be a locally small stable category with small colimits. Given an object \(x\) of \(C\), the following conditions are equivalent.

  • (i) The object \(x\) is compact in \(C\).

  • (ii) The functor \(\SpHom_C(x,-)\colon C\to\Sp\) commutes with filtered colimits.

  • (iii) The functor \(\SpHom_C(x,-)\colon C\to\Sp\) commutes with small colimits.

Proof

Corollary 18.3.11 implies that conditions (ii) and (iii) are equivalent. We have an adjunction \[ \Sigma^\infty((-)_+)\colon\An\rightleftarrows\Sp\colon\Omega^\infty \] with the left adjoint preserving compact objects, hence the right adjoint \(\Omega^\infty\) commutes with small filtered colimits. We know from Proposition 19.2.12 that the collection of functors \(\Omega^\infty\circ\Sigma^n\) indexed by \(n : \N\) is jointly conservative. We observe that \[ \Omega^\infty(\Sigma^n(\SpHom_C(x,y)))=\Hom_C(x,\Sigma^n(y)) \] for all \(y\). Therefore, condition (ii) simply means that each functor \[ y\mapsto\Hom_C(x,\Sigma^n(y)) \] commutes with filtered colimits. Since the functors \(\Omega^n\) commute with all colimits, this is clearly equivalent to the property of compactness for \(x\).

Corollary 19.4.5. Let \(C\) be a compactly generated stable category and let \(C^\simeq\) be the full subcategory of its compact objects. Then \(C^\simeq\) is stable and the embedding \(C^\simeq\hookrightarrow C\) is exact.

Proof

We know that \(C^\simeq\) is stable under finite colimits in \(C\). The preceding proposition implies that it is also stable under the functor \(\Omega\) since functors of the form \(\SpHom(x,-)\) commute with \(\Omega\) and that the latter commute with filtered colimits – being an equivalence. This implies this corollary right away.

Proposition 19.4.6 (Compact generation criterion). Let \(C\) be a locally small stable category with small colimits, and let \(\Gg\) be a small family of compact objects. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. (1) The smallest full stable subcategory of \(C\) that contains \(\Gg\) and is closed under small colimits is \(C\);

  2. (2) The functors \[ \Hom_C(\Sigma^nG,-)\colon C\to\An, \qquad G\in\Gg,\ n\in\Z, \] are jointly conservative;

  3. (3) The functors \[ \SpHom_C(\Sigma^nG,-)\colon C\to\An, \qquad G\in\Gg, \]

  4. (4) Every object of \(C\) is a filtered colimit of objects in the smallest full subcategory of \(C\) containing \(\Gg\), closed under shifts and under finite colimits.

If these conditions hold, then \(C\) is compactly generated, and its compact objects are precisely the objects that are retracts of objects in the subcategory described in (3).

Proof

For any objects \(X\) and \(Y\) of \(C\) and any integer \(n\), we have a functorial identification of the form: \[ \Omega^\infty(\Sigma^n(\SpHom_C(X,Y)))=\Hom_C(X,\Sigma^n(Y))\, . \] It follows thus from Proposition 19.2.12 that assertions (3) and (4) are equivalent. Write \(C^{\Gg\text{-}\mathrm{fin}}\) for the smallest full subcategory containing \(\Gg\) and closed under shifts, finite colimits. This subcategory is small. Its objects are compact, since filtered colimits of animae commute with finite limits, and compactness is preserved under shifts and retracts.

Condition (4) implies (1), because every full stable subcategory closed under small colimits and containing \(\Gg\) contains \(C^{\Gg\text{-}\mathrm{fin}}\) and all its filtered colimits. Suppose next that (1) holds. To prove (3), it is enough, by stability, to show that the displayed functors jointly detect zero objects. Let \(X\) satisfy \[ \SpHom_C(G,X)\simeq 0 \] for every \(G\in\Gg\). It follows from Proposition 19.4.4 that each functor \(\SpHom_C(G,-)\), with \(G\) in \(\Gg\), commutes with small colimits. Therefore, the full subcategory of objects \(Y\) for which \(\SpHom_C(G,X)\) vanishes for all \(G\) in \(\Gg\) is stable and closed under small colimits. It contains \(\Gg\), and hence equals \(C\) by (1). Taking \(Y=X\) shows that \(X = 0\).

It remains to prove that (2) implies (4). But condition (2) implies by Remark 18.2.7 and Theorem 18.3.8 that \(\Ind(C^{\Gg\text{-}\mathrm{fin}})=C\). This proves (4), obviously. This also implies the last assertion since the full subcategory of compact objects of \(C\) precisely is then the idempotent completion of \(C^{\Gg\text{-}\mathrm{fin}}\) by Proposition 17.4.4.
