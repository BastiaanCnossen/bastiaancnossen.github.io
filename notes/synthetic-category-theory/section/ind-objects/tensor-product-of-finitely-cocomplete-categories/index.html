Tensor product of finitely cocomplete categories

Construction 18.6.1. Let \(C\) and \(D\) be two small categories with finite colimits. We have a Yoneda embedding: \[ h\colon C\times D\to\rex{C\times D}\, . \] We let \(W\) be the smallest family of morphisms in \(\rex{C\times D}\) stable under pushouts and satisfying the \(2\)-out-of-\(3\) property containing the following maps, where \(0\) denotes an initial object of \(\rex{C\times D}\):

  • (i) \(0\to h(x,y)\) if \(x\) is an initial object of \(C\);

  • (ii) \(0\to h(x,y)\) if \(y\) is an initial object of \(D\);

  • (iii) the canonical comparison map \(h(x_1,y)\amalg_{h(x_0,y)}h(x_2,y)\to h(x,y)\) for any pushout square in \(C\) and any object \(y\) in \(D\);

  • (iv) the canonical comparison map \(h(x,y_1)\amalg_{h(x,y_0)}h(x,y_2)\to h(x,y)\) for any pushout square in \(D\) and any object \(x\) in \(C\).

We define finally \[ C\otimes D:=L(\rex{C\times D},W) \] to be the localization of \(\rex{C\times D}\) by \(W\), with localization functor \[ \gamma\colon\rex{C\times D}\to C\otimes D\, . \] By construction, there is a canonical functor \[ \otimes\colon C\times D\to C\otimes D\ , \quad (x,y)\mapsto x\otimes y:=\gamma(h_{C\times D}(x,y))\, . \]

Definition 18.6.2. Let \(C\), \(D\) and \(E\) be categories with finite colimits. A functor \[ \varphi\colon C\times D\to E \] is right exact in each variable if, for each object \(x : C^\simeq\), the functor \[ \varphi(x,-)\colon D\to E \] is right exact, and, for each object \(y : D^\simeq\), the functor \[ \varphi(-,y)\colon C\to E \] is right exact.

We denote by \(\BRex(C,D;E)\) the full subcategory of \(\Fun(C\times D,E)\) spanned by functors that are right exact in each variable.

Example 18.6.3. By construction, for any two categories with finite colimits \(C\) and \(D\), the canonical functor \[ \otimes\colon C\times D\to C\otimes D\ , \quad (x,y)\mapsto x\otimes y \] is right exact in each variable.

Remark 18.6.4. For any \(B\), \(C\) and \(D\) with finite colimits the identification \[ \Fun(B,\Fun(C,D))=\Fun(B\times C,D) \] restricts to: \[ \Rex(B,\Rex(C,D))=\BRex(B,C;D)\, . \]

Theorem 18.6.5. Given \(C\) and \(D\) two categories with finite colimits, the assignment \[ \varphi\mapsto ((x,y)\mapsto\varphi(x\otimes y)) \] defines, for any category \(E\) with finite colimits, an equivalence \[ \RexCat(C\otimes D,E)\simeq\BRex(C,D;E) \]

Proof

The localization functor \(\gamma\colon\rex{C\times D}\to C\otimes D\) is right exact by Corollary 17.1.9. By Corollary 17.3.7, there is therefore an equivalence: \[ \Rex(C\otimes D,E)\simeq \RexCat^W(\rex{C\times D},E)\, . \] On the other hand, we have \[ \RexCat(\rex{C\times D},E)=\Fun(C\times D,E) \] by Theorem 18.5.2. This produces a canonical full embedding \[ \Rex(C\otimes D,E)\hookrightarrow\Fun(C\times D,E) \] whose image at the level of objects obviously is \(\BRex(C,D;E)\), by definition of \(W\).

Corollary 18.6.6. Let \(u\colon A\to B\) be a right exact functor between small categories with finite colimits. Then, for any compactly generated category \(C\), the functor \[ u^*\colon\Lex(B\catop,C)\to\Lex(A\catop,C) \] defined by composition with \(u\catop\) has a left adjoint \[ u_!\colon\Lex(A\catop,C)\to\Lex(B\catop,C)\, . \]

Proof

We may assume that \(C=\Lex(E\catop,\An)\) for some small category with finite colimits \(E\) by Theorem 18.3.8. We observe that, for any category \(D\) with finite limits, we have \[ \Lex(A\catop,D)=\Rex(A,D\catop)\catop \] which allows us to reinterpret Theorem 18.6.5 through \[ \Lex(A\catop,C)=\Lex((A\otimes E)\catop,\An)\, . \] Doing the same for \(B\), replacing \(u\) by the induced functor \[ u\otimes\id_E\colon A\otimes E\to B\otimes E \] we see that it suffices to consider the case where \(C=\An\). This is then a particular case of Theorem 18.2.8.

Example 18.6.7. We define \(\FinAn=\rex{[0]}\). We thus have \[ \RexCat(\FinAn,C)=C \] for any category \(C\) with finite colimits. For any finite poset \(P\) we have \begin{align*} \Rex(\Fun(P\catop,\FinAn),C) &=\Rex(\FinAn,\Fun(P,C))\\ &=\Fun(P,C)\\ &=\Fun(\rex{P},C)\, . \end{align*} In other words, we have: \[ \rex{P}=\Fun(P\catop,\FinAn) \] In general, for any categories with finite colimits \(C\) and \(D\), we have: \begin{align*} \Rex(\rex{P}\otimes C,D) &=\Rex(\rex{P},\Rex(C,D))\\ &=\Fun(P,\Rex(C,D))\\ &=\Rex(C,\Fun(P,D))\\ &=\Rex(\Fun(P\catop,C),D)\, . \end{align*} In other words, we have \[ \rex{P}\otimes C=\Fun(P\catop,C) \] for all \(C\) with finite colimits.

Remark 18.6.8. If \(A\) and \(B\) are pointed right exact categories, the functor \[ A\times B\to B\times A\ , \quad (a,b)\mapsto (b,a) \] induces a unique right exact functor \[ \theta_{A,B}\colon A\otimes B\to B\otimes A \] such that \(\theta_{A,B}(a\otimes b)=b\otimes a\) for all terms \(a : A\) and \(b : B\). This comes from the fact that the canonical equivalence \[ \Fun(A\times B,C)=\Fun(B\times A,C) \] restricts to an equivalence of the form \[ \BRex(A,B;C)=\BRex(B,A;C)\, . \] The unicity of the functor \(\theta_{A,B}\) above implies that it must be an equivalence with inverse \(\theta_{B,A}\).

Similarly, for \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) pointed right exact categories, the identification \[ (A\times B)\times C=A\times(B\times C)\ , \quad ((x,y),z)\mapsto(x,(y,z)) \] yields a unique identification of the form \[ (A\otimes B)\otimes C=A\otimes(B\otimes C)\ , \quad (x\otimes y)\otimes z\mapsto x\otimes (y\otimes z)\, . \]

Definition 18.6.9. Let us choose a universe of large categories \(\Cat'\) (so that \(\Cat\) is both an object and a full subcategory of \(\Cat'\)). We define \(\PrL_\omega\) as the subcategory of \(\Cat'\) whose objects are the compactly generated categories and whose morphisms are the functors that commute with small colimits and that preserve compact objects.

Given \(A\) and \(B\) two compactly generated categories, we denote by \(\FunL_\omega(A,B)\) the full subcategory of \(\Fun(A,B)\) spanned by the functors that preserve small colimits as well as compact objects. In particular, we have: \[ \Hom_{\PrL_\omega}(A,B)=\FunL_\omega(A,B)^\simeq\, . \]

Theorem 18.6.10. Given a small category with finite colimits \(C\) and a compactly generated category \(D\), there is a canonical equivalence of categories of the form \[ \Rex(C,D^\omega)\simeq\FunL_\omega(\Ind(C),D)\, . \] In particular, assigning the full subcategory of compact objects defines a fully faithful functor \[ \PrL_\omega\to\RexCat\ , \quad D\mapsto D_\omega \] that is right adjoint to the functor \[ \Ind\colon\RexCat\to\PrL_\omega\ , \quad C\mapsto\Ind(C)\, . \]

Proof

This is a particular case of Theorem 17.4.9.

Proposition 18.6.11. The category \(\PrL_\omega\) is compactly generated: \[ \PrL_\omega : (\PrL_\omega)^\simeq\, . \]

Proof

This is a particular case of Theorem 17.4.12.

Lemma 18.6.12. For any finite poset \(P\), the category \(\Fun(P\catop,\FinAn)\) is a compact object of \(\RexCat\).

Proof

For any small category with finite colimits \(C\), we have \begin{align*} \Hom_{\RexCat}(\Fun(P\catop,\FinAn),C) &=\Rex(\Fun(P\catop,\FinAn),C)^\simeq\\ &=\Rex(\FinAn,\Fun(P,C))^\simeq\\ &=\Fun(P,C)^\simeq\\ &=\Hom_{\Cat}(P,C)\, . \end{align*} Since the forgetful functor \(\RexCat\hookrightarrow\Cat\) commutes with small filtered colimits, the lemma follows from the fact that finite posets are compact objects of \(\Cat\) (since they are finite categories).

Proposition 18.6.13. If \(A\) and \(B\) are compact in \(\RexCat\), then so is \(A\otimes B\).

Proof

If there are finite posets \(P\) and \(Q\) such that \(A=\rex{P}\) and \(B=\rex{Q}\) respectively, then we have \[ A\otimes B=\rex{P\times Q} \] The product of finite enumerations of \(P\) and \(Q\) gives a finite enumeration of \(P\times Q\), and its product order is decidable. Thus \(P\times Q\) is a finite poset, so this particular case follows from the previous lemma.

On the other hand, we know that \(\rex{[1]}\) is a compact generator of \(\RexCat\). Therefore, applying Proposition 17.4.4 to the finite-colimit closure of \(\rex{[1]}\) in \(\RexCat\), with \(\kappa\) the subuniverse of finite posets, we see that for a full subcategory of \(\RexCat\) to contain all compact objects of \(\RexCat\), it suffices that it is stable under finite colimits, under the formation of images of projectors, and contains \(\rex{[1]}\).

Given \(A\) in \(\RexCat\), since \(A\otimes(-)\) commutes with small colimits, the full subcategory \(X_A\) of \(\RexCat\) spanned by those small categories with finite colimits \(B\) such that \(A\otimes B\) is compact is stable under finite colimits as well as under the formation of images of projectors (these being a particular kind of colimits, as explained in the proof of Lemma 17.4.2). If \(A=\rex{P}\) for some finite poset \(P\), we deduce that any compact object of \(\RexCat\) belongs to \(X_A\). Since \(A\otimes B= B\otimes A\), this means that, for \(A\) an arbitrary compact object of \(\RexCat\), \(X_A\) contains all categories of the form \(\rex{P}\) for any finite poset. Therefore, for any compact object \(A\) of \(\RexCat\), \(X_A\) contains all compact objects of \(\RexCat\), and this proves the proposition.

Corollary 18.6.14. A small category with finite colimits \(A\) is compact in \(\RexCat\) if and only if the functor \[ \Rex(A,-)\colon\RexCat\to\Cat \] commutes with small filtered colimits.

Proof

Since \(\RexCat\) is compactly generated, this follows straight away from the formula \[ \Hom_{\RexCat}(A\otimes B,C)=\Hom_{\RexCat}(A,\Rex(B,C)) \] and from Proposition 18.6.13.
