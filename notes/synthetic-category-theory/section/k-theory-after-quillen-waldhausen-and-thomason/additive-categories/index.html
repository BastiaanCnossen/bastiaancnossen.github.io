Lemma 20.2.1 (the Eckmann-Hilton argument, le lemme de Godement). Let \(A\) be an anima together with a term \(e : A\) as well as two maps \[ \circ \colon A\times A\to A\ , \quad (a,b)\mapsto a\circ b\quadtext{and} \bullet\colon A\times A\to A\ , \quad (a,b)\mapsto a\bullet b\, . \] We assume that the following rules hold.

  • (i) For every term \(a : A\), there are terms of the following types: \begin{align*} u^l_a& : a= e\circ a\\ u^r_a& : a\circ e= a\\ v^l_a& : a= e\bullet a\\ v^r_a& : a\bullet e= a \, . \end{align*}

  • (ii) For every term \(a,b,c,d : X\), there is a term of type \[ \gamma_{a,b,c,d} : (a\bullet b)\circ (c\bullet d)= (a\circ c)\bullet (b\circ d)\, . \]

Then, for any terms \(a,b : A\), there are terms \[ \kappa_{a,b} : a\bullet b = a\circ b \quadtext{and} \kappa'_{a,b} : a\circ b = b\bullet a\, . \]

Proof

The term \(\kappa_{a,b}\) is obtained as follows. \begin{align*} a\bullet b &=(a\circ e)\bullet(e\circ b)\\ &=(a\bullet e)\circ(e\bullet b)\\ &=a\circ b\, , \end{align*} Similarly, the sequence \begin{align*} a\circ b &=(e\bullet a)\circ (b\bullet e)\\ &=(e\circ b)\bullet (a\circ e)\\ &=b\bullet a \end{align*} yields \(\kappa'_{a,b}\).

Example 20.2.2. Given an anima \(X\) and a term \(x : X\), we can form \[ \Omega(X,x):=\Hom_X(x,x)\, . \] there is thus a composition law \[ c_{X,x}\colon\Omega(X,x)\times\Omega(X,x)\to\Omega(X,x)\, . \] This operation defines a functor \[ \Omega\colon\An_\bullet\to\An_{\bullet} \] that commutes with products. We define \[ \bullet\colon\Omega^2(X,x)\times\Omega^2(X,x) =\Omega(\Omega(X,\id_x)\times\Omega(X,\id_x))\xrightarrow{\Omega(c_{X,x})} \Omega^2(X,x) \] where \(\Omega^2(X,x):=\Omega(\Omega(X,x),\id_x)\). There is also the composition law \[ \circ:=c_{\Omega(X,x),\id_x}\colon\Omega^2(X,x)\times\Omega^2(X,x)\to\Omega^2(X,x)\, . \] One checks immediately that Lemma 20.2.1 applies to the anima \(A=\Omega^2(X,x)\) with these definitions of \(\circ\) and \(\bullet\).

Definition 20.2.3. A category \(C\) is semiadditive if its has an initial object \(0\) as well as binary coproducts with the following properties:

  • (i) The initial object \(0\) turns out to be a terminal object as well. In particular, for any object \(x\) of \(C\), there is a map \(p_x\colon x\to 0\).

  • (ii) For any objects \(x\) and \(y\), the maps \[ p_x\amalg\id_y\colon x\amalg y\to x\amalg 0=x \quadtext{and} \id_x\amalg p_y\colon x\amalg y\to 0\amalg y=y \] exhibit \(x\amalg y\) as a product of \(x\) and \(y\) in \(C\).

Construction 20.2.4. Let \(C\) be a semiadditive category. For any objects \(x,y : C^\simeq\), there is an operation \[ \Hom_C(x,y)\times\Hom_C(x,y)\to\Hom_C(x,y)\ , \quad\mapsto (f,g)\mapsto f+g \] defined by the following composition \[ \Hom_C(x,y)\times\Hom_C(x,y)=\Hom_C(x\amalg x,y)\xrightarrow{\Delta^*}\Hom_C(x,y) \] with \(\Delta\colon x\to x\amalg x\) the map defined by the pair \((\id_X,\id_x)\) using the description of \(x\amalg x\) as a product.

This construction is functorial in the sense that for any functor between semiadditive categories \[ f\colon C\to D \] that preserves finite coproducts (hence finite products as well), we have a canonical commutative square of the form We observe furthermore that \(\Fun(I,C)\) is semiadditive for any category \(I\) whenever \(C\) is pre-additive. This expresses how functorial is this construction in the variables \(x,y : C\).

Example 20.2.5. The category \(\RexCat\) is semiadditive. The initial object is the category \([0]\) and the coproduct is given by the cartesian product of the underlying categories.

Construction 20.2.6 (\(2\times 2\) matrices). In a semiadditive category \(C\), given for objects \(x,y,z,w\) a map \[ \varphi \colon x\amalg y\to z\amalg w \] amounts to a pair of maps of type \(u\colon x\amalg y\to z\) and \(v\colon x\amalg y\to w\) (seeing \(z\amalg w\) as a product) and seeing \(x\amalg y\) as a coproduct, \(u\) correspond to a pair of maps of the form \[ a\colon x\to z\quadtext{and} b\colon y\to z \] whereas \(v\) corresponds to a pair of maps of the form \[ c\colon x\to w\quadtext{and} d\colon y\to w\, . \] In other words, defining a map \(\varphi\) as above is equivalent to defining a \(2\times 2\)-matrix: \[ \varphi= \begin{pmatrix} a & b\\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \] The map \(\varphi\) is defined in formula by \(\varphi(x,y)=(a(x)+b(y), c(x)+d(y))\).

Definition 20.2.7. An additive category is a semiadditive category \(C\) with the property that, for any object \(x\) of \(C\), the matrix \[ \begin{pmatrix} \id_x & \id_x\\ \id_x & 0 \end{pmatrix} \] (with \(0\colon x\to x\) the unique map factorizing through \(0\)) defines an automorphism of \(x\amalg x\).

Proposition 20.2.8. Any stable category is additive.

Proof

This means that, for any objects \(x\) and \(y\) in a stable category \(C\), for any maps \(f,g\colon x\to y\) in \(C\), one can reconstruct canonically the pair \((f,g)\) from the pair \((f+g,f)\). For this, it suffices to produce a map \(-g\colon x\to y\) such that \((f+g)+(-g)=f\) holds true. Since we have \[ \Hom_C(x,y)=\Omega^2(\Hom_C(x,\Sigma^2(y)),0)\, , \] Lemma 20.2.1 together with Example 20.2.2 tell us that one can see the operation \((f,g)\mapsto f+g\) as the composition law in the anima \(\Omega(\Hom_C(x,\Sigma(y)),0)\). Therefore, inverses for this composition law exist and this proves the proposition.

Corollary 20.2.9. Any full subcategory closed under finite (co)products of a stable category is additive.
