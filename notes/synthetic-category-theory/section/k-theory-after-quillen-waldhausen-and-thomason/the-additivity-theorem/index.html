Synthetic Category Theory · Chapter 20 · Section 20.6

The additivity theorem

← Previous chapterChapter 19 → Next chapterChapter 21 ← Previous sectionSection 20.5 → Next sectionSection 20.7

Recall that \(\underset{\Delta\catop}{\colim}\colon\PSh(\Delta)\to\An\) is called the geometric realization functor.

Lemma 20.6.1. Let be a commutative triangle of simplicial animae. We assume that, in any anima context, for any map \(\sigma\colon\Delta^n\to S\), the induced map of type \[ \Delta^n\times_SX\to\Delta^n\times_SY \] induces an isomorphism in \(\An\) after geometric realization. Then the geometric realization of \(u\) is an isomorphism.

Proof

Our assumptions mean that, for any small anima \(\Gamma\) and any term \(n : \N\), for any map of the form \(\sigma\colon\Gamma\times\Delta^n\to S\), the induced map of type \[ \sigma^*(u)\colon(\Gamma\times\Delta^n)\times_SX\to(\Gamma\times\Delta^n)\times_SY \] induces an isomorphism in \(\An\) after geometric realization. But any simplicial anima is canonically a colimit of simplicial animae of the form \(\Gamma\times\Delta^n\). Furthermore, for any morphism \(f\colon A\to S\) in \(\PSh(\Delta)\), the pullback \[ f^*\colon\PSh(\Delta)_{/S}\to\PSh(\Delta)_{/A} \] has a right adjoint: we can identify \[ \PSh(\Delta)_{/S}=\PSh(\Delta_{/S}) \] so that \(f^*\) is the pullback functor along the functor \[ f_!\colon\Delta_{/A}\to\Delta_{/S}\, . \] In particular, the functor \(f^*\) commutes with colimits. Therefore, the map \(u\) is canonically a colimit of morphisms of the form \(\sigma^*(u)\) induced by pullbacks from maps \(\sigma\) as above. Since the realization functor commutes with colimits (this is a colimit functor itself by definition), the geometric realization of \(u\) is the colimit of the geometric realization of each of the \(\sigma^*(u)\)’s. We conclude by the obvious observation that colimits preserve isomorphisms.

Remark 20.6.2. Let \(n : \N\) and \(A\) a small category. Let \(h\colon\Delta^1\times\Delta^n\to\Delta^n\) be the map induced by the functor expressing the fact that \(n\) is a terminal object of \([n]\).

The map \(h\) induces a functor \[ \eta\colon\Delta_{/[1]}\times_\Delta \Delta_{/[n]}\to\Delta_{/\Fun([1],[n])} \] sending \(u\colon [n]\to [m]\) and \(v\colon[m]\to [1]\) to the unique natural transformation \[ \kappa_{u,v}\colon u\to \bar u \] where \(\bar u\colon [m]\to [n]\) is defined as the composition of \((v,u)\) with \(h\). Since \(u(i)\leq\bar u (i)\), this defines \(\kappa_{u,v}\).

The evaluation functor \[ \ev\colon[1]\times\Fun([1],[m])\to [m] \] is functorial in \([m]\) hence so is its product with the identity of \([1]\) \[ \id_{[1]}\times\ev\colon [1]\times [1]\times\Fun([1],[m])\to [1]\times [m]\, . \] This defines a functor \[ c_{[m]}\colon [1]\times \Fun([1],[m])\to \Fun([1],[1]\times [m]) \] functorially in \([m]\). With the identification \[ \Fun([1]\times \Fun([1],[m]),A)= \Fun( \Fun([1],[m]),\Fun([1],A)) \] this defines a functor \[ c^*_{[m]}\colon\Fun(\Fun([1],[1]\times [m]),A)\to\Fun( \Fun([1],[m]),\Fun([1],A)) \] functorially in \([m]\) and \(A\). Assigning each \(u\colon [m]\to \Fun([1],[n])\) to the corresponding map \([1]\times [m]\to [n]\) defines a functor \[ u^*\colon \Fun(\Fun([1],[n]),A)\to\Fun(\Fun([1],[1]\times [m]),A) \] functorially in \(u\), seen as a term of \(\Delta_{/\Fun([1],[n])}\). Composing with the functor \(\eta\) above, this means that, for any functor \[ x\colon\Fun([1],[n])\to A \] if, for any map \(w\colon[m]\to [n]\) one writes \[ w^*(x)\colon\Fun([1],[n])\to A \] for the composition of \(x\) with the functor induced by \(w\) by applying \(\Fun([1],-)\), then for any maps \(u\colon[m]\to[n]\) and \(v\colon[m]\to[1]\), one has a canonical map \[ \kappa_{u,v,x}\colon u^*(x)\to \bar{u}^*(x) \] functorially in \((u,v)\).

Lemma 20.6.3. Let \(A\) be a Waldhausen category in which weak equivalences all are isomorphisms. The functor \[ (\ker,\coker)\colon\ExactSeq(A)\to A\times A \] induces a morphism of simplicial animae \[ \Scons(\ExactSeq(A))^\simeq\to \Scons(A)^\simeq\times\Scons(A)^\simeq \] that yields an isomorphism after geometric realization.

Proof

We have a commutative triangle of the form and we will apply Lemma 20.6.1 to it. We consider a map \[ x\colon \Delta^n\to \Scons(A) \] i.e. a functor \(x\colon\Fun([1],[n])\to A\) satisfying the conditions of Definition 20.5.4. We have to prove that the induced map \[ \Delta^n\times_{\Scons(A)^\simeq} \Scons(\ExactSeq(A))^\simeq \to \Delta^n\times_{\Scons(A)^\simeq}(\Scons(A)^\simeq\times \Scons(A)^\simeq) \] induces an isomorphism after geometric realization. We will consider first the case where \(n=0\). In that case \(x\) simply is the initial object \(*\) in \(A\). It suffices to check that the comparison map between pullbacks above is an equivalence of simplicial animae, which can be checked objectwise. If we evaluate at \([p]\), this is the same as evaluating at \(0\), up to replacing \(A\) by \(\Wald([p+1],A)\). Therefore, it suffices to check that we have an equivalence when we evaluate at \([0]\). The left hand side is then the collection of pushout squares of the form whereas the right hand side is the collection of pairs of objects of the form \((*,x'')\). It is clear that assigning \(x''\) to each pushout square as above is an equivalence. Let us consider the general case. Let us write \(x_0\colon\Delta^0\to \Scons(A)\) for the restriction of \(x\) along \(\{0\}\hookrightarrow\Delta^n\). This induces a pullback square of the form in which the upper horizontal map is an equivalence (by the preceding discussion). Therefore, it suffices to check that the two vertical maps induce an isomorphism after geometric realization. But the vertical map \(j\) on the right is equivalent to \[ \Scons(A)^\simeq\iso\{0\}\times\Scons(A)^\simeq\hookrightarrow \Delta^n\times\Scons(A)^\simeq\, . \] Since the geometric realization functor commutes with finite products (because \(\Delta\catop\) is sifted), it suffices to check that any map of the form \(\Delta^0\to \Delta^n\) induces an isomorphism after geometric realization. This follows from the fact that the geometric realization of \(\Delta^n\) is contractible (as is the colimit of any representable presheaf on a small category).

What precedes tells us that \[ \Delta^0\times_{\Scons(A)^\simeq} \Scons(\ExactSeq(A))^\simeq\simeq\Scons(A)^\simeq\, . \] There is a canonical map \[ p\colon \Delta^n\times_{\Scons(A)^\simeq} \Scons(\ExactSeq(A))^\simeq\to \Scons(A)^\simeq) \] obtained through the second projection \[ \Delta^n\times_{\Scons(A)^\simeq} \Scons(\ExactSeq(A))^\simeq \to \Scons(\ExactSeq(A))^\simeq \] followed from the map \[ \Scons(\ExactSeq(A))^\simeq\to \Scons(A)^\simeq \] induced by the functor \[ \coker\colon\ExactSeq(A)\to A\, . \] It is immediate to see that the vertical map \(i\) on the left hand side of the square above is a section of \(p\). It suffices now to prove that the other composite \(i\circ p\) simplicially homotopic to the identity.

Let \(h\colon\Delta^1\times\Delta^n\to\Delta^n\) be the map induced by the functor expressing the fact that \(n\) is a terminal object of \([n]\). Keeping track of the notations of Remark 20.6.2, for any maps \(u\colon [m]\to [n]\) and \(v\colon[m]\to [1]\) to the unique natural transformation \[ \kappa_{u,v}\colon u\to \bar u \] produces a canonical map \[ \kappa_{u,v,x}\colon u^*(x)\to \bar{u}^*(x) \] functorially in \((u,v)\). A term of the category \(\Delta_{/X}\) for \[ X=\Delta^1\times \Big(\Delta^n\times_{\Scons(A)^\simeq}\Scons(\ExactSeq(A))^\simeq\Big) \] literally is a tuple of the form \((u,v,\xi)\) with \((u,v)\) as above any \(\xi\) a tern of the category of pushout square in \(A\) of the form below. Such a term \(\xi\) is thus canonically the back face of a term of the category of commutative cubes in \(A\) of the form in which the back face, the front face, as well as the top face are pushout squares. We define and the rule \[ H(u,v,\xi)=(\bar{u},\bar{\xi}) \] produces a well-defined functor over \(\Delta\), hence a morphism of simplicial animae \[ H\colon \Delta^1\times \Big(\Delta^n\times_{\Scons(A)^\simeq}\Scons(\ExactSeq(A))^\simeq\Big) \to \Delta^n\times_{\Scons(A)^\simeq}\Scons(\ExactSeq(A))^\simeq \] which is a homotopy between the identity and \(i\circ p\).

Lemma 20.6.4. The functor \(\underset{[m] : \Delta\catop}{\colim}\, \underset{[n] : \Delta\catop}{\colim}\, w_m\Scons_n(-)^\simeq\colon\WaldCat\to\An_{\bullet}\) is additive.

Proof

We observe that \[ w_m(\ExactSeq(A))=\ExactSeq(w_m(A)) \] functorially in \([m]\) and \(A\). Therefore, it follows from Lemma 20.6.3, it suffices to prove that the functor \[ \Phi\colon A\mapsto \underset{[m] : \Delta\catop}{\colim}\, w_m(A)^\simeq \] sends the functor \[ i\colon A\to w_1(A)\ , \quad x\mapsto \id_x \] to an equivalence. Let us consider the functor \[ \ev_0\colon w_1(A)\to A\, . \] It suffices to check that \(i\circ\ev_0\) becomes simplicially homotopic to the identity after applying the functor \[ w(-)^\simeq \colon\WaldCat\to\PSh(\Delta)\, . \] The map \(\min\colon [1]\times [1]\to [1]\) induces a functor \[ h\colon[1]\times\Fun([1],A)\to\Fun([1],A) \] corresponding by transposition to the functor \[ \ev^*_0\colon\Fun([1],A)\to\Fun([1]\times [1],A)\, . \] Taking the Rezk nerve yields a map \[ N(h)\colon\Delta^1\times N(\Fun([1],A))\to N(\Fun([1],A))\, . \] We observe that there is a canonical embedding \[ w(A)^\simeq\hookrightarrow N(\Fun([1],A)) \] so that the map \(N(h)\) restricts to a map \[ H\colon \Delta^1\times w(A)^\simeq\to w(A)^\simeq \] that is the homotopy we were looking for.

Theorem 20.6.5 (Waldhausen). The \(K\)-theory functor \(K\colon\WaldCat\to\An_{\bullet}\) is additive.

Proof

This follows from Lemma 20.6.4 since the loop functor commutes with finite products.
